Retired New Ulm physician Daniel Groebner released a self-published hardcover volume titled "Good to Be a Musician: Community Bands in New Ulm," chronicling the musical traditions that shaped civic life across Brown County, The Journal reported. On Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026, Groebner held an official launch and signed copies at the Brown County Historical Society Museum from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m.

The Journal reported that the volume is bound in an expansive landscape format. Groebner, who practiced internal medicine for 34 years at New Ulm Medical Center before shifting into regional history, told the newspaper he wanted to make his single lifetime volume as nice as possible.

Groebner plays the baritone horn in the New Ulm Municipal Band and spent years researching how early agricultural settlements relied on town bands before radio or recorded music existed. According to The Journal, he wrote every word himself and held final editorial control, though he brought in eight reviewers to proofread the pages. Groebner noted that when authors proofread their own writing, they know what they meant to say and routinely overlook their own mistakes.

Sarah Reed Jirak wrote the book's foreword in April 2026, and Brown County Historical Society Research Librarian Darla Cordes Gebhard authored the back cover summary. Gebhard and Pat Eckstein joined Groebner at the Saturday signing, where between eight and ten visitors stopped by the museum annex for coffee and chocolate chip cookies, which The Journal noted are Groebner's favorite childhood treat.

The volume details town bands across Sleepy Eye, Springfield, Hanska, and Comfrey, showing how brass ensembles served as community glue at Fourth of July parades, funerals, school graduations, political rallies, and barn raisings. Historic downtown gathering spots featured in the text include Turner Hall and the 1890s Union Hall at North Broadway and First North Streets. Ensembles documented in the book include the 1878 Silver Cornet Band, the Great Western Band that became the Minnesota National Guard 2nd Regiment Band in 1898, the Goosetown Band founded in 1892 by Bohemian immigrant John Lindmeyer, and the New Ulm Municipal Band founded in 1946.

Regional civic bands remained exclusively male until the New Ulm Municipal Band integrated women after World War II, a development Groebner described as simply right for the community. The book also covers New Ulm's mid-20th-century reputation as the Polka Capital of the Nation and profiles Whoopee John Wilfahrt, the son of Bohemian immigrants who became the town's first old-time musician to broadcast on radio and make phonograph records.

Copies are on sale at the Brown County Historical Society Museum, Lykke Books, and the Minnesota Music Hall of Fame. Board member Neal Bond reported that Groebner delivered copies to the hall, where leaders are scheduling an upcoming presentation with him. The historical society will host a second signing on Friday, Nov. 27, 2026, from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. Groebner confirmed to The Journal that he will not write another book because he has other things he wants to do, including mowing his lawn.
