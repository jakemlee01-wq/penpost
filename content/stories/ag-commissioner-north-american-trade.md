Canada and Mexico buy more Minnesota farm products than anyone else. Right now, one of those relationships is in a tariff fight.

Agriculture Commissioner Thom Petersen told MPR News on Oct. 5 that he had just attended the 35th annual Tri National Agricultural Accord, which brought together officials from all 11 Canadian provinces, 30 U.S. states and 10 Mexican states, plus the USDA. He said turnout was a record.

The backdrop: the U.S. and Canada have traded tariff actions, and Petersen said he isn't a fan of tariffs. He also said the USMCA, the trade agreement among the three countries, was renewed this summer for one year instead of the 16 he wanted. A one-year window makes long-term plans harder for anyone who ships across a border.

The ties run both ways. Petersen said Minnesota brings in roughly 500,000 to 1 million piglets from Canada each year. Some are raised in southern Minnesota and later exported to Mexico as ham.

He flagged costs closer to home too. One farmer he spoke with is paying $2.50 more per gallon for diesel this fall than last year.

What to watch is whether the tariff fight with Canada eases before the trade agreement comes up again. A meeting like this one doesn't settle that, but it keeps the people who manage these relationships talking.
