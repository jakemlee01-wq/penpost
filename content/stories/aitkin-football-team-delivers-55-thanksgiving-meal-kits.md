Fifty-five roasting pans packed with holiday food arrived at the Community Food Shelf at First Lutheran Church in Aitkin.

Players and families from the Aitkin High School Gobblers football team gathered 550 shelf-stable items and assembled them into 55 complete meal kits, according to reporting from Message Media and the Aitkin Independent Age. The team organized the community outreach effort under the name "Thanksgiving in a pan."

The team hand-delivered the completed pans directly to the food shelf building at 107 2nd St. SE. According to church records, the Community Food Shelf at First Lutheran Church has served Aitkin County residents for over 20 years. It operates primarily on the second and fourth Thursdays of each month, assisting an average of 140 households monthly.

Booster club leader Kim Jones and the Aitkin Football Association organized and spearheaded the collection and packaging effort. Local community members Amanda and Peter Lowe contributed funds to cover extra packaging and project expenses, Message Media reported.

Pat Perrine, coordinator for the local food shelf, expressed gratitude for the donation. Perrine stated that the players, coaches, and parents were respectful and helpful, noting that many local families will enjoy a holiday dinner thanks to the team's work.

Gobblers Head Coach Alan Hills stated that high school sports should teach core life lessons. Hills noted that teams in a smaller community lean heavily on local residents for support, making it important to return that support.

Aitkin Activities and Athletic Director Jim Henrickson praised the project, citing the school's mandatory community service days as an effort he takes pride in. Henrickson submitted information about the project to the Minnesota State High School League to highlight the team's community impact.
