Amazon has renewed its 103,000-square-foot corporate office lease at the T3 building in Minneapolis's North Loop neighborhood.

The agreement covers the top three floors of the property at 323 Washington Ave. N., keeping the full footprint the company first took in 2016 rather than downsizing. An Amazon spokesperson confirmed the renewal on Friday, October 16, 2026, to the Minneapolis / St. Paul Business Journal, while declining to release financial details or specific terms.

Real estate specialists noted to the Business Journal that the deal represents one of downtown Minneapolis's largest office lease renewals over the past year. CoStar News and the Business Journal reported that the agreement follows law firm Faegre Drinker's renewal of a 175,000-square-foot downtown lease earlier in the year.

Amazon has operated as an anchor tenant in the building for nearly a decade, using the space as a regional tech operations center for corporate engineering staff. While current headcount figures were not disclosed, Amazon noted in 2018 that it had 150 employees at the site and planned to add 200 more. Operations at the facility focus on Amazon Web Services cloud computing, transportation technology, and Amazon Lockers.

The seven-story, 224,000-square-foot building is owned by Chicago-based LaSalle Investment Management, which acquired the property for $87 million in 2018. Developed in 2016 by Houston-based Hines, T3 stands for "transit, timber and technology." It was the first modern mass-timber commercial building of its scale constructed in the United States.

Brokers Brent Robertson and Teig Hutchison of JLL represented LaSalle Investment Management in the transaction. Brian Ginkel and Alex Ach, also of JLL, represented Amazon.

The building amenities include first-floor social lounges, conference areas, a fitness center with showers, 63 underground executive parking spaces, and a rooftop patio with barbecue grills.

Outside of its downtown corporate offices, Amazon has maintained active real estate operations across the region. According to the Minneapolis / St. Paul Business Journal, those projects include distribution hubs in Duluth and potentially Chaska, as well as land acquisitions in Eagan and Becker.
