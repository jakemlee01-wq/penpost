A car-sized gourd grown in an Anoka backyard is officially the heaviest pumpkin in California this autumn. Travis Gienger, a horticulture teacher from Anoka, won first place on Monday, October 12, 2026, at the 53rd Annual Safeway World Championship Pumpkin Weigh-Off in Half Moon Bay, California, according to reporting from the Associated Press and FOX 9. His pumpkin, nicknamed "Rocky," weighed in at 2,389 pounds, or 1,083 kilograms.

The victory earned Gienger $21,501 in prize money, calculated at the competition rate of $9 per pound, the Associated Press reported. Organizers hosted the event at the I.D.E.S. Grounds on Main Street and presented Gienger with the Mel Mello Sr. Grand Champion Grower's Jacket and a Pumpkin King Champion's Ring. Official measurements reported by FOX 9 showed the gourd spanned 138 inches side-to-side, 131.5 inches end-to-end, and measured 231 inches around its circumference.

CBS News reported that Gienger hauled the massive gourd more than 2,150 miles during a 30- to 35-hour drive from Minnesota to Northern California. Gienger described the trip to CBS News, explaining, "It was stressful, you know. You're white-knuckling it and then going through Tahoe. You got all the trucker bumps, and yeah, it's an incredible journey."

The win marks Gienger's fifth career title at Half Moon Bay, following previous victories in 2020, 2022, 2023, and 2024, according to FOX 9. In 2023, he set a world record at the site with a 2,749-pound pumpkin named "Michael Jordan," and he returned in 2024 with a 2,471-pound entry called "Rudy." Following his win on Monday, the Associated Press reported that Gienger declared at the scale, "Five-time champion, that's what we came here to do."

Gienger explained that he named this year's gourd after the cinematic boxer portrayed by Sylvester Stallone because he was seeking a comeback, the Associated Press reported. In 2025, Gienger missed the competition after his pumpkin cracked and broke on the vine during the growing season. Contest organizer Ronan Fowler told the Associated Press that Gienger grew Rocky in an outdoor backyard patch in Anoka while navigating subfreezing fall temperatures. Gienger noted he may transition to a climate-controlled greenhouse in future years to keep pace with top competitors.

Second place went to California grower Jake Leonard of Rocklin, whose 2,273-pound pumpkin earned $3,000, plus a $1,000 bonus for being the heaviest California entry, according to KPIX-TV. Brian Christensen of Rexburg, Idaho, placed third with an 1,873-pound entry to take home $2,500.

While Rocky took top honors in California, the Associated Press noted the pumpkin fell short of a new benchmark set on October 10 by Ron Wallace of Greene, Rhode Island, who grew a 3,156-pound pumpkin at a New England weigh-off. Gienger commented on Wallace's record, telling the Associated Press, "My hat's off to him, that's a big pumpkin."

Gienger and Rocky are scheduled to headline the 54th Annual Half Moon Bay Art and Pumpkin Festival on October 17 and 18, 2026. Event organizers noted the entry will appear in the Great Pumpkin Parade on Main Street before going on display for visitor photos.
