Approximately 30 first responders and public safety officials across Martin and Faribault counties spent five days practicing how to calm high-stress situations. The 40-hour situational crisis intervention training seminar wrapped up on Friday afternoon, October 16, 2026, at the Southern Minnesota Educational Campus at 115 South Park Street in Fairmont, according to reporting from the Fairmont Sentinel.

The sessions brought together personnel from several local emergency services. Participants included six Fairmont police officers, deputies and staff from the Martin County Sheriff’s Office, the Faribault County Sheriff’s Office, the Winnebago Police Department, the Martin County Jail, local Minnesota Department of Corrections officers, and South Central EMS paramedics.

Anna Garbers of Faribault Martin County Human Services coordinated the regional program. A grant was secured to allow all attendees to participate free of charge. Fairmont Police Chief Mike Beletti noted that this marked the first time to his knowledge that the training was hosted locally in Fairmont, rather than requiring area departments to send personnel on travel to the Twin Cities.

The Minnesota Crisis Intervention Team, known as MN CIT, facilitated the coursework. The traveling organization holds sessions across Minnesota to assist municipal and rural agencies in updating crisis response protocols, with the goal of reducing injury risks for civilians and responders alike.

The five-day curriculum trained officers, jail staff, and paramedics in de-escalation communication and scenario assessment. Lessons centered on responding to emergency calls involving severe mental illness, acute emotional trauma, and cognitive disorders. In addition to classroom presentations, the course covered discussions on psychiatric and substance symptoms.

Instruction also depended heavily on live simulations using professional actors. During a Friday exercise, Fairmont Police Officer Klay Brown practiced de-escalation techniques directly with CIT actor Glen Stone, who portrayed an individual experiencing an acute manic episode.

Mental Health Presenter Morganne Napoleoni explained that acting out these scenarios helps responders build muscle memory under stress. Napoleoni emphasized that responders should focus on identifying observable symptoms and behaviors in the field rather than trying to make a medical diagnosis, which allows them to choose the right communication tactics. Napoleoni also commended the local group's dedication, stating that the responders are already doing the work while the program tunes up skills and provides knowledge.

CIT Facilitator Peggy Hutchinson stated that trainers try to build stressful situations during the simulations so that helpful thoughts come to mind when responders face real pressure.

Chief Beletti stated that communication is key to success, and that de-escalating situations to reach a resolution using a minimal amount of force is the safest route. Beletti added that training together ensures law enforcement, corrections, and EMS operate on the same page when resolving calls peacefully or coordinating medical transport.
