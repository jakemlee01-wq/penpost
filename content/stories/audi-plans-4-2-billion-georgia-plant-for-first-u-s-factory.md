German automaker Audi announced plans to invest between $4 billion and $4.2 billion to construct its first dedicated vehicle assembly facility in the United States, according to reporting from Fox Business, Dow Jones Newswires, and The Atlanta Journal-Constitution.

President Donald Trump and Audi Chief Executive Officer Gernot Döllner first announced the investment during a campaign event in Perry, Georgia, on Saturday, Oct. 24, 2026, ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm elections. Official corporate and gubernatorial releases followed on Sunday, Oct. 25, Reuters and The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported. Trump attributed Audi's decision to federal import tariffs, according to ET Auto.

Döllner called the project a long-term investment and a fantastic opportunity, stating that the company believes in the American market. The office of Georgia Gov. Brian Kemp stated that the facility will deliver historic investment and jobs to middle Georgia. Döllner confirmed that while preliminary agreements are set, parent company Volkswagen Group's supervisory board must still vote on a final formal authorization of the capital expenditure, Reuters reported.

The plant will be constructed at the 1,500-acre Middle Georgia Megasite off Georgia Highway 96 on the eastern outskirts of Fort Valley in Peach County, about five miles west of Interstate 75 and 25 miles southwest of Macon, according to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution and Trains Magazine. State officials noted that the parcel can expand to 2,400 acres. The property was acquired and marketed collaboratively through the Mid-Georgia Gateway Joint Development Authority, which represents Peach, Houston, and Macon-Bibb counties, Kemp's office and FOX 5 Atlanta reported.

The planned facility is designed to support an annual production capacity of up to 200,000 vehicles once fully operational, shifting assembly lines currently located in Bratislava, Slovakia, Reuters and The Guardian reported. Audi plans to dedicate the site to large premium sport utility vehicles tailored for North American drivers, specifically the Audi Q7, Q8, and Q9 models.

The project is projected to create approximately 4,500 new manufacturing and advanced engineering jobs in middle Georgia, according to Audi and FOX 5 Atlanta. Company projections reported by Fox Business indicate the site could support as many as 30,000 automotive and related supply-chain jobs.

Fox Business reported that groundbreaking and site construction could begin as early as November 2026. According to FOX 5 Atlanta and Trains Magazine, ground preparation and grading are scheduled to begin in 2027, plant construction is slated to start in 2028, and commercial assembly is targeted to launch in the early 2030s, with a projection for 2031.

Trains Magazine reported that the site will be directly rail-served along its northern edge by Norfolk Southern's Albany District main line, providing freight access 171 miles to the Port of Savannah and highway access 98 miles south of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport. FOX 5 Atlanta reported that Georgia Quick Start and local colleges in the Technical College System of Georgia will design a custom training curriculum and build a dedicated local training facility to prepare incoming assembly and maintenance technicians.

Reuters and Seeking Alpha reported that Audi historically imported 100% of its U.S. vehicle fleet from Europe and Mexico, which cost the automaker an estimated 1.2 billion euros ($1.4 billion) in tariffs over the preceding year. Parent company Volkswagen is also expanding its $2 billion Scout Motors plant in Blythewood, South Carolina, by 25,000 units annually, the outlets reported.
