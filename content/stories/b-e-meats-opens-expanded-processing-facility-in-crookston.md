B & E Meats has finished construction and opened an expanded beef and pork processing facility at 330 3rd Avenue SW in Crookston. The addition brings livestock processing on-site, giving the family-owned business the capacity to handle up to 20 cattle and 30 hogs each week, according to reporting by KROX Radio.

The layout includes an enclosed, 24-hour drive-through unloading bay. Livestock producers can pull their trailers completely indoors out of the weather and unload animals directly into holding chutes. Owner Brent Epema collaborated with animal behavior consultants, including Dr. Temple Grandin, to incorporate curved chutes and rails designed to keep cattle calmer and easier to move through the space.

A facility tour detailed by KROX Radio showed a succession of dedicated areas. The building includes holding chutes sized for 20 cows or 30 pigs, a knocking pen for humane slaughter, a hot room, an aging cooler, and a main processing floor. The design also incorporates a large storage cooler for finished orders alongside a designated customer pickup area.

Epema told KROX that the expansion required four years of planning, zoning approvals, and financing. The municipal groundwork was set in October 2025, when the Crookston City Council unanimously approved setback variances and zoning updates to permit heavy industrial slaughterhouse operations in the local industrial district. City leaders acted in part to help resolve severe regional backlogs in meat processing.

Early work at the plant has already included handling livestock that can be difficult to manage. The facility recently processed horned Scottish Highland cattle brought in by a producer from Northwood, North Dakota. Epema reported positive feedback from that farmer, telling KROX, "With the drive-through and the way that we handle the animals here, the farmers are way happier. Our customers are way happier, knowing how they're handled and taken care of."

The business traces back to Epema learning butchering from his father, Kenny Epema, starting out by processing game and livestock together in their garage. Brent Epema went on to purchase Fisher Meats in Fisher, Minnesota, in 2004. Two years later, he acquired the former Camas Concrete property in Crookston, opening full-time year-round in 2007. In 2013, he added a 1,400-square-foot retail and deli space to the site.

Along with handling livestock for area farmers ahead of the fall harvest and hunting season, Epema plans to use the new capacity to meet broader community demand. He also plans to launch an online sales platform for retail products, including bacon, sausages, snack sticks, and custom cuts.
