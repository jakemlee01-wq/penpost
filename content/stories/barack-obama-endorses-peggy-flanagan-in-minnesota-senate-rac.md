Minnesota's open Senate contest picked up a familiar voice from national politics this week. Former President Barack Obama endorsed Democratic nominee and current Lieutenant Governor Peggy Flanagan in a video released Monday, October 26, 2026, CBS Minnesota and KFGO reported.

Flanagan is running to succeed retiring Democratic U.S. Senator Tina Smith, Lakes Area Radio reported. Her opponent is Republican nominee Michele Tafoya, a former television sports broadcaster and podcaster, according to reporting from CBS News and Southernminn.com.

In the endorsement video, Obama recalled Flanagan's early political work on his first presidential campaign. "As an organizer, I learned that real change starts from the ground up. Peggy Flanagan did too," Obama said in the video, according to CBS Minnesota.

Obama credited Flanagan's state-level record in Minnesota, pointing to her leadership in raising the state minimum wage and securing paid family and medical leave, CBS News and KFGO reported. He stated that Flanagan is seeking the Senate seat to fight for universal health care, confront corruption in Washington, and serve as a voice for Minnesotans. In the video, Obama told voters to make a plan to vote by November 3, saying Minnesota "showed the world what's possible when communities stand up for what's right."

CBS News reported that the campaign paired Obama's remarks with footage of community rallies and protests against Operation Metro Surge, a Department of Homeland Security immigration enforcement operation in the Twin Cities. The campaign posted the clip with the caption: "We stood up to Donald Trump once. On November 3rd, let's do it again."

Tafoya picked up her own high-profile backing earlier in October, when President Donald Trump endorsed her at a rally in Youngstown, Ohio, calling her "a very respected person," according to CBS News and KFGO. Tafoya previously urged Trump not to run for president again. Following the endorsement, CBS News reported, Tafoya kept her distance and stated that the only endorsement she cares about is the one voters deliver on November 3.

Flanagan is an enrolled member of the White Earth Band of Ojibwe and would become the first Native American woman to serve in the U.S. Senate if elected, according to reporting from The Guardian and the Star Tribune. A Republican has not won a U.S. Senate election in Minnesota since 2002, CBS News and Southernminn.com noted.

The video arrived two days after an October 24 campaign stop in Duluth, where independent U.S. Senator Bernie Sanders joined Flanagan and Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison before a crowd of about 1,000 people, Lakes Area Radio and Southernminn.com reported.

Polling averages from 270toWin show Flanagan leading Tafoya 47% to 42.6%, Southernminn.com reported, while the Cook Political Report recently adjusted its rating on the race from "Likely Democratic" to "Lean Democratic," according to Newsweek. Voters will decide the seat on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with eight days left in the campaign when the endorsement dropped, CBS News reported.
