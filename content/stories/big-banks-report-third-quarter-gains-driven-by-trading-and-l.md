Goldman Sachs reported on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, that third-quarter net income rose 29% year-over-year to $5.29 billion, or $17.01 per share, according to TradingView, Mint, and CNBC. That beat Wall Street consensus forecasts of $13.02 per share. CNBC and Xinhua reported that Goldman generated $18.68 billion in total net revenue, topping analyst expectations of $16.71 billion to mark the second-best quarterly revenue in firm history. Forbes and Mint noted that equities trading revenue surged 72% to $6.44 billion, offsetting softer fixed-income trading. Goldman also led Wall Street dealmaking advisory tables with $3.22 billion in investment banking fees, a 21% increase from a year earlier, according to Forbes and TradingView. Chief Executive Officer David Solomon stated on an analyst call that the economy remains resilient with a tech supercycle and encouraging regulations, according to Reuters. Solomon also cautioned that data center resistance and geopolitical tension could pressure infrastructure deals, Beinsure reported. Chief Financial Officer Denis Coleman told Beinsure that equity financing demand continues to outpace available banking capacity.

Across at JPMorgan Chase, third-quarter profit rose to $15.3 billion, or $5.57 per share, up from $14.4 billion a year earlier, according to Newsday and Mint. Newsday and Reuters reported that the result fell short of analyst estimates of $5.85 to $5.93 per share, marking the bank's first quarterly profit miss in nearly three years. Xinhua reported that total revenue came in at about $50.2 billion, missing expectations of $51 billion to $51.3 billion, while Quartz reported managed revenue of $50.9 billion, an 8% gain year-over-year. Quartz and Xinhua reported the bank absorbed a $658 million loss on investment securities, without which net income reached $15.8 billion, or $5.75 per share. Forbes and Quartz reported that net interest income, which is the money earned on loans minus what is paid on deposits, grew 12.8% to $27.04 billion alongside a 10% gain in average loans. Equity trading revenue jumped 49% to $5.0 billion, according to Forbes, while fixed-income trading fell 4% and home lending declined 6%, according to Reuters and Yahoo Finance. Chief Executive Officer Jamie Dimon noted strong consumer spending while citing risks from inflation, deficits, and interest rates, Newsday and Xinhua reported. Chief Financial Officer Jeremy Barnum told Reuters and Yahoo Finance that deals were delayed by market volatility rather than canceled.

Wells Fargo posted third-quarter net profit of $5.95 billion, or $1.87 per share, topping analyst expectations, according to TradingView and Xinhua. Total revenue rose 2% year-over-year to $21.87 billion, narrowly missing projections, TradingView reported. Average loans and customer deposits both expanded 11% compared to the prior year, according to TradingView, while net interest income reached $12.52 billion, Forbes reported. Forbes also reported that client assets in wealth and asset management grew to $2.45 trillion, while the bank increased its credit loss provision, which is money set aside for unpaid loans, to $1.03 billion. Chief Executive Officer Charlie Scharf cited wage growth, customer spending, and low loan delinquency rates as signs of consumer resilience, CNBC reported. Xinhua reported that Wells Fargo shares fell more than 2% on Tuesday, while TradingView noted the bank issued guidance projecting $13 billion in net interest income for the fourth quarter and about $50 billion for the full year.

Citigroup reported third-quarter net profit rose 40% year-over-year to $5.25 billion, or $2.85 per share, on $23.85 billion in revenue, TradingView and Xinhua reported. Citigroup reported returning $4.1 billion to shareholders through repurchases and dividends, achieving an 85% payout ratio. Forbes reported Citigroup generated $16.98 billion in net interest income. Chief Executive Officer Jane Fraser noted the revenue print was the bank's highest third-quarter total in a decade, powered by a 15% increase in Services revenue, a 14% rise in Equities, and deal roles with SoftBank and SK Hynix. Even with the earnings beat, Citigroup shares dropped roughly 4% on Tuesday as higher reward costs and credit provisions weighed on card margins, according to TradingView and Xinhua.
