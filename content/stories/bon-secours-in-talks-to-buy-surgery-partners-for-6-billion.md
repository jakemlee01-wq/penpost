Bon Secours Mercy Health is in late-stage negotiations to acquire a controlling majority stake in Surgery Partners, according to reporting from the Financial Times.

The proposed take-private transaction values Surgery Partners at an enterprise value of approximately $6 billion, including roughly $3.5 billion in debt, according to Reuters and the Financial Times. Bon Secours is offering about $20 per share in cash. That price represents a premium of 25% to 25.8% over Surgery Partners' previous closing price of $15.90.

Following reports of the deal, shares of Surgery Partners rose more than 21% in premarket trading on Monday to reach $19.09, Investing.com reported. Prior to that jump, the stock had fallen roughly 31% over the past 12 months, which had reduced the company's equity market value to about $2.1 billion.

Private equity firm Bain Capital will retain its nearly 40% equity stake under the proposed terms rather than cashing out, Reuters and the Financial Times reported. Sources familiar with the talks told both outlets on Sunday that an agreement could be announced as soon as Monday. Reuters reported that neither Bon Secours Mercy Health nor Surgery Partners immediately responded to requests for comment outside of normal business hours.

Surgery Partners operates 176 short-stay surgical facilities and hospitals across 30 states, according to the Financial Times. The acquisition aims to speed up Bon Secours' move toward lower-cost outpatient care, which provides medical procedures that do not require an overnight hospital stay. The Financial Times and Investing.com noted that this strategy follows a similar move earlier this year by hospital chain Ascension, which purchased Amsurg in a $3.9 billion deal.

Bon Secours Mercy Health is one of the largest Catholic nonprofit health systems in the country. It manages 47 hospitals across the United States and Ireland and generates about $14 billion in annual revenue, according to Market Briefs and Seeking Alpha. Earlier this year, Bon Secours sold a majority stake in Ensemble Health Partners to Thoreau Group at an $11.75 billion valuation while keeping a minority share, the Financial Times reported.

A completed deal would end more than a year of stop-start sale efforts for Surgery Partners, according to the Financial Times and Reuters. In June of last year, the company turned down an earlier buyout proposal led by Bain Capital that valued the business at $6.7 billion including debt.
