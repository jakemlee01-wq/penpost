The dating app that once commanded a $15 billion valuation on Wall Street is leaving public markets in an all-cash buyout. Bumble Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement on October 13, 2026, to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings for $1.3 billion, according to reporting from Business Wire and Reuters. Class A shareholders will receive $6.05 per share in cash, while Class B shares are valued at $0.000001, according to SEC filings and BestMediaInfo. Seeking Alpha reported that the $6.05 offer delivers an approximate 138% premium over Bumble’s October 9 closing price and a 119% premium over its 30-day volume-weighted average price.

Shares responded quickly to the deal terms. According to Briefs Finance and Quartz, Bumble’s stock surged roughly 50% on October 12 after reports of discussions surfaced, then jumped another 57% on Tuesday to reach $5.98. Reuters and 24/7 Wall St. noted morning trading gains exceeding 50% immediately following the formal announcement. Business Wire reported that major shareholders delivered requisite approval via written consent, including founder Whitney Wolfe Herd and affiliates of Blackstone, which held about a 14% stake according to Quartz. BestMediaInfo and Quartz reported that equity financing is backed by a commitment letter from Greenoaks Prime Radiant.

The transaction installs new management at the top. Ithaca co-founders and co-CEOs Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman will take over as co-CEOs of Bumble when the transaction closes, marking the first time men will lead the company since its public debut, as reported by Business Insider and BestMediaInfo. Inc. and Quartz noted that Ithaca was formed earlier in 2026, bringing together Gupta, a Sequoia Capital partner and former Instacart chief operating officer and chief financial officer, and Hegeman, who served as chief revenue officer at Meta Platforms and sits on Robinhood’s board.

Wolfe Herd, who founded Bumble in 2014, will step down as chief executive but will remain actively involved by rolling her Class A shares and Beehive Holdings III into a new Topco entity instead of cashing out, according to Inc. and SEC filings. BestMediaInfo and MediaPost reported that an independent Special Committee evaluated the sale, and Wolfe Herd recused herself from board discussions and voting. In remarks reported by Inc. and Business Insider, Wolfe Herd stated that her belief in the company’s impact has never been stronger, that she looks forward to working with Gupta and Hegeman, and that Ithaca believes in the app's women-first mission. In the company's official announcement, Gupta and Hegeman stated that they see huge potential to take Bumble into its next era as dating habits evolve while building on Wolfe Herd's foundation.

The acquisition follows an extended financial slide. MediaPost, Briefs Finance, and Quartz tracked Bumble from its February 2021 initial public offering at $43 per share, which peaked above $85, to a market capitalization that tumbled more than 90% to roughly $335 million by October 9, 2026. BestMediaInfo and MediaPost reported that second-quarter 2026 revenue dropped 15.2% year-over-year to $210.5 million, while paying subscribers fell 16.4% to 3.2 million after a 30% workforce layoff earlier in the turnaround effort. Briefs Finance and Seeking Alpha reported that in August 2026, Bumble moved away from its signature requirement that women send the opening message, allowing men to initiate contact to address user fatigue. SEC filings show the merger pact sets a $29 million company termination fee, a $102 million parent termination fee, and a July 12, 2027 end date. Seeking Alpha and Briefs Finance reported that Bumble will operate as a private company with its headquarters expected to stay in Austin alongside offices in New York City and London.
