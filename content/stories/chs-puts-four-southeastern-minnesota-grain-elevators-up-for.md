Agricultural cooperative CHS Inc. is selling four grain elevator sites and two commercial properties in southeastern Minnesota through a sealed-bid auction, according to reporting by DTN.

The sale encompasses approximately 7.5 million bushels of grain storage capacity across the four elevator facilities. Rochester-based Maas Companies is managing the auction, with sealed bids due by 4:00 p.m. Central Time on Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026.

Three of the facilities, located in Kasson, Ostrander, and Wykoff, will remain open to receive grain deliveries through the conclusion of the 2026 fall harvest. DTN reported that this harvest will mark their final season under CHS ownership.

The Kasson location at 107 8th Ave. SW is the largest property in the portfolio. Marketing materials cited by DTN show it includes roughly 3.5 million bushels of grain storage capacity, an office, a truck scale, a self-serve fueling station, and leased commercial warehouses that generate rental income.

In Ostrander, the elevator facility sits on 15 acres at 110 3rd Street. It includes about 2.2 million bushels of storage capacity, multiple grain receiving systems, a 5,000-bushel-per-hour Zimmerman grain dryer, a machine shed, and a 4,037-square-foot main office building on North Main Street.

The Wykoff elevator occupies nearly 2.5 acres on East Fillmore Street with approximately 1.4 million bushels of storage capacity, a 5,000-bushel-per-hour GSI grain dryer, grain handling equipment, an office, and an adjacent scale property.

A fourth facility in downtown Claremont has 584,000 bushels of storage on roughly two acres along an out-of-service Canadian Pacific rail spur. CHS had already closed elevator operations at the Claremont site, and the cooperative is offering to lease back the office and truck scale for two years with an option for a two-year extension.

The auction also includes two standalone commercial properties. One is a six-acre commercial shop and equipment storage parcel on County Road 1 in Ostrander. The other is a roughly half-acre site next to City Hall in Dodge Center, which contains two warehouse buildings totaling about 2,036 square feet.

The Rochester Post Bulletin and Bring Me The News reported that CHS notified employees in June 2026 that it intended to close or sell the Kasson, Ostrander, and Wykoff elevators after the harvest. CHS also closed its Claremont facility and a grain terminal at the Port of Duluth-Superior in 2025.

Earlier in 2026, CHS reported a second-quarter net loss of $147.1 million, which DTN noted was a 95 percent increase compared to the $75.8 million net loss it posted during the second quarter of 2025.

CHS Senior Vice President of Operations Mike Johnston told Brownfield Ag News that the regional grain market has shifted toward ethanol plants, soybean crush facilities, and Mississippi River terminals. Johnston said thin margins make it uneconomical for CHS to hold grain at local elevators and move it back into those processing markets later in the year.

CHS stated that it plans to keep buying grain from area farmers for direct delivery to regional processing plants, such as its facilities in Mankato and Fairmont, as well as river terminals. The cooperative added that it will continue providing agronomy and energy services in the region.
