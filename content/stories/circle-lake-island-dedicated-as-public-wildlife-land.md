A century ago, golfers rode a ferry across Circle Lake to reach a private island fairway. As Southernminn.com and county records reported, that 1920s resort folded during the Great Depression, and the 101-acre patch of land turned into pasture before trees grew back over the greens.

On Friday afternoon, October 9, 2026, state and local conservation leaders gathered along the shores of Circle Lake to dedicate the island as permanently protected public land, according to reporting by Southernminn.com and KYMN Radio. The dedication expanded the Circle Lake Wildlife Management Area to 262 total contiguous and island acres, Faribault Daily News and Southernminn.com reported.

The island sits in the middle of Circle Lake in Rice County. According to KYMN Radio and Rice County records, a maple-basswood forest rings the waterline, while the interior rolls through open grasslands, brush, and small wetlands. Settlers cleared the original timber during early Minnesota statehood. Now, after decades of natural regrowth, the Trust for Public Land bought the island tract in late 2025 and conveyed it to the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources, APG State News reported. The conservation nonprofit previously bought a mainland tract along the lake's northwest arm in 2017. Minnesota's Outdoor Heritage Fund provided the money for the island acquisition.

The ceremony coincided with Northfield hosting the 14th annual Minnesota Governor's Pheasant Hunting Opener over October 9 and 10, local reports and WJON noted. Attendees along the shoreline included Gov. Tim Walz, U.S. Sen. Amy Klobuchar, DNR Southern Region Wildlife Manager Joe Stangel, and Clean River Partners Executive Director Jennifer Tonko.

Stangel welcomed attendees and praised the multi-year partnership between sporting clubs, local watershed districts, and state agencies. He said the island acquisition, paired with mainland habitat work, will provide hunting opportunities for pheasants, waterfowl, deer, and turkeys, along with fishing and hiking reached by boat from the lake's north landing. In his remarks, Stangel stated that the land will provide wildlife recreation and ecological benefits for future generations.

Tonko spoke about connecting neighbors to the surrounding watershed. She told attendees that sharing the moment outdoors is why Clean River Partners works to connect people and place.

With this addition, Rice County now contains 7,887 acres managed by the DNR, which makes up roughly 2.5% of the county's total area, based on agency data. Statewide, the island joins 1,521 public wildlife management areas covering 1.39 million acres of fields, woods, and wetlands.

While the new island remains open for public outdoor recreation, organizers confirmed that the 2027 Governor's Pheasant Hunting Opener will move to Redwood Falls.
