Clean Energy Economy Minnesota is dedicating $10,000 in scholarship funding to help students train for clean energy and skilled trade careers, according to reporting from KAXE and Minnesota News Connection.

The gift establishes the EmPowering Minnesota Scholars Fund, which provides micro-scholarships of $1,000 each to 10 postsecondary students facing financial hurdles. The fund is run in partnership with the Minnesota State Energy Center of Excellence, an office based in Granite Falls that coordinates energy education across the 33 colleges and universities in the Minnesota State system.

The $10,000 commitment was launched to mark the 10th anniversary of Clean Energy Economy Minnesota, which business leaders formed in 2016. Holly Lahd of the organization stated that investing in the next generation is the best way to mark a decade in operation, adding that graduates who benefit will help the state reach a workforce of 100,000 clean energy employees.

Logan Schrader, executive director of the Minnesota State Energy Center of Excellence, reported that a typical two-year technical program requires roughly 45 credits and costs about $6,000 per year. Schrader stated that the $1,000 awards cover a noticeable portion of those tuition costs, making degree programs more accessible for students aiming to support their families and aid the clean energy transition. Schrader also noted that renewable energy engineers in Minnesota can make over $100,000 a year, describing the scholarships as an investment in wealth building for local families.

Funding will focus on trades tied directly to decarbonization and grid modernization. Eligible career pathways include wind energy technician work, solar photovoltaic installation, electrical trades, powerline transmission, and HVAC and building automation. Campuses offering supported training include Minnesota West Community & Technical College, which runs hands-on solar installation instruction.

Individual campus scholarship committees will disburse the money each year, evaluating applicants on financial need and their interest in energy trades. Other businesses and community groups across Minnesota are also permitted to contribute to the fund through the Energy Center to expand the scholarship pool in future semesters.
