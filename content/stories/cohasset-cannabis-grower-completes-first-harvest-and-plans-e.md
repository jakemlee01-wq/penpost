In Cohasset, crews are wrapping up the first commercial outdoor cannabis harvest for M.N. Technology, a business legally registered as LeafRV Ventures LLC. The harvest marks the completion of the company's inaugural growing season in northern Minnesota, as reported by the Grand Rapids Herald-Review and MN Cannabis Hub.

The enterprise was formed by two neighboring married couples from Harris Township: Mandy Nintzel, who serves as chief executive officer; Andy Nintzel, the chief cultivation officer; Jamielee Maturi, co-owner and chief operations officer; and Craig Maturi, co-owner and vice president of operations, according to reporting from KAXE and the Grand Rapids Herald-Review. After missing out in the state's initial licensing lottery, the partners purchased an eligible cultivation license from an initial winner and finalized the transfer in 2026. State records reported by MN Cannabis Hub show the business holds Office of Cannabis Management cultivator license CUL-L24-000028, with site approval granted on November 4, 2025. Craig Maturi told the outlets that the company is the fourth cultivation business in Minnesota to secure full operational approval for adult-use cannabis.

The outdoor crop stretched across 26 rows, each running roughly 500 feet, as detailed by the Grand Rapids Herald-Review. Plants were grown in 15-gallon fabric pots using clones propagated from mother stock, and every plant was monitored using Metrc, the state's regulatory tracking software. The company divided the field in nearly equal proportions among four cultivars: Aloha Sugar, Kanobi Sunset, Daily Grape, and Killer Cupcake. Independent laboratory testing showed dry-equivalent total THC levels above 22% across all four varieties, ranging from 22.12% for Aloha Sugar to 23.76% for Daily Grape.

M.N. Technology does not sell products directly to consumers through retail dispensaries or farm-gate transactions, according to the Grand Rapids Herald-Review and MN Cannabis Hub. The business operates strictly as a wholesale cultivator, selling harvested flower to licensed extractors, processors, and manufacturers. The outdoor season in Cohasset has relied on an active harvest crew of 21 seasonal workers alongside approximately 12 core employees.

Even as the outdoor harvest concludes, work has shifted toward a permanent indoor cultivation facility on the grounds of the former West Cohasset Bible Chapel, the Grand Rapids Herald-Review reported. Craig Maturi estimated the three-phase construction project at $25 million, stating that the buildout is funded through private investment and business revenue without public grants, state funding, or local tax subsidies. Maturi noted that the company used commercial bank lending solely to purchase the original chapel property, telling the paper, "We are proud to say we did not use $1 of local taxpayer money or any state funding or grants. We did it all on our own."

Phase 1 of the indoor facility is scheduled for completion in 2027, with additional construction phases expected to roll out through 2029, according to the Grand Rapids Herald-Review. Company plans reported by both the Herald-Review and MN Cannabis Hub call for expanding the permanent workforce to between 60 and 70 staff members once indoor operations are running. In addition to local payroll, the company pledged to donate 2% of its annual net profits to nonprofit organizations, regional clubs, and community initiatives across Itasca County.
