A newly appointed congressional commission has started work to evaluate bringing the Weitzman National Museum of American Jewish History into the Smithsonian Institution, the Philadelphia Jewish Exponent reported. The eight commissioners held their inaugural meeting and took their oaths of office on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, administered by Pennsylvania's U.S. senators.

The museum stands on Independence Mall across from Independence Hall in downtown Philadelphia, according to the Jewish News Syndicate. Founded in 1976, the museum moved into a 100,000-square-foot building designed by James Polshek in 2010, and it is the only museum in the country dedicated exclusively to interpreting the American Jewish experience, according to museum records and The Jerusalem Post. If Congress and the president ultimately approve the transfer, the site would become the Smithsonian's first museum focused specifically on Jewish American history and culture, and potentially the network's 22nd museum.

The panel is financed entirely through private donations and operates without federal appropriations or public funds, as reported by The Jerusalem Post and the Philadelphia Jewish Exponent. Congress authorized the study under legislation introduced by Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz, alongside Representative Brendan Boyle and Senator Bob Casey, which received unanimous bipartisan approval before being signed into law in December 2024.

Over the next two years, the commission is tasked with reviewing the museum's collections, governance, finances, long-term operating sustainability, and its role in countering antisemitism. The commissioners must produce a final evaluation report, a fundraising plan, and statutory proposals for Congress and the White House. The Jerusalem Post and the Philadelphia Jewish Exponent reported that the panel may also organize a national conference on American Jewish life, art, and culture. Commission chairman Philip Darivoff stated that the panel intends to finish its work and submit its findings ahead of the two-year deadline, according to the Jewish News Syndicate.

Darivoff, a former longtime board chair and current chairman emeritus of the Weitzman Museum, was appointed to lead the study panel by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Jewish Insider reported. Senate Majority Leader John Thune appointed author and historian Dara Horn as vice chair. The other six members, reported by The Jerusalem Post and the Philadelphia Jewish Exponent, are former University of Pennsylvania President and former U.S. Ambassador to Germany Amy Gutmann, Washington Free Beacon publisher Michael Goldfarb, former U.S. Representative Kathy Manning, New-York Historical Society President and CEO Louise Mirrer, former federal heritage preservation chairman Paul Packer, and presidential historian Tevi Troy.

Darivoff said the commission must carefully examine what is required to bring American Jewish history to a national platform through the Smithsonian, adding that the museum holds a history that is distinctly Jewish and deeply American, according to the Philadelphia Jewish Exponent and The Jerusalem Post. Darivoff told reporters from the Jewish News Syndicate that too many people in America learn about Jews from people who dislike them, arguing that joining the Smithsonian offers an urgent opportunity to counter misinformation.

Horn told interviewers from the Jewish Telegraphic Agency that joining the Smithsonian would help counter an epidemic of ignorance. She observed that American school curricula often limit Jewish history to the Holocaust rather than exploring how Jewish traditions influenced foundational American values like religious pluralism.
