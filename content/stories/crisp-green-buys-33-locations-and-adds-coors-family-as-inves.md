Crisp & Green is acquiring 33 restaurant locations from Salad Collective and taking on the seller's owner, Colorado's Coors family, as its first outside equity investor, according to reports from Restaurant Business and FastCasual.com. The agreement was announced on Tuesday, October 13, 2026.

The deal covers 33 locations operating under the MAD Greens and Snappy Salads brands across Colorado, Texas, and Arizona, as reported by QSR Magazine. Crisp & Green plans to gradually convert and rebrand each site into a Crisp & Green restaurant, redesigning the spaces and swapping in its lineup of scratch-made salads, grain bowls, wraps, sandwiches, and smoothies. Restaurant Business noted that the companies did not release a specific timetable for completing the conversions.

Crisp & Green currently operates approximately 60 restaurants across the Midwest and southern United States, QSR Magazine reported. Founder Steele Smiley opened the chain's first location in Minnesota in 2016. Combining the 33 acquired stores with previously scheduled corporate and franchise openings will push Crisp & Green to 100 total locations by the end of 2026, reaching that mark within a decade of its launch, according to Fast Casual and the Denver Business Journal.

The purchase establishes Crisp & Green's presence in Phoenix, Arizona, and Austin, Texas, while adding store density in Denver, Colorado, and the Dallas to Fort Worth metroplex, QSR Magazine reported. Fast Casual noted that Smiley intentionally prioritized growth throughout the Midwest and central United States instead of primary coastal hubs.

Axios Pro and the Denver Business Journal reported that the Coors family will become a strategic minority partner in Crisp & Green. That investment marks the first institutional or outside equity partner in the restaurant chain's history. Until this transaction, Crisp & Green expanded solely through corporate store development, franchise agreements, and funding from friends and family, according to Fast Casual and EIN Presswire. The Denver Business Journal reported that the financial terms of the purchase and the exact size of the Coors equity stake were not disclosed.

Smiley explained that he made an exception to his stance against outside investment because his strategic direction aligned closely with the Coors family, the Denver Business Journal reported. In statements cited by EIN Presswire and Restaurant Business, Smiley said the company spent a decade proving it could build a national brand through disciplined growth and franchise partners without relying on institutional money. Fast Casual and Restaurant Business reported that Darden Coors said Crisp & Green brings the right leadership, brand, and franchise network to carry out the next phase of that vision.

Salad Collective, based in Golden, Colorado, took shape after the Coors family acquired MAD Greens in 2013 and later merged it with Dallas-based Snappy Salads, according to Restaurant Business. Salad Collective is keeping Tokyo Joe's, an Asian fast-casual chain with roughly 25 locations that serve sushi, poke, rice bowls, and noodles. Restaurant Business also noted that earlier in 2026, Salad Collective reappointed John Montgomery as chief executive officer as Darden Coors stepped into the role of chairman.
