Mall of America announced on Oct. 19 that Dave's Hot Chicken will open a new restaurant inside the Bloomington shopping center this winter. The restaurant will be situated on Level 3, North, near the Culinary on North food court, featuring colorful graffiti-style murals and neon lighting.

Carrie Charleston, vice president and head of leasing at Mall of America, highlighted the brand's bold flavors and passionate fan base, stating that the mall looks forward to welcoming the chain to its dining lineup.

A separate franchisee is handling the Mall of America development. Franchisee Alex Humphries noted that his family group's exclusive Minnesota franchise rights exclude non-traditional locations such as airports and the mega-mall.

The Humphries group is directing its efforts toward regional retail corridors. The chain opened its first St. Cloud restaurant on Friday, Oct. 23, in a 2,600-square-foot end-cap space at Rivertown Village Shopping Center, 3959 2nd Street South, Suite 214. The opening fills a former GAP store that sat vacant for years.

For the St. Cloud debut, KNSI reported that the first 100 guests receive free slider cards redeemable on future visits, along with commemorative aluminum cups.

Humphries stated that St. Cloud fits the company's broader expansion strategy alongside Rochester and Mankato, pointing to strong regional centers with growing populations, active retail corridors, and the ability to draw customers from surrounding towns.

The next site on the schedule will be Mankato. Humphries told Bring Me The News that the Mankato shop is on track to open before Thanksgiving.

That Mankato launch will mark the 12th Dave's Hot Chicken in Minnesota. Seven of those restaurants have opened across the state over the past 13 months, according to reporting by Bring Me The News.

The broader Minnesota expansion is run by a family group that includes former NBA player Kris Humphries and his cousin Alex Humphries. The team opened the state's first Dave's Hot Chicken in Minnetonka in June 2023 with an initial target of 10 Twin Cities shops, before raising the goal to 30 locations statewide.

The California-based company was founded in 2017 when four friends pooled $900 for a deep fryer in an East Hollywood parking lot. The business later attracted celebrity backing from investors including Drake, Samuel L. Jackson, and Usher.

The menu centers on jumbo hand-breaded Nashville-style hot chicken tenders and sliders, served with kale-slaw, mac and cheese, and seasoned fries. Meals are offered in seven heat levels ranging from No Spice up to Reaper, an option that requires diners to sign a liability waiver.
