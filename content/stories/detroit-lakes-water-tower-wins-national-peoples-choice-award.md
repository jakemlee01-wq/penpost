The Detroit Lakes municipal water tower won the People's Choice award in the 2026 Tank of the Year contest on October 19, 2026, after receiving the highest total public votes nationwide, according to Lakes Area Radio (KDLM) and industrial coating manufacturer Tnemec Company, Inc.

Online balloting ran from October 5 through October 16, 2026, with the public restricted to one vote per email address, FOX 9 reported. As reported by FOX 9 and the Geneseo Current, Detroit Lakes moved up from second place in the final days of voting, surpassing national contenders from Staunton, Virginia, and South Haven, Michigan. The structure also received more votes than nine other nominated Minnesota tanks located in Burnsville, Center City, Conger, Mound, North Branch, Prior Lake, Sauk Rapids, Stillwater, and St. Paul.

The competition marks the 20th anniversary of Tnemec's program, which was founded in 2006 to recognize coating projects and local landmarks across North America. In remarks reported by FOX 9, Kevin Morris, director of water and wastewater sales at Tnemec, stated that while water towers serve as critical infrastructure, the contest gives communities an opportunity to celebrate hometown pride and recognize tanks that have become part of what makes their community home.

The new elevated structure stands 127 feet tall, making it 27 feet taller than the city's previous 100-foot tower, according to reporting from Hoodline and KSTP. Tnemec project records show that the 1.5-million-gallon tank replaced a 39-year-old, 1.0-million-gallon multi-leg tower on the same site. By raising the water system's hydraulic grade, or baseline water pressure level, by 23 feet, the new facility adds 10 pounds per square inch of pressure for local residents and opens previously undevelopable sections of the Detroit Lakes Public Utilities service area.

Hoodline and dl-online.com reported that the project was part of a broader $9.9 million utility upgrade funded through a low-interest loan from the Minnesota Public Facilities Authority. Landmark Construction Co. secured the $8.3 million bid to build the tower and demolish both the aging structure and an old standpipe across the lake. Hoodline reported that foundation work required 116 steel piles driven at least 80 feet deep, followed by a concrete pedestal built at eight feet per day using jump-form techniques, a process using vertically moving concrete forms. On April 13, 2026, crews lifted the 320,000-pound steel bowl into place during a two-hour hoist.

Visible from Detroit Lake and Highway 10, the exterior mural depicts an endless summer sunset skyline over the lakefront with sailboats and shoreline pine trees, according to Lakes Area Connect and Apex Engineering Group. The artwork was designed through a partnership between the project design team and a graphic designer native to Detroit Lakes. Viking Industrial Painting applied the protective coating, and KLM Engineering handled inspection, using specialized systems including Tnemec Series 700 HydroFlon, Series 91-H₂O Hydro-Zinc MCU, Series 21 Epoxoline, Series 1095 Endura-Shield, and Series N140 Pota-Pox Plus.

With the People's Choice win, Detroit Lakes joins 11 other regional finalists under review for the overall 2026 Tank of the Year, which FOX 9 reported will be determined by a Tnemec committee and announced on October 23, 2026. All 12 finalists will appear in Tnemec's 2027 calendar, with the overall winner featured on the January page.
