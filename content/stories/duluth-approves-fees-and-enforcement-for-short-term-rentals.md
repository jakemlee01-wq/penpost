The Duluth City Council met on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, to vote on fee structures and compliance resolutions for short-term vacation rentals, according to reporting from Northern News Now.

The resolutions establish fees across the entire permitting process, covering applications and safety inspections. As reported by Northern News Now, the schedule is modeled after Duluth's existing fee structure for long-term rental licenses.

Municipal oversight, licensing, and code compliance are shifting away from the Planning department to the city's Life Safety Division, according to Duluth City Council records and the North Shore Journal. The Life Safety Division already administers long-term rental inspections and possesses dedicated enforcement mechanisms.

The compliance actions support Ordinance 26-038-O, which passed unanimously with a 9 to 0 vote on Sept. 14, 2026, Bring Me The News reported. The ordinance bans property owners from converting additional standalone single-family homes into non-owner-occupied vacation rentals. The newly approved regulatory enforcement mechanisms and overarching rules take effect on Oct. 14 and 15, 2026.

Duluth city staff estimate that while about 200 vacation rentals hold legal permits, between 400 and 800 unpermitted properties have operated across the city outside local rules, without paying fees or undergoing safety inspections, according to the North Shore Journal and Bring Me The News.

Elected leaders pointed to local housing pressures during the process. Bring Me The News reported that Mayor Roger J. Reinert posted on social media that vacation rentals have absorbed almost 2% of Duluth's entire housing stock away from permanent residents, contributing to housing scarcity and higher prices. Councilor David Clanaugh stated that unlicensed rentals have placed excessive pressure on neighborhoods during an acute housing and homelessness crisis.

Public testimony also addressed neighborhood effects. Bring Me The News reported that Dawn Buck, vice chair of Minnesota Point 50, testified before the council that change is needed to preserve the community, calling past lack of enforcement unacceptable. Council President Lynn Nephew described the framework as a place to start, stating it offers tools to address unlicensed operators while keeping compliant owners intact.

Existing compliant operators remain grandfathered under Section 29-12 of the ordinance. However, licenses are strictly non-transferable, meaning that if a permitted property is sold, the permit expires and cannot transfer to the buyer. Citywide caps on standard non-owner-occupied vacation dwelling units remain between 60 and 120 permits, the North Shore Journal reported.

The framework preserves owner-occupied rental options, according to records from City of Duluth Planning and the North Shore Journal. Those options include accessory home shares and accessory vacation dwelling units limited to 21 unhosted nights per year, as well as rentals located in commercial zoning districts.

Duluth's temporary one-year moratorium on new short-term rental licenses officially concludes in November 2026. Council leadership noted plans to conduct annual evaluations and administrative reviews to update rental fees and enforcement rules.
