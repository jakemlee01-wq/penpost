Developers and civic leaders broke ground on Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026, on The Legacy, a new five-story apartment building across from Lake Superior College. Reported by Wisevoter and the Duluth Area Chamber of Commerce, the project is a joint effort involving developer Titanium Partners, contractor T-2 Inc. led by Pete Pavlovich, Lake Superior College, the City of Duluth, and the Duluth Housing and Redevelopment Authority.

The complex is located at 2029 Trinity Road, directly across from the college's main campus at 2101 Trinity Road near Miller Creek. Titanium Partners confirmed the building will sit on property previously used as the college's lower parking lot. The development will contain 148 residential units, consisting predominantly of studio apartments along with select one-bedroom and two-bedroom layouts.

According to the Duluth Area Chamber of Commerce and reporting by Howie Hanson, 30 of the 148 units are designated as income-restricted affordable housing. Those 30 homes, representing roughly 20% of the building, are reserved for tenants earning 50% or less of the Area Median Income under an agreement with the Duluth Housing and Redevelopment Authority. Titanium Partners noted that while the complex is open to the general public, it is planned to house students, college faculty, alumni, and local workers in fields such as healthcare, aviation, manufacturing, and the skilled trades.

Duluth Mayor Roger Reinert attended the ceremony, stating on social media that Lake Superior College will have housing right on campus for the first time. Records on the Duluth City Council citizen portal show that David Kline, the college's vice president of institutional advancement and external relations, told city officials during review that adjacent housing will serve as a recruitment tool and ease local housing vacancy pressures.

Securing the project involved public financing to cover construction gaps. As reported by Howie Hanson and Duluth HRA filings, HRA Executive Director Jill Keppers helped organize a dedicated housing tax increment financing district, an arrangement where future property tax gains help pay for upfront development costs. In September 2026, the Duluth City Council passed Resolution 793, authorizing a housing tax increment financing package worth approximately $6 million over 26 years to offset rising expenses.

Filings reviewed by Wisevoter show the property will include a mix of underground, indoor, and outdoor surface parking spaces. The building was designed by DSGW Architects and TJPA Architectural Design, with engineering handled by Northland Consulting Engineers and SAS+Associates. According to reporting by Howie Hanson and Titanium Partners, developers and college representatives noted the Trinity Road housing initiative was under discussion and planning for nearly a decade before securing final permits and financing. Framing and ground preparation are underway, with construction scheduled to take 18 months toward an opening in spring 2028.
