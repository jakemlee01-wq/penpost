Duluth Cargo Connect was named the 2026 Port/Terminal Operator of the Year at the eighth Heavy Lift Awards, organized by Heavy Lift & Project Forwarding International at the Royal Lancaster in London. The honor marks the second time in seven years that the Duluth terminal has won the worldwide title, following its first award in Antwerp, Belgium, in 2019, according to reporting by Boatnerd and the Heavy Lift Awards.

Duluth Cargo Connect is an operating partnership between the Duluth Seaway Port Authority, which owns the physical assets, and Lake Superior Warehousing, which operates the terminal, as reported by WDIO-TV and the Great Lakes Commission. An international panel of project cargo shippers, freight forwarders, ocean carriers, and analysts selected Duluth from a shortlist of contenders that included facilities in Italy and the Netherlands, Boatnerd reported.

The winning submission centered on a $19.4 million pier redevelopment at the Clure Public Marine Terminal, Boatnerd reported. The project turned an idle, century-old property into an expanded dock for heavy-lift and oversized cargo. The site originally held a grain elevator built in 1907 that ceased operations in the 1980s and was torn down by the port authority in 1997. Work on the revitalization began in 2015, supported by a $10 million U.S. Department of Transportation grant that funded a roll-on, roll-off dock built in 2016.

The most recent phase finished in 2025 with a $10.5 million reconstruction of Berths 10 and 11. That project added 1,200 linear feet of steel dock wall, reinforced concrete decking, and new mooring bollards, Boatnerd reported. The berths entered commercial service in April 2026, when the tug Meredith Ashton and a 240-foot deck barge arrived with project cargo, making it the first vessel to dock at Berth 10 in several decades.

Additional terminal upgrades accompanied the dock work, according to Boatnerd. The operator invested $2 million in nine new forklifts, including a 70-ton capacity unit designed to lift oversized industrial components. Another $750,000 went toward U.S. Customs and Border Protection cargo inspection infrastructure to speed up clearance for container handling and imported goods. Through newly built space and building acquisitions, the terminal's total warehousing footprint expanded to more than 800,000 square feet.

Kevin Beardsley, executive director of the Duluth Seaway Port Authority, said the award reflects decades of work by past port leadership, government partners, and Lake Superior Warehousing to turn an abandoned pier into productive shipping infrastructure. Beardsley added that earning the recognition a second time shows that the long-term investment is paying dividends.
