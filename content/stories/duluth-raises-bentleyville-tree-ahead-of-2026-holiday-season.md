Iron Workers Local 512 assembled and hoisted the 128-foot-tall, 17-ton steel Christmas tree into place at Duluth's Bayfront Festival Park on Saturday, October 10, 2026, as reported by Northern News Now and WDIO. The installation marks the start of park construction for the Bentleyville Tour of Lights, which enters its 23rd season overall and its 18th consecutive year at the waterfront park.

On Sunday, October 11, Duluth Mayor Roger Reinert rode an aerial boom lift to the top of the tree frame to inspect the assembly, according to Destination Duluth and the mayor's office. Mayor Reinert officially certified the tree ready for the 2026 holiday season, noting that setup took place in unseasonable weather near 80 degrees while hundreds of volunteers worked across the grounds.

The centerpiece consists of three 40-foot sections crowned by an 8-foot ball and strung with 150,000 LED lights, reported Northern News Now. The structure required stabilization work earlier in 2026 after high winds damaged the upper 40-foot section during off-season storage, prompting Iron Workers Local 512 and Sunbelt Rentals to complete emergency repairs, according to 98.1 Minnesota's New Country.

Initial site preparation began in late September, when volunteer crews moved tunnel displays and equipment onto the 20-acre park using 18 semi-trailers and 19 shipping containers, according to Wisevoter. Bentleyville organizers noted that building the walk-through layout, which features more than 5 million lights, takes over 1,200 volunteer hours each autumn. Work sessions are scheduled on Wednesdays from 5 to 8 p.m. and weekends from 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. through mid-November, Destination Duluth reported.

Before the full park opens, Bentleyville will host the Red Kettle Karnival on Wednesday, November 4, from 5 to 8 p.m. Visitors who donate non-perishable food or new toys to The Salvation Army will receive a ticket to walk inside the 128-foot tree frame. The tour officially opens to the public on Saturday, November 21, and runs nightly through Saturday, December 26, WDIO reported.

The event draws roughly 300,000 visitors from across the Midwest each year while keeping admission, cookies, hot cocoa, popcorn, and children's knit hats free. Bentleyville USA reported that its Santa Hat Program plans to distribute 30,000 knit hats and bags of cookies to visiting children aged 12 and under this winter. Studies from the University of Minnesota Duluth Bureau of Business and Economic Research and festival organizers estimate the attraction generates between $21 million and $24 million in regional economic impact.
