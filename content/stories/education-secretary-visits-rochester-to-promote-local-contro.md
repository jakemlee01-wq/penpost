On Wednesday, October 14, 2026, U.S. Secretary of Education Linda McMahon walked through hands-on technical classrooms at Rochester Community and Technical College, testing a computerized law enforcement simulation. The visit to Olmsted County marked the 44th stop on her national Returning Education to the States Tour, which focuses on shifting educational decision-making and authority from the federal government to state and local entities.

During the visit to the college's Heintz Center campus, McMahon was joined by Minnesota U.S. Representatives Brad Finstad and Pete Stauber. The group toured workforce training programs designed for direct-hire industries in Southeast Minnesota, examining facilities for automotive repair, dental simulations, carpentry, and building maintenance. Both McMahon and Finstad tested the college's computerized peace officer training system, MPR News reported.

According to the U.S. Department of Education, the nationwide tour is designed to review local operations and compile a federal toolkit of best practices to help states assume primary education administration. In a statement released by her department, McMahon stated that responsive education systems cannot be driven by federal mandates, adding that success occurs when local educators and state leaders have autonomy to match programs to community workforce needs.

Before arriving in Rochester, McMahon started Wednesday in Eagan at Great Oaks Academy, a K to 10 public charter school. Students there gave a choir performance, read fifth-grade poetry, and recited the Pledge of Allegiance in Latin. McMahon met with teachers at the school before touring curriculum programs in electrical linework, veterinary technology, and process technology at Dakota County Technical College, followed by a luncheon with state lawmakers.

In Rochester, McMahon defended sweeping federal spending cuts and workforce reductions within her agency, asserting that states are better equipped to run educational systems, according to MPR News. During McMahon's 16-month tenure, the administration laid off nearly 50 percent of the Department of Education's federal workforce. MPR News reported that the administration also transferred more than 100 statutory duties and programs, including special education services and Title I grants, to the Department of Labor and the Department of Health and Human Services.

An NPR investigation cited by MPR News and KSTP 5 Eyewitness News reported that department staff members found the cuts and transfers made federal administration more complex, costly, and inefficient, hindering its ability to manage civil rights oversight and distribute formula funding.

Addressing those reductions, McMahon told MPR News that other federal agencies already have some operations running and possess better technology to distribute grant funds. Calling the initiative a proof of concept, McMahon told KSTP 5 Eyewitness News that the administration is consolidating programs, moving duties to other agencies, and working to finish the job.

Following the Rochester visit, McMahon departs for the 45th stop of the national tour in New Hampshire, according to KSTP 5 Eyewitness News.
