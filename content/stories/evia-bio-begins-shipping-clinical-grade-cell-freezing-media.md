Freezing living human cells for medicine usually relies on dimethyl sulfoxide, an industry-standard chemical that prevents ice crystals but can degrade stem cells and trigger toxic side effects in patients.

Minneapolis life sciences startup Evia Bio announced on Monday, October 19, 2026, that the clinical-grade version of its alternative freezing mixture, CellShield MSC, is now available for commercial customer shipments. The product is manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice standards, which are strict quality benchmarks required for clinical use, for regenerative medicine clinics and therapy developers.

Customers can purchase the media directly through Evia Bio's online portal for immediate direct delivery, as reported by BioSpace. The liquid is produced by Teknova, a commercial supplier of critical reagents for cell therapies, vaccines, and molecular diagnostics, under an ongoing manufacturing partnership.

CellShield MSC is entirely free of dimethyl sulfoxide, or DMSO. The formulation stems from University of Minnesota laboratory research led by founder Allison Hubel, an expert cryobiologist. Chief Executive Officer Jeffrey Allen stated that the non-toxic mixture delivers equivalent or better performance compared to conventional standards while protecting patient safety at the point of care.

To preserve delicate samples, the system pairs its chemical formulation with a proprietary controlled-rate freezing profile designed to maximize post-thaw recovery, attachment, viability, and cell function. The liquid is also protein-free and fully defined, meaning developers can skip post-thaw washing steps and scale up to large batch sizes without compromising cell phenotype or differentiation potential.

According to BioSpace, the clinical-grade product specifically targets developers advancing mesenchymal stem cell therapies from preclinical stages into human trials and commercial production. Evia Bio first introduced the product line in November 2025, when it launched a research-grade version priced at $550 per 100-milliliter kit, BioSpace and Medical Alley reported.

The commercial rollout follows a 4.7 million euro seed-stage investment round tracked in September 2026 to support production scaling. Evia Bio operates out of 2001 6th St. SE in Minneapolis, where it originally spun out of university research under the name BlueCube Bio before rebranding in December 2021, according to university records.
