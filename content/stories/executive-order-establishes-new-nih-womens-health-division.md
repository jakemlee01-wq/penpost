On Monday, Oct. 19, 2026, President Donald Trump signed an executive order in the White House Rose Garden titled "Advancing Women's Health to Improve Outcomes in All Phases of Life," reported CBS News and HHS.

First Lady Melania Trump championed the policy, addressing attendees and symbolically signing the document alongside the president after holding working sessions with doctors, medical researchers, and HHS leadership, according to the Associated Press and CBS News.

CBS News and Al Jazeera reported that Melania Trump told the gathering, "Beginning today, we will change the reality of women’s health. Previous generations fought for women’s rights. Our generation will fight to discover the cures that will ensure a woman can live her life in health and to the fullest." President Trump remarked during the ceremony, "Think of it, a women's health division. Who the hell wouldn't have a women's health division? Melania said, 'They don't have it,'" as reported by CBS News.

The directive orders the Department of Health and Human Services to establish a dedicated Women's Health Division within the National Institutes of Health within 90 days, according to Contemporary OB/GYN and Fierce Healthcare. Reporting from Becker's Hospital Review and Fierce Healthcare notes this new division will not replace the NIH Office of Research on Women's Health, which was established in 1990. Instead, it will operate alongside that office and enforce compliance with rules requiring scientific research to account for sex as a biological variable. The division is also tasked with setting accountability metrics for grant recipients to ensure women and girls are included in clinical trials, Contemporary OB/GYN reported.

The order concurrently creates the White House Women's Health Task Force, which convened its first working session on Monday afternoon, according to The American Presidency Project. The Associated Press and CBS News reported that the task force is co-chaired by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, who recently completed treatment for breast cancer. Al Jazeera and Becker's Hospital Review reported that the group will coordinate an all-of-government strategy across federal agencies, with administrative expenses covered by HHS, according to the AP and Dailyfly News.

The White House outlined Melania Trump's "Five Pillars of Women's Health Innovation." As reported by CBS News and Newsmax, these pillars focus on funding cures for conditions disproportionately impacting women, using artificial intelligence to accelerate discoveries, speeding therapies from labs to patients, ensuring affordability, and eliminating taboos surrounding women's health topics.

According to the American Hospital Association and Fierce Healthcare, the order prioritizes research into cardiovascular disease, stroke, Alzheimer's disease, maternal health, menopause, infertility, endometriosis, uterine fibroids, polyendocrine metabolic ovary syndrome, osteoporosis, breast cancer, and autoimmune disorders.

Federal health officials voiced support for the effort in announcements cited by Becker's Hospital Review. NIH Director Dr. Jay Bhattacharya said the division will close evidence gaps, maximize research investments, and bring discoveries closer to patients. CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz stated the action will help prevent disease, diagnose conditions earlier, and expand treatment options.

Rep. Diana Harshbarger, a Tennessee Republican who attended the signing, stated the policy builds on tools like Moms.gov and TrumpRx fertility savings, according to her office.

The executive order did not allocate a specific dollar amount or new budget line, Becker's Hospital Review reported, noting that NIH currently directs about 10% of its budget, roughly $5 billion annually, toward women's health research. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, a Connecticut Democrat and ranking member of the House Appropriations Labor-HHS Subcommittee, criticized the administration's fiscal 2027 budget proposal, warning it would cut overall NIH funding by $6 billion, or 12%, despite the new directive.
