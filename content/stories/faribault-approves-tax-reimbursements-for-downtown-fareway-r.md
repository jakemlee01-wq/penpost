The Faribault City Council approved an agreement to reimburse up to $273,000 of Fareway's property taxes over a 10-year period, as reported by the Faribault Daily News. The funding supports the grocery chain's plan to demolish and rebuild its downtown supermarket at 430 Second Ave. NW.

Fareway officials stated that the project would not move forward without the tax abatement, according to Faribault Community and Economic Development Director Dave Wanberg. Southernminn.com reported that Fareway will not seek property tax abatements from Rice County or the local school district.

The reimbursement period is scheduled to start on Aug. 1, 2029, calculated from the site's assessed value on Jan. 2, 2027. Faribault will rebate 100% of its collected property taxes on the property back to Fareway during the first two years. That reimbursement rate will decrease by 10% every two years across the 10-year span.

The existing downtown grocery store is scheduled to close in mid-January, with demolition taking place in January and February 2027. Construction of the replacement supermarket is scheduled for completion in time for Thanksgiving 2027.

According to city plans and Southernminn.com, the modern replacement building will measure roughly 25,000 square feet. That footprint is slightly smaller than the current building, allowing room for an expanded parking lot. The Shelby Report noted that Dean Snyder Construction was selected as the general contractor, and the plans incorporate a 2,700-square-foot Fareway Liquor store, backup generator infrastructure, and accommodations for a rooftop solar array.

Fareway confirmed that the new store will retain its full-service bakery, meat department, and current inventory. Koby Pritchard stated that keeping the store downtown remained Fareway's top priority despite logistical hurdles. Fareway CEO Reynolds W. Cramer described the project as a long-term commitment to downtown Faribault, and Faribault Mayor Tom Spooner stated that partnering on the store meets an investment local residents have sought.

While the downtown site is closed for construction, Fareway will operate an interim order pickup hub in the Fourth Street commercial strip near Papa Murphy’s and Great Clips. Orders placed through Fareway's website and app during this period will be filled and delivered from the chain's Owatonna store.

The Shelby Report also reported that Fareway has pledged to offer all current Faribault employees temporary positions at other company stores during construction, along with jobs in the new downtown building once it opens.
