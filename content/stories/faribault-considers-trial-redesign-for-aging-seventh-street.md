Seventh Street in Faribault has reached the end of its lifespan, and the city is looking at major redesigns before rebuilding it. Faribault City Engineer Mark DuChene presented long-term redevelopment options to the Faribault City Council on October 13, according to reporting from the Faribault Daily News and Southernminn.com.

Most municipal streets in Faribault operate on a 50- to 60-year cycle. Seventh Street has hit that mark, requiring a complete reconstruction. The mile-long stretch also covers some of Faribault's oldest underground utilities, meaning the city will have to replace water mains, sanitary sewers, and storm sewers. DuChene also noted that the existing bridge along the corridor fails to comply with state aid standards.

Southernminn.com reported that the intersection of Seventh Street and Second Avenue handles the corridor's heaviest traffic and logs the highest crash numbers. City staff are considering putting a new compact roundabout there to manage the flow. Faribault is also weighing whether to convert intersections at Fourth Street, Eighth Street, and Central Avenue into four-way stops.

City staff brought forward two roadway layouts after collecting feedback from more than 200 residents across three public informational open houses. The first option is a two-lane design that allows on-street parking along the north side of certain segments. The second is a wider three-lane design with a center two-way left-turn lane to help drivers turn into and out of private driveways.

Both designs include an off-street multi-use trail on the north side of Seventh Street and a pedestrian sidewalk on the south side. DuChene recommended testing the three-lane design on a three-year trial run. That trial would let the city monitor traffic flow and safety before locking in permanent pavement markings and roadway geometry.

Southernminn.com reported that city engineering staff plan to finish the feasibility study and cost projections by November 2026. The city plans to use that finished study to apply for federal highway grant money for 2030 and 2031, with groundbreaking targeted for 2030.
