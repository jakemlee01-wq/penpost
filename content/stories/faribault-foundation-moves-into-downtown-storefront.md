The Faribault Foundation is moving its administrative office into the heart of downtown Faribault after receiving a building renovation at zero expense. The Faribault Daily News reported that local developer TriMo Development covered the project as a community contribution.

According to reporting by Southernminn.com, the nonprofit is relocating to a 781-square-foot storefront at 10 Third St. NE, located just off Central Avenue near the Faribault Post Office. TriMo Development co-owner Trevor Morris is leasing the renovated office space to the organization. Morris and his custom home building firm provided all labor and materials free of charge to complete the full interior and structural renovation.

The foundation manages endowed funds, which are invested charitable dollars meant to produce ongoing income, and distributes grants to area educational, artistic, and social programs. Foundation officials noted that eliminating the capital renovation expenses ensures charitable dollars remain reserved for local grants rather than facilities overhead.

The transition has been led by Faribault Foundation Executive Director Laura Bock and Morris. Bock noted that the organization's earliest directors worked off kitchen tables and used their personal cell phones to take calls. The move wraps up several months of internal planning to secure standalone quarters. The foundation previously operated out of shared office space at the Faribault Area Chamber of Commerce and Tourism building at 530 Wilson Ave.

The new street-level storefront is designed to improve access and visibility for donors, grant applicants, and civic partners. The finished interior features a workstation for an executive assistant, private spaces for donor consultations, and enlarged meeting rooms, including a large conference room for the board of directors. The upgraded layout is set up to host gatherings for local nonprofit boards and advisory committees.

Downtown advocates noted that filling the commercial address supports economic vitality and the preservation of historic downtown Faribault. Morris stated that the foundation is dedicated to the city, adding that contributing the work so the group can give back to the town felt like a win. Looking at the wider area, Morris also stated that over the next 10 years, Faribault will be half the price of Lakeville or Elko, making the town's housing price point a major draw for home buyers.

Southernminn.com reported that the foundation completed the relocation in October 2026. The organization mounted its exterior signage and opened its doors for public walk-ins and consultations ahead of the year-end charitable giving season.
