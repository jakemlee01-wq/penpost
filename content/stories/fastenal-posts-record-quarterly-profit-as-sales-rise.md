Fastenal Company generated global sales of $825 million in September, a 4% increase compared to the same month last year. Daily sales for the month grew 14.4% across all customer segments, end markets, and geographic regions, according to reporting from Industrial Distribution and TipRanks.

The September results closed out a third quarter that ended September 30, 2026, with net sales climbing 15.5% year-over-year to $2.463 billion, Business Wire and Industrial Distribution reported. Operating income rose 20.2% to $530.8 million. Seeking Alpha and Industrial Distribution noted that this $90 million annual increase represents the largest single-quarter operating profit increase in the company's corporate history.

From its headquarters at 2001 Theurer Boulevard in Winona, Fastenal remains the city's largest employer. The Winona Journal reported that the company's local payroll includes approximately 1,800 workers, while its total global workforce expanded by 1% over the past year to nearly 27,500 people.

Even with global head count up, the distributor's on-site sales force declined by more than 400 positions during the quarter. Executives cited by IndustryWeek and BigGo Finance attributed that reduction to digital platform efficiencies and structural automation rather than layoffs.

Quarterly net income reached $401.6 million, an increase of 19.7%, bringing diluted earnings per share to $0.35 from $0.29 a year earlier. Operating margin, which measures how much profit a company keeps from each dollar of revenue after operational expenses, improved by up to 90 basis points to 21.5%.

During an earnings call covered by Seeking Alpha and BigGo Finance, Chief Executive Officer Jeffery Watts said sales grew 15.5% while earnings grew faster, pointing to market share gains among key accounts and new contract wins. Chief Financial Officer Max Tunnicliff said demand improved and expense controls converted higher revenue into expanded operating margins.

Contract customers generated more than 76% of total net sales, with daily sales in that category rising 18.8%. In coverage from IndustryWeek and Seeking Alpha, Fastenal reported that roughly 3,300 customer locations spent at least $50,000 per month during the summer, a 19% gain from a year ago. Sites spending at least $10,000 monthly increased 8% to 13,135.

Industrial activity varied by sector. Daily sales in heavy manufacturing rose 20.2%, helped by orders from aerospace, machined products, and data center projects, according to reporting by Intellectia.AI and TipRanks. At the same time, business softened in agriculture, food production, and non-residential building.

Gross margin declined by roughly 40 to 50 basis points to 44.8%. Corporate filings and trade reports indicated that price and cost pressures, along with business shifting toward larger national contracts, offset savings gained from direct product sourcing, supplier rebates, and tariff refunds.

Fastenal generated $422 million in operating cash flow during the quarter. The company sent $328 million back to investors through $298 million in cash dividends and $30 million in share repurchases, buying back 650,000 shares at an average price of $46.49.

Looking ahead, company management kept its full-year guidance of 27,000 to 29,000 weighted device signings for Fastenal Managed Inventory hardware. Corporate filings also projected full-year 2026 capital spending between $310 million and $330 million, with a goal of reaching price-cost neutrality by the first quarter of 2027. Shares traded between $51 and $52 following the financial release, extending a six-month upward stretch on the Nasdaq exchange.
