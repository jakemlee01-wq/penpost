On Monday, Oct. 19, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration granted accelerated approval to dostarlimab-gxly, marketed as Jemperli by GSK, for adult patients with stage II or III locally advanced rectal cancer. The approval applies to tumors identified by an FDA-authorized test as mismatch repair deficient (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H).

As reported by Fierce Pharma and GSK, dostarlimab is the first and only immunotherapy approved in the United States for locally advanced rectal cancer. The American Journal of Managed Care, MedPage Today, and GSK reported that this genetic signature impairs the cellular mechanism that repairs DNA copying errors, making tumors vulnerable to PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors. The biomarker appears in approximately 3% to 10% of rectal cancer diagnoses.

According to the FDA and Oncology Nursing News, the regulatory decision was supported by interim results from the multicenter, open-label phase 2 AZUR-1 clinical trial of 154 patients with previously untreated disease. Patients received 500 milligrams of dostarlimab intravenously every three weeks for up to nine cycles, lasting roughly six months.

Targeted Oncology and the FDA reported that at a median follow-up of 26.3 months, 110 of the 154 patients (71%) achieved remission on dostarlimab alone. These patients required no surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy, and experienced no disease progression.

Under independent central review, the trial met its primary endpoint with a 12-month sustained clinical complete response rate of 47% and an overall response rate of 79%, as reported by OncLive and the FDA. Under investigator review, the 12-month sustained response rate was 68% and the overall response rate was 82%.

FDA records show that the most frequent adverse reactions in the trial were fatigue at 32%, rash at 22%, and diarrhea at 18%. Serious adverse events occurred in 20% of participants, and 3% discontinued therapy due to toxicity.

Standard treatments for rectal cancer frequently result in permanent sexual, urinary, and bowel dysfunction, along with a roughly 30% rate of permanent colostomy. Fierce Pharma reported that Dr. Andrea Cercek, section head of colorectal cancer at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center and principal investigator of AZUR-1, noted that dostarlimab preserved organ function in most trial participants and allowed five babies to be born to patients whose fertility would have otherwise been eliminated by pelvic radiation.

Dr. Cercek stated in remarks cited by GSK and MedPage Today that the trial demonstrates a fundamentally different approach tailored to the biology of the disease. Tony Wood, chief scientific officer at GSK, noted to OncLive that rectal cancer treatment has historically depended on invasive surgeries and radiation, describing the decision as a step toward more personalized care. Angelo de Claro, MD, director of the FDA’s Oncology Center of Excellence, stated to MedPage Today and Pharmacy Times that the approval provides an option that avoids major life-changing interventions. Michael Sapienza, CEO of the Colorectal Cancer Alliance, told MedPage Today that avoiding traditional interventions represents a meaningful advance for patients worrying about life after treatment.

The American Journal of Managed Care and OncLive noted that dostarlimab previously gained attention after an earlier 50-patient study at Memorial Sloan Kettering achieved a 100% complete response rate without surgery, radiation, or chemotherapy. BioSpace reported that the therapy received Breakthrough Therapy and Fast Track designations, clearing review ahead of its February 2027 target date after GSK used a National Priority Voucher.

According to Oncology Nursing News and the FDA, patients who achieve complete response enter a non-operative monitoring program that includes endoscopy, rectal MRI, and CT scans every four months for two years, and every six months thereafter. BioSpace and The American Journal of Managed Care reported that GSK will present full AZUR-1 interim data at the ESMO Congress in Madrid on Oct. 25, 2026, while the FDA requires continued follow-up to evaluate long-term durability.
