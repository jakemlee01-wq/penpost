On Wednesday, October 14, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration expanded the approved use of the immunotherapy drug Anktiva in combination with Bacillus Calmette-Guérin, or BCG. The clearance allows the combination to be used in adult patients with high-grade non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer that has not responded to BCG and features papillary-only tumors, according to announcements from the FDA and The ASCO Post.

The therapy is known generically as nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmln. It is licensed to and manufactured by Altor BioScience, LLC, an indirect subsidiary of Culver City, California-based ImmunityBio, Inc., Oncodaily reported. The agency noted that Anktiva is the first medical treatment specifically designated and approved for this group of patients with BCG-unresponsive, high-grade papillary-only disease.

ImmunityBio reported that papillary disease accounts for roughly 85% of the overall non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer population. The company noted that adding this group broadens the eligible patient base across BCG-unresponsive cases.

The regulatory decision came nearly three months ahead of the agency's January 6, 2027 goal date under the Prescription Drug User Fee Act, following an application submitted in May 2026, according to Oncology Nursing News.

Anktiva was originally approved by the FDA on April 22, 2024, for adults with BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer that presented as carcinoma in situ, with or without papillary tumors, Dow Jones Newswires reported. In March 2026, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network added the combination of Anktiva and BCG as a Category 2A treatment option for papillary-only disease, Oncology Nursing News noted.

Rather than requiring a new, dedicated clinical trial for papillary-only cases, regulators evaluated evidence from an earlier study. That trial, Cohort A of the QUILT-3.032 study, followed 77 patients with carcinoma in situ. It showed a 62% complete response rate, with 58% of responders maintaining that response for at least 12 months and 40% maintaining it for at least 24 months, according to the FDA and OncLive.

The FDA determined that the biological similarities between carcinoma in situ and high-grade papillary tumors, both being aggressive urothelial cancers that carry a high risk of returning, justified extrapolating the earlier efficacy results. Dr. R. Angelo de Claro of the FDA stated that reviewing those shared characteristics allowed the agency to build on findings from carcinoma in situ patients to broaden options for those with high-grade papillary tumors, adding that the regulatory approach could help guide future drug reviews. The FDA stated it is also working to finalize draft guidance setting formal standards for applying carcinoma in situ data to papillary-only indications in the future.

Anktiva is an interleukin-15 receptor agonist immunotherapy, which means it works by activating natural killer cells and killer T cells to attack tumor tissue, Oncodaily reported. The medication is given alongside BCG directly into the bladder through a catheter, a procedure known as intravesical instillation, rather than through an intravenous line.

Dr. Patrick Soon-Shiong, founder and executive chairman of ImmunityBio, stated that the decision marks an important step toward using the patient's own immune system to achieve lasting, tumor-free outcomes.

The treatment also carries clear limits and warnings. According to Oncology Nursing News and CURE Today, prescribing information will continue to warn doctors and patients that delaying a cystectomy, the surgical removal of the bladder, can allow bladder cancer to spread and become fatal if intravesical drug therapy fails to work.
