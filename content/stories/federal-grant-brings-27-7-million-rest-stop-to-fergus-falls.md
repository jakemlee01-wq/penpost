The Minnesota Department of Transportation will receive $27,741,777 in federal funding to build a new highway rest facility near Fergus Falls, according to reporting from KFGO.

The money comes through the federal Nationally Significant Multimodal Freight and Highway Projects program, known as INFRA, which awards grants to large-scale freight and highway work. According to Land Line Media, the award is part of a broader $200 million federal package spanning 12 states and 14 separate projects that will create more than 2,400 truck parking stalls across the country.

The Fergus Falls project grant will cover final design engineering, site acquisition, and construction along Interstate 94 in Minnesota's 7th Congressional District. Design documents designate the build as a Class I Safety Rest Area, which is MnDOT's largest classification offering the highest tier of traveler amenities. State plans call for the facility to serve drivers traveling in both directions along Interstate 94.

CDLLife reported that the layout will include roughly 63 dedicated parking stalls for commercial motor vehicles, as well as 60 spaces for passenger cars.

The new facility will replace the former eastbound Lake Iverson Rest Area near mile marker 60. MnDOT permanently closed that site in 2010 and tore it down in 2011. Fergus Now reported that repeated localized flooding and persistent high groundwater tables had compromised the drinking water wells at the Lake Iverson location.

MnDOT rest area guidelines state that facilities should be spaced between 50 and 60 miles apart. The removal of the Lake Iverson stop left a service gap on the interstate east of Moorhead that the new project is designed to resolve.

Federal data highlighted by KFGO indicates that roughly 40 percent of commercial truck drivers spend more than an hour each day hunting for safe, legal parking, causing lost work time and elevating safety risks. U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced the grant package to expand public parking along primary freight routes and lower the danger of fatigue-related accidents. Duffy noted that commercial drivers work to stock store shelves, fuel gas stations, and deliver packages, and should not have to spend over an hour searching for safe rest at the end of their shifts.
