A plastic security badge at a White House gate determines who walks into the briefing room to ask questions each morning. On Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, U.S. District Judge Timothy J. Kelly issued a preliminary injunction requiring the White House to let journalists from CNN, MS Now, and Politico keep those credentials, as reported by The Guardian, Reuters, and CBS News.

The ruling prevents federal officials from carrying out a Sept. 18, 2026, decision to revoke the permanent hard passes held by reporters from the three organizations, The Guardian reported. News4JAX reported that the revocation stripped passes from a total of 78 journalists across the three newsrooms.

President Donald Trump ordered the ban after publicly accusing the three outlets of reporting "fake news," according to The Guardian. The Guardian and Courthouse News reported that Trump announced the restriction on social media, writing that the organizations were barred for publishing fiction and lies. The next morning, Secret Service officers turned the reporters away and confiscated their credentials, with the administration sending formal notices of administrative appeal several days later on Sept. 22, according to reporting from CBS News and Forbes.

The three media companies filed a joint civil lawsuit on Sept. 21 at the E. Barrett Prettyman Federal Courthouse in Washington, The Guardian reported. They argued that taking the passes violated their First Amendment press protections and Fifth Amendment due process rights. Judge Kelly, who was appointed to the federal bench by Trump in 2017, granted a 14-day temporary restraining order in the predawn hours of Sept. 24, CBS News and The Washington Post reported.

That temporary order had been set to expire on Tuesday. The new 26-page decision replaced it, ordering that the administration and its agents shall not enforce the credential revocation while the case moves forward, maintaining the status quo, according to The Guardian, Anadolu Agency, and CBS News.

During court arguments, Judge Kelly stated that the administration had not justified excluding the press corps while legal challenges remain pending, The Guardian reported. CBS News and The Washington Post reported that Kelly found the news outlets are likely to succeed on their constitutional claims. The judge wrote that the revocation is the kind of action that would deter a reasonable reporter or newsroom from publishing stories to which the president objects.

Reuters reported that Kelly rejected the administration's late claim that the journalists presented a national security threat. Relying on Trump's public statements, Kelly concluded the ban was motivated by coverage the president disliked, calling it unlawful viewpoint discrimination.

Kelly also noted boundaries in his order. The Washington Post reported that the judge cautioned that his ruling does not mean Trump's concerns about media fairness are unfounded, or that the government cannot act to protect national security when classified information is unlawfully disclosed. The order binds federal officials and agents, but explicitly exempts the president personally, The Guardian reported.

Theodore Boutrous Jr., lead attorney for the media companies, stated that the president blatantly violated due process and the First Amendment, adding that the court was right to block the ban, CBS News and News4JAX reported.

The preliminary injunction protects the credentials indefinitely as the lawsuit heads toward a trial expected to take several months, according to The Guardian. Reuters, CBS News, and News4JAX reported that Kelly denied an administration request to put his decision on hold during an appeal, while Trump previously posted on Truth Social that the Justice Department would appeal to the D.C. Circuit. Even with their hard passes restored, The Washington Post reported that reporters from CNN and Politico remained barred from boarding Air Force One for pooled travel duties.
