Fair Isaac Corp. will hold wholesale pricing flat at $10 for credit scores through 2027 for lenders and brokers enrolled in its Preferred Program, Scotsman Guide reported.

The wholesale price freeze covers a bundled pull of both the Classic FICO Score and the newer trended-data FICO Score 10T model. The rate is available exclusively through participating tri-merge credit report reseller partners. To secure the $10 price, mortgage lenders and brokers must commit to FICO through those partners and agree to pull FICO scores on all relevant mortgage applications, according to HousingWire.

FICO has not announced updated pricing for its direct licensing scores, Scotsman Guide reported. FICO Chief Executive Officer Will Lansing stated that holding the rate at $10 ensures lenders have access to the predictive tools they need to assess risk with confidence, adding that the firm has helped lenders make risk decisions for nearly three decades.

The price cap follows sustained pressure from federal housing regulators and mortgage industry stakeholders after consecutive rounds of score fee increases in recent years. Mortgage lender and broker groups have argued that escalating credit check fees add friction to closing costs, which lenders frequently pass along to prospective homebuyers, Scotsman Guide reported.

The freeze gives originators cost certainty as margins stay compressed under 30-year fixed mortgage rates hovering between 7.24% and 7.32%, according to Forbes Advisor and Scotsman Guide. The decision also aligns with broader federal initiatives aimed at trimming third-party transaction expenses, automated valuation costs, and mortgage insurance fees across the housing finance system.

Market competition has also mounted. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac approved VantageScore 4.0, which has been offered to lenders for as low as $0.99 per score compared to FICO's $10 rate, HousingWire reported. Earlier in October 2026, TransUnion introduced its First Look Functionality and Equifax launched Mortgage Score Select, giving lenders tools to separate credit report inquiries from score purchases.

Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte responded on X that abusive monopolies will not be allowed to take advantage of consumers, adding that the agency is taking action to ensure competition is real, according to HousingWire.

Investors reacted swiftly. FICO shares tumbled between 9% and 16% on October 26, 2026, trading around $736 to $762, leaving the stock down roughly 50% year-to-date, according to TIKR. Wells Fargo analyst Jason Haas downgraded FICO from Overweight to Equal Weight and cut his price target from $800 to $735, reporting that locking in flat pricing eliminates anticipated price increases for 2027.

Other brokerages followed. JPMorgan lowered its price target from $1,530 to $900, UBS lowered its target from $1,100 to $942, and BMO Capital trimmed its target to $897 while reducing its fiscal 2027 earnings estimate to $44.87 per share, according to TIKR. Analysts tracked by TIKR project FICO's revenue growth will slow from roughly 27% in fiscal 2026 to about 8% annually through fiscal 2028. The company is scheduled to report its fiscal fourth-quarter results on November 4, 2026.
