Flapping Airplanes has secured $1 billion in a Series A financing round that values the artificial intelligence research lab at $6 billion, according to reporting from The Daily Star, Bloomberg, and TechCrunch. The transaction lifts the startup's total funding to $1.18 billion. The financing round was co-led by venture capital firms Index Ventures and Lux Capital, with Index partner Mark Xu taking a seat on the startup's board of directors.

The new valuation marks a fourfold increase over roughly nine months, reported The Wall Street Journal and The Next Web. The company emerged publicly from stealth in January 2026 following an initial $180 million seed round that valued the business at $1.5 billion, according to reporting from Crunchbase News and The Wall Street Journal.

A broad group of venture firms and individual financiers joined the Series A round. Reporting from TechCrunch, Traded, and FinSMEs identified institutional backers including Nvidia, Sequoia Capital, Alphabet’s GV, Kleiner Perkins, Valor Capital Group, Atreides Management, Bain Capital Ventures, CRV, and A*. Individual investors who participated in the round include hedge fund billionaire Steven A. Cohen, KKR co-founder Henry Kravis, and Ribbit Capital founder Micky Malka, according to Traded and FinSMEs.

The startup was established in late 2025 by chief executive Ben Spector, his brother Asher Spector, and Aidan Smith, according to MIT News, Bloomberg, and The Daily Star. Before founding the company, Smith worked as an engineer at Elon Musk’s Neuralink, while Asher Spector studied statistics at Stanford University. Ben Spector earned degrees from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2022 before studying computer science at Stanford. Wowtale and Index Ventures reported that Ben Spector previously conducted research in Stanford’s Hazy Research lab under professor Chris Ré, co-authoring GPU kernel optimization projects that included the ThunderKittens framework and the "Look Ma, No Bubbles!" megakernel architecture.

Flapping Airplanes is developing what it describes as a megakernel framework, which TechCrunch reported as custom software that bundles AI model training, inference, and interactive workloads inside a single graphics processing unit program. By running workloads together rather than executing separate sequential steps, the software avoids idle gaps between computational operations. TechCrunch also reported that the startup's software overlaps memory-bound inference tasks with compute-bound training tasks on a single graphics chip so hardware rarely sits idle. Co-founder Aidan Smith stated that the company created its own specialized operating code to drive Nvidia graphics chips more flexibly rather than relying strictly on standard CUDA operating layers, according to Bloomberg and FinSMEs.

Co-founder Asher Spector told Bloomberg that the company's thesis rests on demand for artificial intelligence outrunning the supply of chips and data. He noted that even a 10% performance gain per chip would be economically valuable, adding that the most successful business will be the one that squeezes the most out of its inputs. He also told TechCrunch that a model that is significantly more data efficient is easier to put into the economy. On the social platform X, the company stated that its goal is to open new markets such as robotics and scientific discovery by developing efficient models that do not require ingesting half the internet, as cited by The Daily Star. Lux Capital partner Brandon Reeves told Briefs Finance that getting more out of existing computing hardware is critical for the next wave of artificial intelligence.

Flapping Airplanes remains pre-product and pre-revenue with a staff of a couple dozen people, according to reporting from Dealroom, FinSMEs, and Bloomberg. The company plans to use the new capital to accelerate research pipelines, recruit deep-tech engineers, test larger infrastructure setups, and gradually commercialize its software stack.
