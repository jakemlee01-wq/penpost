Naturally Minnesota launched the ninth annual Food. Ag. Ideas conference on Monday, Oct. 26, 2026. The gathering brings together farmers, food startup founders, agtech investors, and consumer packaged goods executives, according to Naturally Minnesota.

This year's event centers on the theme "The Minnesota Model: Building a Living Economy from the Ground Up." Naturally Minnesota framed the theme around how the state's century of agricultural cooperatives, soil stewardship, and food infrastructure could provide a template for national supply chain resilience.

Programming opened Monday afternoon with a behind-the-scenes tour of the Minnesota State Fairgrounds running from 12:45 p.m. to 3:00 p.m. Session leaders Chuck Tryon, Jill Nathe, Mary Chung, and Michelle Butler guided attendees through the grounds to examine one of the state's central agricultural showcase institutions, according to event details posted on Glue Up.

The main conference convenes Tuesday, Oct. 27, at Machine Shop at 300 2nd St. SE in Minneapolis, running from 7:30 a.m. to 5:25 p.m., according to reporting from Davis Wright Tremaine Food Venture Financing News and Naturally Minnesota. Standard general admission registration was priced at $249, with a $149 rate for Naturally Minnesota members and separate admission for the opening fairgrounds tour, according to ConferenceFindr.

Allison Hohn, executive director of Naturally Minnesota, oversaw the program's development, while food and agriculture entrepreneurship consultant Maureen Ballatori serves as the summit emcee, according to the Agricultural Utilization Research Institute. Participating organizations include the University of Minnesota's Forever Green Initiative, Bay State Milling, and local agricultural innovators.

Tuesday's schedule covers shifting consumer habits and market pressures, with sessions titled "Eating Reimagined: What the GLP-1 Era Means for Food & Ag," "Food as Health: From Category Trend to System Infrastructure," and "Nourishing the Next Economy," along with discussions on supply chain and trade volatility. The daytime program concludes with a keynote panel titled "The Playbook: Can the Minnesota Model Travel?"

The event also highlights emerging food businesses, including Pitch Slam winner Farmented, which produces fermented foods using cosmetically imperfect produce bought from local farmers. The conference concludes Tuesday night with a networking reception from 5:30 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. at the Mill City Museum in downtown Minneapolis, co-hosted with Reuters Events: Transform Food & Agriculture USA and sponsored by Bay State Milling.
