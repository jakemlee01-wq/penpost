Total U.S. retail and food services sales rose 0.8% in September 2026 to reach $745.0 billion, according to advance Census Bureau estimates reported by Seeking Alpha. The Associated Press reported that the increase topped the 0.3% gain expected by economists surveyed by FactSet. Spending from the prior month was also stronger than first thought, with the Commerce Department revising August retail growth upward from 1.2% to 1.3%, according to Reuters.

Compared to September 2025, sales climbed 6.3%, Census Bureau data showed. For the full third quarter running from July through September, retail sales finished 5.5% higher than the same period a year earlier.

A closer look at the cash registers shows that fuel costs carried most of the weight. ActionForex reported that gasoline stations accounted for roughly 71% of September's total dollar growth, providing $4.07 billion of the overall $5.70 billion increase. Trading Economics reported that station receipts climbed 6.4% during the month. According to Bloomberg Economics and ActionForex, gas station receipts jumped 28.1% compared to September 2025, marking the sharpest monthly jump in six months after gasoline and diesel prices rose roughly 10% in September.

Piedmont Crescent Capital reported that the average price for regular unleaded gas rose 7.3% between August and September, moving from $4.06 per gallon to $4.35 per gallon. Newsday reported that Michael Pearce, chief U.S. economist at Oxford Economics, stated the headline increase in retail sales was almost entirely due to those higher gas prices. When gasoline stations are removed, total retail sales grew by a more modest 0.2%, according to the Associated Press.

Auto dealerships and parts stores recorded sales growth of 0.2%, Seeking Alpha reported. Core retail sales, a measure that excludes car dealerships, climbed 0.9%, which Piedmont Crescent Capital noted beat market expectations of 0.5%. Stripping out both cars and gasoline left overall sales growth at 0.2%, according to ActionForex.

The core control group paints an even quieter picture. That metric, which removes gas stations, car dealers, food services, and building material suppliers to calculate goods consumption for gross domestic product, rose by just 0.1%. Trading Economics noted that followed a 1.2% increase in August. Reuters and Bloomberg Economics reported the 0.1% figure fell short of the 0.3% gain economists had projected.

Shoppers showed selective interest across merchant aisles, with sales rising in eight of 13 Census categories, according to the Associated Press. Department stores led retail categories with a 1.6% rise, while furniture and home furnishings stores rose 1.3%. Spending at restaurants and drinking establishments increased 1.1%. Electronics and appliance stores picked up 0.6%, and online and non-store retailers rose 0.3%. Sales at clothing stores were unchanged.

Broader indicators also suggest consumers are turning cautious. Reuters reported that the Federal Reserve's Beige Book found consumer spending showed little to no change across most districts into early October. Data from the Bank of America Institute, also cited by Reuters, showed nearly one in five households using buy now, pay later installment plans carried monthly repayments exceeding $500.

Economists estimate annualized consumer spending grew at a 3.3% to 3.4% pace during the third quarter, down from 3.8% in the second quarter, according to Oxford Economics, Piedmont Crescent Capital, and Reuters. Those economists warned that the low growth in core control-group goods demand signals slower momentum heading into the fourth-quarter holiday shopping period.
