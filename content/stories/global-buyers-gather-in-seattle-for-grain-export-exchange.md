Export Exchange 2026 concluded on Saturday, October 17, at the Hyatt Regency Seattle after three days of commercial meetings. Organizers reported that the biennial event drew more than 500 agricultural leaders, commercial agribusinesses, and international grain buyers representing 48 countries. American Ag Network reported that the gathering marked a record-setting turnout.

The conference was organized and co-sponsored by the U.S. Grains & BioProducts Council, Growth Energy, and the Renewable Fuels Association. It served as a central trading and networking forum connecting international buyers with domestic sellers of corn, sorghum, barley, and distiller's dried grains with solubles, the nutrient-dense animal feed co-product left over from ethanol distillation. More than 20 industry exhibitors set up trade show booths across the venue.

In opening remarks, Ryan LeGrand emphasized that attendees could build direct business relationships with American agricultural leaders to cultivate future trade. Northwest Seaport Alliance Commissioner Ryan Calkins delivered welcoming remarks to the Pacific Northwest, and Chris Riker, deputy administrator for trade policy and geographical affairs at the USDA Foreign Agricultural Service, presented an overview of U.S. farm goods performance in overseas markets.

Commercial agreements accompanied the trade sessions. Jay Reiners, chairman of the U.S. Grains & BioProducts Council, signed a memorandum of understanding with BioPower Energy. BioPower Energy plans to construct and operate an integrated biorefinery in the United Arab Emirates, with plans to procure between 50% and 100% of its feedstock from U.S. suppliers.

Presentations during the event focused on grain efficiency and processing co-products. Mark Sevier, manager of global strategies and trade for the council, moderated an industry panel examining feed performance and cost efficiency for international millers. Joe Ward, executive director of the Distillers Technology Council, delivered a presentation analyzing emerging feed innovations and enhanced co-products entering global trade channels. Collin Bradley, an international trade specialist with the U.S. Department of Agriculture, briefed participants on federal programs designed to expand overseas markets for U.S. agricultural commodities.

The gathering also extended into field visits across the country. Leading up to the Seattle meeting, 13 international trade teams toured farms and logistics facilities. Those visits included Thai millers inspecting Tri-Fecta Farms in Wisconsin, Middle Eastern and African buyers visiting Mississippi River barge elevators, and a Vietnamese delegation touring an ADM grain elevator in Tennessee.

Following the general sessions, seven specialized delegations departed for post-conference tours scheduled from October 18 through October 21. According to American Ag Network, those groups are inspecting farm operations, ethanol processing plants, and shipping ports. A delegation from Bangladesh is visiting agricultural operations in North Dakota, while a trade group from China is traveling to export and processing sites in Texas.

At the close of the event, LeGrand told attendees he hoped they left convinced of the quality of U.S. commodities, claiming the event shattered previous records. The conference is held every two years. Surveys collected after the previous 2024 conference in Fort Worth, Texas, tracked 968,030 metric tons of corn, sorghum, and co-products sold, generating $242 million in direct sales. That total was driven primarily by 472,230 metric tons of distiller's dried grains.
