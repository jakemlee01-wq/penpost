Google has formally confirmed it will forgo a property tax abatement for its planned data center in Hermantown, KAXE and FOX 21 reported. Following that announcement, a Hermantown city councilor proposed using the resulting tax revenues to provide property tax cuts to local homeowners, according to FOX 21.

A property tax abatement is an agreement where local governments temporarily reduce or forgive taxes to encourage new business investment. As reported by MPR News, Bring Me The News, and KAXE, Google had sought an 85 percent real property tax abatement capped at up to $80 million over 28 years under proposals debated since April. Local officials tabled those terms on May 4 after community pushback.

Without that tax break, city officials project the data center will produce more than $240 million in regional property tax revenues across the next 20 years. Hermantown Economic Development Director Chad Ronchetti told KTTC that the facility could generate up to that $240 million regional total. Reporting from KAXE and Northern News Now indicates that nearly $90 million of those collections would flow into Hermantown municipal coffers, while more than $120 million would go to St. Louis County.

Ronchetti told Northern News Now that collecting $120 million for the county over 20 years is significant compared to offering tax breaks. Hermantown city leadership projects Google will pay roughly $3 million in property taxes in its first year of operation, which exceeds double the city's entire current commercial and industrial property tax base combined. According to the City of Hermantown, the project site currently yields about $80,000 annually in taxes, meaning full development would increase the property's tax yields more than tenfold.

Hermantown Mayor Wayne Boucher told Northern News Now that he is glad Google continues to listen to community and regional needs. Google confirmed it will invest $100 million to $130 million to build required public utility and municipal infrastructure at the location, according to Northern News Now, WDIO, and KAXE. Google also announced plans to support Hermantown Community Schools through alternative community gifts and contributions rather than an arrangement linked to tax abatements.

The multi-phase data center campus is planned for roughly 200 acres near Midway Road and Morris Thomas Road, MPR News reported. The Hermantown City Council cleared an environmental review step on Oct. 5 by voting unanimously to adopt the project's Final Alternative Urban Areawide Review, according to FOX 21 and the city.

Opposition to the campus continues. The citizen group Stop the Hermantown Data Center previously campaigned against offering public incentives to a corporation, arguing relief belonged with local taxpayers, KAXE reported. Following Google's decision to drop the abatement request, the group told Northern News Now and KAXE that the change does not resolve their environmental, noise, and zoning concerns. The group stated that it remains opposed to what it called an unwanted, inappropriately located industrial project that usurped the public process.

The project now moves through municipal reviews. KAXE and Northern News Now reported that the data center's special use permit request goes before the Hermantown Planning and Zoning Commission on Tuesday, October 20, 2026. The Hermantown City Council is scheduled to formally consider the permit alongside development and land use agreements on Monday, October 26, 2026.
