Minnesota kicker Luke Drzewiecki drilled a 46-yard field goal as time expired Saturday, Oct. 24, giving the Golden Gophers a 13-10 upset victory over the 15th-ranked Iowa Hawkeyes, according to reports from the Associated Press and MPR News.

An announced crowd of 53,004 fans filled Huntington Bank Stadium, and spectators stormed the field to celebrate after the kick cleared the uprights, the Star Tribune reported. The win returned the 98-pound bronze pig trophy, Floyd of Rosedale, back to Minnesota for the first time since 2023 and for just the second time in the past 12 seasons.

The victory was head coach P.J. Fleck's second win over Iowa in 10 tries, according to the Star Tribune, and marked Minnesota's first home win against the Hawkeyes since 2014. With the outcome, Minnesota improved to 5-2 overall and 2-2 in Big Ten play, while Iowa moved to 5-2 overall and 2-2 in the conference, MPR News reported.

Drzewiecki joined the Gophers roster in August after briefly attending rookie minicamp with the Baltimore Ravens and suing the NCAA to secure an extra season of college eligibility, according to the Star Tribune. The newspaper also reported that Drzewiecki heard the prediction, "You’re gonna make a game-winner tomorrow," ahead of Saturday's matchup.

The final scoring drive spanned 44 yards across seven plays in the game's closing 1:46, MPR News and MinneapoliMedia News reported. The decisive sequence opened up with 39 seconds remaining, when starting quarterback Drake Lindsey found receiver Jalen Smith for a 39-yard pass on third down from Minnesota's 33-yard line, moving the ball into scoring range, according to the Associated Press. In comments reported by MPR News, the quarterback recalled telling the offense they would win, while telling Smith, "You're going to make a big play right here," adding that Smith "made a big-time play in a big-time moment."

Minnesota won despite being held to a season-low 209 yards of total offense, while Iowa finished with 288 total yards, according to MPR News and CBS News. Coach Fleck called the effort "maybe one of the best defensive performances I've been around at the University of Minnesota," telling reporters that his players dominated turnovers and tackled well.

Minnesota's defense forced four turnovers against an Iowa offense that had committed only three all season prior to the game, intercepting three passes and recovering a fumble, MPR News reported. The defense also limited Iowa's run game, which entered averaging 194 yards per game, to 90 yards, according to CBS News and MPR News. Big Ten leading rusher Kamari Moulton managed 22 yards before leaving in the second quarter with an undisclosed injury.

Drzewiecki opened the scoring in the first quarter with a 31-yard field goal, the Associated Press reported. Iowa kicker Caden Buhr tied the score with a 32-yard kick midway through the second quarter, sending the teams into halftime at 3-3, CBS News reported. Iowa had replaced starting quarterback Jeremy Hecklinski midway through the second quarter after he completed 9 of 16 passes for 62 yards and an interception.

Minnesota took a 10-3 lead with 8:08 remaining in the third quarter when running back Darius Taylor scored on a 1-yard run, which followed an Iowa offsides penalty that negated a fourth-down stop, according to MPR News and MinneapoliMedia News. Iowa tied the game at 10-10 with 3:46 left in the fourth quarter on a four-play, 98-yard drive that ended with a 37-yard touchdown pass from backup quarterback Hank Brown to Reece Vander Zee, CBS News reported, before Drzewiecki sealed the win.
