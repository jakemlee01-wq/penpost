Some companies find out about a viral rumor when their phones start ringing. Gudea wants them to find out first.

The Minneapolis startup, profiled this week by Twin Cities Business, uses an AI platform to watch social media for coordinated online campaigns, misinformation and made-up narratives. When something about a client starts circulating, Gudea flags it, along with who appears to be behind it and whether it looks likely to go viral.

CEO Keith Presley calls it "the storm tracker for the internet." It's a fair comparison. A forecast doesn't stop the storm, but it gives you time to bring the patio furniture in.

Gudea was founded in 2023 by Presley, COO Jonathan Sperber and James Thomin, and moved from Maryland to Minneapolis in 2025. Sperber told TCB that customers were part of the reason. Minnesota is home to a lot of Fortune 500 companies, and big companies are exactly who Gudea sells to.

The company says it closed a seed round of nearly $7 million this summer and has 23 full-time employees. A seed round is a startup's first sizable outside investment, a bet on what it could become. TCB didn't name the investors or any customers, so how much traction Gudea has is still an open question.

Still, it's a new name on the local tech map, and one that chose Minneapolis on purpose.
