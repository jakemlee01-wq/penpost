Alliance Bank has offered to donate its downtown commercial branch at 112 Broadway to the City of Hanska free of charge, ahead of closing the location permanently on December 4. The branch is currently the only commercial banking facility operating in the town.

If the city decides not to accept the donation, the bank plans to place the building on the commercial real estate market, according to reporting by The Journal.

To gauge community interest, the Hanska City Council hosted a public hearing in the basement of the Hanska Community Center at 201 Broadway. City Clerk Christine Fischer published official notices on Oct. 1 and Oct. 8 inviting residents to attend the Oct. 13 meeting or send comments by mail and email. During the hearing, City Council Member William Wurtz told attendees that public feedback would weigh heavily on the council's decision.

Municipal estimates project that operating the bank building would cost Hanska $10,133 per year for utilities and insurance, which equals about $844 each month. Wurtz described the site as a ready to use building that requires minimal immediate maintenance, though he noted that bathroom upgrades would be necessary to convert the space into a library. City officials anticipate using grant funding to cover those accessibility and code renovations.

Under the proposal discussed by council members, the city could offset ongoing operating costs by moving the Hanska Public Library across the street into the larger bank building. The city could then sell the current library property at 109 Broadway, which carries an estimated market valuation of $51,000.

Council members pointed to the previous sale of Hanska's municipal liquor store as a precedent for the move. Proceeds from that liquidation were deposited into the city's general fund and later paid for a town tractor, maintenance in city parks, and improvements to the community center.

Hanska Library Director Angela Pick spoke in favor of the relocation during the meeting, The Journal reported. Pick explained that the current library consists of a single room, which limits community programming. She noted that the library serves as a gathering spot for rural families whose children attend different school districts, calling it a valuable space for residents who grew up on area farms.

The city council is reviewing public feedback and financial estimates as it prepares to take a formal vote on accepting the deed transfer before the bank closes on December 4.
