On Monday night, October 26, 2026, left-wing Twitch commentator Hasan Piker joined Democratic U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar and Minnesota State Sen. Omar Fateh for a live political forum in Minneapolis. The event, titled "I'm the Host Now!" and held eight days before the November general election, took place at the 245-seat Theatre in the Round in the Cedar-Riverside neighborhood. The forum was streamed live across Piker's Twitch channel.

Sen. Fateh hosted the forum in partnership with local civic organization Baddies 4 Progress and communications agency HAUS of C. Sen. Fateh noted that tickets for the venue sold out within five minutes of release. MPR News reported that while the outlet was initially invited to cover the gathering, Sen. Fateh barred media access at the door, citing space limitations inside the theater.

Rep. Omar spent more than 20 minutes on stage with Piker and Fateh before departing, according to reporting from CBS News Minnesota and The Minnesota Star Tribune. The discussion addressed Omar's political beginnings and the national attention often directed at Minnesota politics. Piker claimed that national fixation on the state is driven by jealousy over progressive local policies, adding that Minneapolis politics has moved in a positive direction year over year.

Piker also addressed recent federal immigration enforcement efforts, stating that Minneapolis residents demonstrated solidarity during Operation Metro Surge. Rep. Omar highlighted community resistance to federal enforcement, noting that more than 300 organizations gathered in her office in November 2024 to coordinate responses. Urging the audience to participate in early voting and turn out on Election Day, Rep. Omar told attendees that Minnesota does not come after its refugees, but sends one to Congress.

Following Omar's appearance, Fateh and Piker held an onstage discussion for roughly 50 minutes. The Minnesota Star Tribune reported that the conversation remained focused on progressive movement-building, economic inequality, media coverage, and criticism of the Trump administration. An attendee, Erik Tanner, stated that Democrats need to move further left, adding that officials currently in charge do not seem to represent younger voters.

The gathering drew sharp pushback from prominent Minnesota DFL leaders, as reported by CBS News Minnesota and MPR News. U.S. Rep. Angie Craig declared Piker's rhetoric polarizing and dangerous, stating that embracing him as a party is a mistake. U.S. Sen. Amy Klobuchar warned that a number of Piker's past statements are reckless. Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey and state Sen. Ron Latz publicly chided the event, while a spokesperson for Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan stated that Flanagan declined to attend and condemns hateful rhetoric.

The Jewish Community Relations Council of Minnesota and the Dakotas, along with four Minnesota Republican U.S. representatives, also issued statements criticizing the decision to host Piker. Sen. Fateh defended the event, describing Piker and Omar on social media as fearless champions fighting for the community. Omar's office defended partnering with prominent influencers as an effort to boost lagging early voter turnout among constituents under age 40.
