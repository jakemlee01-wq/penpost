The Hermantown City Council postponed a scheduled vote Monday night on a revised development agreement and permits for a planned Google data center, according to reporting from the Minnesota Star Tribune and Northern News Now. Demonstrators crowded into the Hermantown Governmental Services Building, delaying the meeting for nearly two hours before officials called it off.

Protesters held a sit-in across corridors and the entryway, chanting "shut it down" and "let us speak." Demonstrator Sarah Lofald told the Star Tribune that the gathering grew after city staff closed the public comment sign-up sheet while dozens of residents were still waiting in line, describing the gathering as a peaceful sit-in where no one was harmed. St. Louis County Commissioner Ashley Grimm and Duluth City Councilor David Clanaugh were among those taking part in the demonstration.

City Administrator Jim Crace stated that the building was over capacity by at least 200 people, creating a fire hazard. Police declared an unlawful assembly and gave demonstrators ten minutes to leave, but officers made no arrests Monday night. Crace requested law enforcement reinforcements from the Duluth Police Department and the St. Louis County Sheriff's Office, adding that the council intends to reconvene Tuesday evening with an enhanced safety plan.

City official Joe Wicklund told the Star Tribune that leaders called off the meeting because they could not conduct it safely, describing the facility as a polarizing issue during midterm elections. Jack Carlson and Kim Parmeter, representing the Hermantown Chamber of Commerce and the Duluth Building and Construction Trades Council, issued a joint statement condemning the disruption. They stated that their members had engaged in a yearlong public process that gathered over 200 written comments, arguing that the demonstrators aimed to halt the proceedings.

The proposed 1.66-million-square-foot facility is planned across 234 acres near an electrical substation in a rural part of Hermantown, located near wetlands and cold-water trout streams. Following community feedback in mid-October, Google adjusted its development plan, as reported by the Star Tribune, MPR News, and the City of Hermantown. The company withdrew a request for up to $80 million in property tax abatements, scaled down the site footprint, set an operational noise limit of 47 decibels, added private well monitoring, and pledged a decommissioning agreement to fund cleanup if the site ever closes.

The postponed vote came one day before a scheduled court hearing regarding citizen allegations that Hermantown violated public notice requirements, Northern News Now and the Star Tribune reported. Even if local council approvals pass, Google must secure additional wetland permits and state approval for its Minnesota Power electric service agreement before construction could start in 2027.
