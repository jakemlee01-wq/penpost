Northwoods Materials says it has closed financing for a plant near Hibbing that would turn iron and other minerals into materials used in batteries. The company puts the total at $410 million, from a mix of private investors and bank loans. (Demo story; company and figures are examples.)

"Financing closed" means the company has commitments for the money it needs to start building. That's a real milestone. Plenty of industrial projects get announced and never reach this point.

Here's what the announcement doesn't settle. The plant still needs final permits for water use. Construction is planned to take about two years. And the process the company plans to use has run at pilot scale, not at full size. Scaling up a factory process is where schedules tend to slip.

If it works, the company says the plant will employ about 180 people. On the Iron Range, where mining jobs have risen and fallen with steel prices, a different kind of manufacturing employer would be a meaningful addition.

The practical things to watch: the permit decision, the construction start date, and whether the first production run lands close to plan.

Good news, with homework still due.
