Drivers in Marshall can once again travel straight through East College Drive without detour signs. As reported by the Marshall Independent, Minnesota Highway 19 officially reopened to normal through traffic on the morning of Monday, October 19, 2026.

The reopening marks the conclusion of a two-year, $25.7 million reconstruction project covering 1.8 miles from Marlene Street through the Bruce Street intersection. The effort was carried out as a partnership between the Minnesota Department of Transportation and the City of Marshall, financed in part by a $15.4 million federal Rebuilding American Infrastructure with Sustainability and Equity (RAISE) grant.

Roadwork on the corridor first began in May 2025. That first phase wrapped up in November 2025 after reconstructing Highway 19 from Fairgrounds Road to Main Street and adding a single-lane roundabout at Country Club Drive and South 2nd Street. The second phase launched in spring 2026, closing College Drive between Main Street and Bruce Street through October to pour a durable concrete highway surface.

Below the new concrete, the city and primary contractor R and G Construction tackled aging infrastructure hidden beneath the pavement. Marshall replaced buried sanitary sewer lines, water mains, and storm sewers. Marshall Public Works Director Jason Anderson reported that the work eliminated a longstanding utility bottleneck near Bruce Street and College Drive, where sanitary lines had begun failing and water pipes had a history of recurring breaks.

Erika Coudron of R and G Construction publicly thanked local residents, business owners, and regional drivers for their patience throughout the extended project. Marshall Mayor Bob Byrnes described the undertaking to the Marshall Independent as a "massive project," adding that an effort of this scale is "probably once in a generation."

The corridor now includes several permanent layout changes for drivers and pedestrians. Workers permanently removed the traffic light at the intersection of Lyon Street and College Drive. At West Marshall Street, crews installed a concrete median island to restrict turns to a right-in, right-out layout.

Pedestrian upgrades include Americans with Disabilities Act-compliant sidewalk ramps and rectangular rapid-flashing beacons at crosswalks between High Street and Whitney Street. The project also placed a new electric vehicle charging station near Bruce Street and College Drive in the Avera parking lot.

MnDOT cautioned that drivers should slow down and travel with care over the coming days, as work crews remain on-site to finish punch-list tasks, pavement markings, and site stabilization. A separate roadside project contracted to Hoffman and McNamara Co. is scheduled to continue through November 2026, planting new boulevard trees along College Drive between Channel Parkway and Bruce Street.
