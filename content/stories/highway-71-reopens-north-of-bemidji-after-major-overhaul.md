Summer detours north of Bemidji have officially given way to fresh pavement. The Minnesota Department of Transportation announced on Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026, that major construction along Highway 71 between Turtle River and Tenstrike has concluded.

Knife River Materials served as the prime contractor on the $8.8 million project. Groundbreaking began on June 1, 2026, following a May 19 open house at the Turtle River Town Hall on South Arm Court in Bemidji, where MnDOT reviewed timeline expectations and detour routes with area residents.

For four months, through traffic could not use the corridor. Lakeland PBS reported that the highway closed between Beltrami County Roads 23 and 29 from June into early October, routing drivers onto detours along those two county roads.

MnDOT District 2 launched the project to tackle aging infrastructure along the stretch. Crews executed a full pavement reconstruction, replaced entrance culverts, and added a southbound bypass lane at Turtle River Lake Road to improve drainage, operational efficiency, and surface longevity.

Highway 71 reopened to through traffic on Saturday, Oct. 3, 2026. That same day, crews closed Beltrami County Road 23 at the Highway 71 junction to rebuild and widen the intersection.

Workers placed the final lift of asphalt under live traffic beginning Monday, Oct. 5, 2026. Flaggers and pilot cars guided motorists through the work zone while crews completed the west section of the County Road 23 junction.

MnDOT issued a statement thanking local residents, businesses, and travelers for their patience and safety focus throughout the construction window.

Main travel lanes and intersections are open, but the work zone is not entirely deserted. MnDOT and Lakeland PBS reported that crews remain on-site finishing permanent signage and road striping, with transportation officials advising drivers to slow down and give the remaining workers space.
