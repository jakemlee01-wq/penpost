Walkers and bicyclists will soon be able to cross the Blue Earth River on an iron span that first carried horse-drawn wagons 153 years ago. Mankato city officials announced that the relocated Kern Bridge will formally open to the public on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, at 1:00 p.m. with a community celebration, according to KEYC News Now.

The 189-foot-long crossing links Sibley Park on one bank with Land of Memories Park on the other. Converted from vehicle traffic to a pedestrian and bicycle route, it serves as a central link in the Minnesota River Trail network. The City of Mankato noted that the regional trail corridor will eventually provide an uninterrupted path connecting Minneopa State Park to the City of Faribault.

The bridge is the only surviving bowstring through-truss bridge in the state, according to the Minnesota Department of Transportation. Builder John Mahowald and the Ohio-based Wrought Iron Bridge Company erected the structure in 1873, just 15 years after Minnesota achieved statehood, MPR News reported. It originally crossed the Le Sueur River in South Bend Township south of Mankato, becoming Minnesota's oldest standing roadway truss bridge before being placed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980.

State and county officials closed the bridge to motor vehicles around 1990. MnDOT and Blue Earth County then dismantled the span in February 2020 to protect it from failing river abutments and kept it in storage. In August 2026, contractors from Redstone Construction used heavy cranes to hoist the restored wrought-iron frame onto new concrete piers over the Blue Earth River. Mankato City Engineer Cory Bienfang told MPR News that preserving, reconditioning, and adaptively reusing the landmark was a unique civil engineering effort.

The relocation and restoration project carried a total cost of roughly $10.5 million. Following an October 12 city council meeting, Mankato City Manager Susan Arntz reported that approximately 75% to 80% of the funding came from state and external grants rather than local municipal funds. City leadership told KEYC that outside funding allowed the city to complete the project and connect the two parks.

Work at the site has entered its final phases. In an October 8 update, the City of Mankato reported that WEB Construction and municipal crews placed base-course asphalt along Sibley Parkway and the Land of Memories service road, paved trail approaches on both banks, graded black dirt at Sibley Park, and began installing safety railings. Contractors are completing safety rails, bituminous surfacing, and turf restoration ahead of the ribbon cutting, with final landscaping touches scheduled to continue immediately after the opening.
