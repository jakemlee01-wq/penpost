Single-family home construction picked up in September, even as apartment building slowed down. Privately owned housing starts reached a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1,354,000 units, according to the U.S. Census Bureau and the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). That total fell 0.5% from August's revised estimate of 1,361,000 units and sat 0.7% below the September 2023 pace of 1,363,000, as reported by CalculatedRisk and the Census Bureau.

Single-family construction expanded 2.7% month over month to an annualized rate of 1,027,000 units, up from a revised 1,000,000 in August, according to the Census Bureau, Atlanta Agent Magazine, and Zillow Research. Compared to September 2023, single-family starts rose 5.5% from 973,000 units. On a year-to-date basis, single-family home construction is up 10.1%, based on figures from the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB).

Larger apartment construction moved in the opposite direction. Multifamily housing starts for buildings with five or more units dropped 4.5% from August to an annualized pace of 317,000 units, according to the Census Bureau. That is 15.7% lower than the year-ago rate and represents the slowest pace for the sector since May, according to CalculatedRisk and NAHB.

At the same time, finished homes came onto the market at a significantly faster rate than last year. Total housing completions reached an annualized rate of 1,680,000 units in September, marking a 14.6% increase over the 1,466,000 units finished in September 2023, according to reports from Floor Daily and Mish Talk. That pace dipped 5.7% from August’s revised rate of 1,781,000 units. Single-family completions ran at an annual pace of 1,000,000 units, while multifamily completions in buildings with five units or more reached 671,000 units, based on Census Bureau and HUD data.

Permit activity, which signals future building, showed a slower pipeline. Privately owned building permits dropped 2.9% month over month and fell 5.7% from a year ago to an annualized rate of 1,428,000 units, according to the Census Bureau and CalculatedRisk. Authorizations for single-family homes ticked up 0.3% to a rate of 970,000 units, while multifamily permits stood at 398,000, Scotsman Guide reported. Building was led by a surge in the Northeast, while Indianapolis, Phoenix, San Diego, San Antonio, and Milwaukee posted the strongest relative single-family permitting gains, according to Realtor.com and Zillow Research.

Industry analysts pointed to borrowing costs and limited supply as central factors. Robert Dietz of the NAHB noted that single-family starts gained as the Federal Reserve began easing monetary policy and builder sentiment improved. Dietz added that higher mortgage rates in October are likely to dampen next month's numbers, though he forecasts a gradual decline in mortgage rates over coming quarters. NAHB chairman Carl Harris stated that rising single-family construction mirrored builder confidence surveys, adding that increasing supply is the most effective way to address housing affordability, as reported by Irrigation & Lighting.

Existing inventory remains constrained. Odeta Kushi observed in Atlanta Agent Magazine that the market remains structurally underbuilt, with homeowners holding locked-in low mortgage rates keeping existing inventory low. Lisa Sturtevant reported for Realtor.com that more new construction is essential to ease affordability pressures, which remain a major constraint for first-time buyers and lower-income renters.
