Marching band routines run on strict timing, but Lakeville South High School found its fall season threatened by a delivery that arrived in pieces, KARE 11 reported. Co-band director Chad Bieniek discovered that custom uniform orders faced severe production delays, leaving the entire ensemble without outfits for their competition.

According to KARE 11, the company hired to manufacture the uniforms ran into severe manufacturing bottlenecks, including an overwhelmed printer and insufficient staffing to assemble the gear. The timing was critical. Lakeville South's competitive field show was titled "Rise," and directors had designed the routine and music around custom pink and blue garments symbolizing the sky. Sophomore flautist Maggie Berglund told KARE 11 that custom uniforms represented a huge milestone, explaining that competing schools used custom outfits and that the look marked a major step for the program.

The scramble escalated on a Wednesday when a shipment of unassembled garment pieces arrived in a box left outside a garage, according to Lakeville South Bands. Visual and color guard director Melissa Nielsen realized the pieces would not be ready in time and called her mother for help, KARE 11 reported. Her mother, Merle Pratt, serves as chair of Quilting for Others, the charity branch of Minnesota Quilters Inc., the state's largest quilting guild. Pratt initially assumed she was volunteering to sew 20 to 30 color guard uniforms before finding out the project covered all 110 band members, KARE 11 reported.

Volunteers from Minnesota Quilters, band parents, and grandparents set up an assembly line in a church basement with roughly two days before competition. The fabric added friction to the process. Pratt noted that the uniforms were made of Lycra, a stretchy material that proved difficult to feed through standard sewing machines. To save time, the volunteers swapped complex zippers for Velcro strips, while workers who could not sew garments attached buttons, KARE 11 reported.

Working across 48 hours, the volunteers cut, pieced, and stitched all 110 uniforms from scratch by Friday night, KARE 11 reported. According to Lakeville South Bands, the volunteers also sorted the garments by size, fitted them to students, and completed final alterations. Students who had been warned they might have to perform in ordinary street clothes were able to arrive early Saturday and suit up, KARE 11 reported. The Edina Current and KARE 11 reported that every uniform was completely field-ready before the scheduled Saturday competition.

Bieniek recounted to KARE 11 that light exuded from the students' faces when they saw the finished uniforms. Lakeville South earned third place at the major regional marching band competition, KARE 11 reported. Band member Solane Abdissa told the station that the community rescue mirrored the show's theme of bringing light and rising to the occasion. The students wore the stitched uniforms one final time on Saturday during their end-of-season benefit concert, KARE 11 reported.
