Jeremy Blackwelder was officially sworn in as a Fifth Judicial District court judge on Friday, October 23, 2026, during an investiture ceremony at the Lyon County Government Center in Marshall. The Marshall Independent and Cedar News reported that District Court Judge Michelle Dietrich administered the oath of office.

Blackwelder's family took part in the ceremony, according to reporting from the Marshall Independent. His daughter, Hudsyn, held the Bible as he took the oath, and his son led the ceremony's prayer. His wife and his brother, Matthew, formally presented him with his judicial nameplate and judge's robe.

Governor Tim Walz announced Blackwelder's appointment on September 23, 2026. Blackwelder fills the seat left vacant after Judge Tricia B. Zimmer passed away in the summer of 2026, according to statements from the governor's office and the local paper.

Minnesota's Fifth Judicial District covers 15 counties across southwestern Minnesota. Blackwelder will maintain official chambers in both Marshall, located in Lyon County, and Ivanhoe, located in Lincoln County.

Blackwelder earned his law degree from the Hamline University School of Law. Earlier in his career, he worked in law enforcement as a police officer for the Upper Sioux Community. He later practiced as a partner at Holmstrom, Kvam, & Blackwelder, PLLP, and served as the city attorney for Granite Falls and Maynard.

In January 2026, Blackwelder moved to the Fifth Judicial District to work as an assistant public defender handling southwestern Minnesota criminal cases alongside Cecil Naatz. The Marshall Independent noted that Blackwelder wrapped up his public defender caseload in mid-October 2026, handing pending matters, including a felony drug case before Judge Dietrich, over to defense attorney Alexis Schroeder to begin his full-time judicial work.

Colleagues addressed Blackwelder's background during the ceremony. Naatz spoke about Blackwelder's perspective across law enforcement and legal defense, while noting that his primary role remains that of a husband and father. Retired Eighth Judicial District Judge Dwayne Knutsen commended Blackwelder for stepping forward to advance the work of the court.

Blackwelder said the appointment was an unexpected opportunity, the Marshall Independent reported. Pledging to listen, learn, and treat people with dignity, he told those gathered, "I'm incredibly proud and honored to be standing here today," and added, "I'm ready to get to work."
