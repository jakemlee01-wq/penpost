Taking charge of a grocery empire begins with knowing the kitchen from the bottom up. John Ghingo, who started in food service at age 14 washing dishes in a New York restaurant, officially took over as president and chief executive officer of Hormel Foods Corporation on Monday, October 26, 2026. The appointment was reported by Twin Cities Business and company announcements.

Ghingo steps into the corner office at Hormel's global headquarters in Austin, Minnesota. He succeeds Jeff Ettinger, who stepped in as interim CEO in July 2025 after longtime leader Jim Snee retired, Twin Cities Business reported. Ettinger, who previously served as Hormel's chief executive from 2006 to 2016 and later led the University of Minnesota as interim president, will remain on Hormel's board as an independent director to maintain continuity.

The handoff follows a steady climb inside the Fortune 500 company. Ghingo rejoined Hormel in September 2024 to guide its retail unit, as Food Business News reported, and rose to company president in July 2025. That promotion placed retail, foodservice, international operations, supply chains, IT, corporate strategy, and research and development under his oversight. He also joined the board of directors in July 2025, where he will continue to serve.

His resume spans more than 25 years in consumer packaged goods. Between 2018 and 2022, Ghingo served as president of Applegate Farms, an organic meat subsidiary owned by Hormel. His earlier career includes more than 15 years in marketing and management at Mondelēz International, leadership over plant-based foods and beverages at The WhiteWave Foods Company, and a tenure as chief executive of snack maker Whisps Acquisition Corporation, according to reporting by FoodBev Media and Investing.com. He holds a bachelor's degree from the University of Notre Dame and an MBA from New York University's Stern School of Business, Feedstuffs and The Star Tribune reported.

Alongside the top transition, Hormel named Joe O'Connor as group vice president of enterprise performance, effective October 26, according to PR Newswire.

Regulatory filings show Ghingo's annual base salary increased to $1.2 million upon taking the top job, with an annual short-term incentive target set at 150% of base pay. Filings cited by Stock Titan and TipRanks also outline a $6.8 million long-term incentive award made up of cash, stock options, and restricted stock units.

Hormel board chairman Bill Newlands credited Ghingo with setting clear direction and executing strategy at an enterprise scale, stating that his background will help accelerate modernization and sustainable growth. In a statement carried by FoodBev Media, Ghingo said he plans to build on the company's momentum by investing in brands and modernizing operations.

Hormel generates more than $12 billion in annual net sales from a brand roster that includes Spam, Skippy peanut butter, Planters nuts, Jennie-O turkey, and Columbus Meats. The company recently agreed to purchase Wisconsin-based chicken processor Brakebush Brothers for $1.055 billion.

Ghingo takes over during the final year of a corporate turnaround plan, The Star Tribune reported. That initiative centers on upgrading supply chains, rolling out product innovations, and balancing higher-end brand sales against pressure from grocery inflation and frugal shoppers.
