KFC is changing what flows from its American soda fountains. The fast-food chain announced on Oct. 15, 2026, that it has chosen The Coca-Cola Company as its new exclusive beverage partner in the United States, according to PR Newswire and Nation's Restaurant News. The transition ends a domestic partnership with PepsiCo that lasted roughly 40 years.

The switch will begin in January 2027. According to reporting from Nation's Restaurant News, Fox Business, and CNBC, Coca-Cola drinks are scheduled to arrive across all 3,718 domestic restaurants by the end of the first quarter of 2027. Reuters reported that the financial terms of the multi-year deal were not publicly disclosed.

The ties between KFC and PepsiCo date back to 1986, when PepsiCo acquired the chicken chain to expand into fast food, as reported by Reuters and BigGo Finance. Before that buyout, CNBC reported that KFC was Coca-Cola's second-largest domestic fountain client, trailing only McDonald's. PepsiCo later spun off its fast-food operations into Yum! Brands in 1997, yet Fox Business and Morningstar noted that American KFC locations kept pouring Pepsi fountain drinks for nearly three decades following the corporate split.

The contract change applies only to KFC restaurants inside the United States. A PepsiCo spokesperson confirmed to Reuters and The Wall Street Journal that PepsiCo and Yum! Brands recently renewed their beverage agreements for KFC locations outside the country. The spokesperson also confirmed a renewed 10-year contract to keep supplying fountain drinks to Taco Bell restaurants.

Inside KFC, management is framing the shift as part of a broader brand overhaul. In an internal staff memo obtained by CNBC, KFC U.S. President Catherine Tan-Gillespie called the vendor change a critical step in the "Kentucky Fried Comeback." She stated that the new agreement provides a platform to innovate, keep up with changing customer tastes, and give diners more reasons to visit. Nation's Restaurant News reported that Tan-Gillespie described both brands as originals with deep consumer loyalty, arguing that the partnership is about more than what sits inside the cup.

The drink swap arrives as KFC tries to lift its afternoon traffic. According to Nation's Restaurant News and Mint, the company has been testing a new drink lineup called "Sips" at a concept restaurant in McKinney, Texas. Dagmar Boggs, head of foodservice for Coca-Cola North America, told PR Newswire that combining KFC's menu with Coca-Cola's catalog will open up new flavor pairings and visit experiences.

KFC is not the first major chain to step away from PepsiCo. Morningstar reported that both Marriott International and Costco Wholesale food courts recently switched their beverage supply contracts to Coca-Cola. Morningstar also cited retail figures from TD Cowen analyst Robert Moskow showing that over the past two years, Coca-Cola gained 70 basis points of market share in carbonated soft drinks, while PepsiCo dropped 130 basis points. A basis point represents one hundredth of a percentage point.

Wall Street tracked the announcement immediately. Barron's and BigGo Finance reported that Coca-Cola shares climbed to an intraday high of $94.10 on Oct. 15. Morningstar and Investing.com noted that Yum! Brands shares rose 2.7%, while PepsiCo stock fell 0.7%, widening its decline to roughly 10.6% for the year.

Reuters reported that market analysts viewed the departure as a setback for PepsiCo leadership. Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets, told Reuters that dropping a flagship customer like KFC is a severe optical blow. Woods said the loss could act as the final straw for PepsiCo Chief Executive Ramon Laguarta, who faces pressure from activist investors alongside sluggish profit margin recovery.
