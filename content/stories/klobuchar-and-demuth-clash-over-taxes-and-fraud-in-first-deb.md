Democratic U.S. Sen. Amy Klobuchar and Republican Minnesota House Speaker Lisa Demuth met Monday morning for the first debate of the 2026 gubernatorial campaign. The 90-minute forum, co-hosted by the Minnesota Chamber of Commerce and the Brainerd Lakes Chamber at Madden’s on Gull Lake in Brainerd, was moderated by KSTP-TV Chief Political Reporter Tom Hauser and streamed live by KSTP. The meeting came 15 days ahead of the Nov. 3 general election, with RealClearPolitics polling averages showing Klobuchar leading Demuth 48.7% to 40.3%, as reported by CBS News and KARE 11. FOX 9 noted that Demuth would become the first Black governor in Minnesota history if elected.

Both candidates ruled out state tax increases and agreed on speeding up environmental permitting and cutting regulatory burdens, according to coverage from MPR News and CBS News. Demuth labeled the state’s current operating climate "unsustainable." She pledged to reduce permitting delays, roll back regulations, and lower what she called burdensome taxes.

Klobuchar framed her platform around economic growth, saying she detests federal tariffs and acknowledges headwinds like fuel prices and federal instability, but argued tariffs cannot be an excuse for sluggish growth. According to reports from CBS News and Alpha News, Klobuchar stated she would like to lower taxes and promised her first executive directive would forbid unfunded state mandates on local governments. Alpha News reported she also signaled support for lifting Minnesota’s moratorium on new nuclear power plants and said the state's paid leave program should be adjusted to remain fiscally responsible.

The candidates diverged on the oversight of utility-scale data centers. Demuth called proposed data centers strictly a local control issue where local governments and residents should decide without state mandates, CBS News reported. Klobuchar argued that while local communities should not be forced to host a facility, state regulators must remain involved because of the size and power demands of the projects.

State spending and administrative oversight prompted the sharpest exchanges. Demuth attacked DFL management of welfare programs, citing her 2019 child care oversight bill that failed under Democratic control. As reported by KARE 11, Demuth alleged Klobuchar sent more than 1,000 press releases without mentioning the Feeding Our Future fraud between 2022 and late 2025, and Demuth vowed to fire all current state agency commissioners if elected.

Klobuchar separated herself from outgoing Gov. Tim Walz by emphasizing her background as a county prosecutor, telling Demuth, "I know you wish you were debating Tim Walz, but I'm the one here right now up here." According to CBS News and Blue North Beacon, Klobuchar said she would have caught the fraud and acted on it. KARE 11 and Alpha News reported that Klobuchar highlighted calling U.S. Attorney Andy Luger, securing extra federal prosecutors for Minnesota, promising a full audit of state government, and stating she would have prioritized budget surpluses for infrastructure and technology.

The candidates face each other in their second debate on Wednesday, Oct. 21, at 7:00 p.m. at the KTTC/KXLT studios in Rochester, co-produced by FOX 9 and Gray Media stations.
