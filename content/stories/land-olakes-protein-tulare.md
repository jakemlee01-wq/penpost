Land O'Lakes is best known for its own butter and cheese. Its next push is something its name won't be on.

The Arden Hills-based cooperative is overhauling its largest manufacturing plant, in Tulare, California, to sell more protein-based ingredients to other food and beverage companies, Twin Cities Business reported. Some unused buildings there will reopen, and the plant will take in more milk.

The first phase is ultra-filtered milk, which beverage companies would sell under their own brands. A second phase, still under consideration, would dry milk into protein powder for products like protein bars.

Because Land O'Lakes is a cooperative, the farmers who supply its milk also own it. About 1,100 dairy farms supply the co-op. Heather Anfang, who leads its dairy foods business, expects the added production to raise the yearly dividends paid to those farmers, with benefits starting in 2027 and growing in 2028.

TCB didn't report an investment amount or job numbers, and the new work happens in California, not here. The Minnesota connection runs through the co-op's headquarters and the farmer-owners who share in its results.

It's a practical move: make more of what other companies are asking for, and send more of the return back to the farms that supply it.
