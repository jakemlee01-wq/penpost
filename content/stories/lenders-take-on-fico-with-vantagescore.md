For decades, getting a mortgage has meant bowing to a single three-digit standard: the Classic FICO score. Now, the mortgage world is opening its doors to another competitor, and the shift is picking up speed.

On October 12, 2026, the Federal Home Loan Bank of Boston announced that its network of more than 400 member financial institutions can pledge mortgage collateral evaluated under the VantageScore 4.0 model. VantageScore estimates the policy will allow New England lenders to evaluate an additional 1.5 million consumers across Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, and Vermont. Anthony Hutchinson stated that accepting the model provides lenders with cost-effective tools and helps expand homeownership across the region.

The Boston bank is not alone. It joins seven other regional Federal Home Loan Banks that already permit VantageScore 4.0 collateral, including Chicago, Cincinnati, Dallas, Des Moines, New York, San Francisco, and Topeka. Member institutions in New England can immediately submit qualifying loans to secure advances and liquidity lines, aligning the regional bank with federal housing programs like Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, the Department of Veterans Affairs, and the Federal Housing Administration.

Big private lenders are moving just as fast. Rocket Mortgage named VantageScore 4.0 its preferred model for eligible direct-to-consumer Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and VA loans beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026. During a four-month evaluation pulling 1.4 million dual credit reports, Rocket Mortgage found that borrowers who benefited saved an average of $1,600 at closing, according to reports from National Mortgage Professional and Scotsman Guide. Jay Bray stated that the mortgage industry relied on one model for decades, adding that Rocket compared both options and picked the one that helped more qualified borrowers.

Other mortgage giants are splitting the difference or rewiring their systems. United Wholesale Mortgage announced plans to pull both FICO and VantageScore reports and apply whichever score is higher. Pennymac deployed VantageScore 4.0 across its direct, broker, and correspondent channels, as reported by Citybiz and Scotsman Guide. Movement Mortgage also updated its underwriting guidelines to evaluate eligible conventional applications with the model.

The surge in volume is already showing up in federal loan packages. Milliman reported that Fannie Mae- and Freddie Mac-backed mortgages originated with VantageScore reached nearly $4 billion in August 2026, leaping from just $8 million in April. Citybiz noted that VantageScore loans made up roughly 3% of the $400 billion in securitizations issued by the two agencies over the six months ending in September.

Federal regulators have helped clear the runway. On September 30, 2026, Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte eliminated separate pricing tiers, establishing a single framework that treats Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 scores equally for loans delivered to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Meanwhile, the FHA published guidelines allowing lenders to use VantageScore 4.0 and FICO Score 10T starting January 1, 2027, according to the ABA Banking Journal. The FHA will keep its minimum credit score thresholds of 500, 580, and 620 unchanged and will still require reports from three credit bureaus.

VantageScore was founded in 2006 by Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion to inject competition into credit evaluation. Its 4.0 model looks at 24 months of trended payment behavior, factoring in rent, utilities, and phone bills. Nationwide, VantageScore estimates the model could expand mortgage eligibility to 13 million prospective borrowers who lack traditional credit histories. Equifax confirmed it will cap its VantageScore 4.0 mortgage price at $1 per score through 2028, projecting up to $1 billion in cumulative savings.

The shakeup has taken a direct toll on the incumbent. Fair Isaac Corporation shares dropped about 60% during 2026, though Money Morning reported the stock later rebounded 11% to close near $740.
