In mid-October 2026, Lockheed Martin announced plans to create 6,000 jobs across the United States to accelerate production of critical munitions, Engineering.com reported. According to reporting from Manufacturing Business Technology, 2,000 of those new jobs will be located directly in Texas.

A central piece of that growth is in Grand Prairie, Texas, where the company held a ribbon-cutting ceremony for a nearly 200,000-square-foot expansion, Engineering.com reported. Defense News reported that Lockheed Martin is investing $300 million into the site to expand its advanced manufacturing capacity. Dow Jones Newswires reported that the addition brings the facility's total manufacturing footprint to more than 735,000 square feet. PR Newswire reported that the work includes 100,000 square feet of newly built space, along with 85,000 square feet of existing office space converted into manufacturing floor.

The Grand Prairie plant currently employs nearly 800 production workers and manufactured roughly 250,000 parts in 2025, according to PR Newswire. Defense News reported that workers recently installed more than 30 new high-precision machines, with site leaders expecting to reach nearly 100 newly added machines across the site by the end of 2026.

Engineering.com reported that the site will install automated machining equipment expected to be fully operational by 2027. As part of that setup, Lockheed Martin is partnering with manufacturing technology company Hadrian to install a 24-machine automated machining cell inside the facility, Engineering.com noted. Dow Jones Newswires reported that the Hadrian automated cell will be operational in early 2027 and will be run directly by Lockheed Martin's production workforce. Hadrian CEO Chris Power stated that warfighters cannot wait on peacetime timelines, and Hadrian is bringing automation, software, and workforce training directly into the plant to build parts faster.

Defense News reported that the Grand Prairie plant supports Lockheed Martin's Missiles and Fire Control division, making parts for PAC-3 MSE and THAAD interceptors, the Precision Strike Missile, and the AIM-260 Joint Advanced Tactical Missile. Defense News added that parts built in Grand Prairie are shipped to final-assembly operations in Camden, Arkansas, and Troy, Alabama.

The Texas investment is part of a broader corporate commitment to invest between $8 billion and $9 billion through 2030 to modernize or expand more than 20 U.S. facilities, Engineering.com reported. Industrial Equipment News reported that this capital program is projected to increase Lockheed Martin's total manufacturing and warehousing footprint for munitions by nearly 50%. Industrial Equipment News also reported that the expansion follows collaboration agreements, including work with GM Defense to use commercial manufacturing techniques and expand domestic production capacity.

Lockheed Martin chairman, president, and CEO Jim Taiclet stated that the company is combining commercial speed with American workforce expertise to deliver munitions at speed and scale, PR Newswire reported.
