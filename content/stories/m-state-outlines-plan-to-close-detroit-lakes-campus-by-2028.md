Minnesota State Community and Technical College announced plans to close its Detroit Lakes campus no earlier than the summer of 2028, according to an M State news release and reporting from CBS News. The facility will remain fully open and operational until at least that time. The recommendation still requires formal review and final approval from the Minnesota State Board of Trustees, as Inside Higher Ed reported.

The shift comes down to a large footprint with few students. As reported by KAXE and Inside Higher Ed, the Detroit Lakes campus serves 111 students, yet it is physically the largest campus in the M State system. Systemwide, the Minnesota State Colleges and Universities Board of Trustees and Chancellor Scott Olson urged schools to right-size facilities, meaning adjust building space to match actual student use, amid rising costs and an estimated $30 million system deficit.

M State expects the move to cut ongoing overhead and halt major maintenance spending. Closing the facility is projected to save approximately $1 million each year in utility, maintenance, insurance, and technology costs, according to KAXE and Inside Higher Ed. The transition also spares the college more than $18 million in deferred maintenance and capital modernization, including roof replacements and HVAC upgrades, Inside Higher Ed and MPR News reported.

President Carrie Brimhall noted that every dollar spent on an aging roof takes away from student support or new programs, according to MPR News. Brimhall stated that the decision was not made lightly, adding that the campus has served the community for generations, though the college must remain strong across all locations. College leadership emphasized that M State as a whole is financially solid and growing enrollment online and at campuses in Fergus Falls, Moorhead, and Wadena, MPR News reported.

Academic shifts will start with a phased rollout in fall 2027 to limit student disruptions, according to WDAY Radio and M State. Brimhall confirmed that every current student enrolled at Detroit Lakes will have a supported pathway to finish their degree, MPR News reported.

Core programs will continue in different locations. M State plans to keep nursing in Detroit Lakes by pursuing a lease agreement with a local community partner to house classes off-campus, according to Fergus Now and MPR News. The radiologic technology program will move about 50 miles away to M State's Moorhead campus. Brimhall noted that 15 percent of radiologic technology students live within 25 miles of Detroit Lakes, while the vast majority commute from the Fargo-Moorhead area.

What happens to the physical property is not yet settled. WDAY Radio and Fergus Now reported that the future use or sale of the building has not been decided, though M State will honor existing leases and evaluate redevelopment options with local officials. The college also stated it will work to minimize effects on Detroit Lakes faculty and staff.

A public community informational meeting is scheduled for Thursday, November 5, to answer questions from residents, according to Fergus Now. M State announced it will post official updates on its website on or around the 15th of each month.
