Alexandria Industries, a regional manufacturer that designs and fabricates precision aluminum components, is teaming up with Minnesota State Community and Technical College on a customized workforce education partnership.

The Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development awarded $49,000 to M State to fund the customized education partnership, KFGO reported. The award is part of a broader $2.5 million package of 14 workforce development grants distributed across eight Minnesota higher education institutions, according to reporting from FOX 9 and KFGO.

Funding flows through the Minnesota Job Skills Partnership, a state initiative that requires employers to pair with accredited post-secondary schools to build targeted training. Under the Alexandria agreement, M State will conduct a 30-hour Serving Through Leadership Program tailored for current and emerging managers. The college will also provide a 15-hour Professional Development Program open to employees across the wider company.

According to DEED, the coursework targets workplace communication, leadership effectiveness, employee engagement, and overall company culture. Instructors will use self-assessments, group discussions, reflection activities, team exercises, coaching conversations, and practical workplace applications. Workers who complete the classes will receive official certificates of completion.

Across Minnesota, this grant cycle is projected to train more than 2,000 workers. DEED Commissioner Matt Varilek stated that workforce training programs help employees build skills and lay foundations for business success, while connecting people to careers in high-demand fields with family-sustaining wages. Varilek pointed to Minnesota higher education institutions as essential partners with proven track records in delivering business instruction.

State figures show the broader scope of the initiative. Since 2019, the Minnesota Job Skills Partnership has awarded $47.5 million in grants, trained more than 47,000 workers, and leveraged $79.1 million in matching private funds.
