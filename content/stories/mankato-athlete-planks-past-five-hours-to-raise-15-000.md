Inside Shared Spaces at Emy Frentz on South 2nd Street in Mankato, supporters packed the room while an athlete held herself off the floor on her forearms for hours. Between sets of questions from spectators, she sang along to music played by an on-site DJ during a free community gathering held from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.

According to reporting from KEYC News Now, Mankato endurance athlete Vusa Bentley completed a continuous forearm plank of 5 hours and 3 minutes on Saturday, October 24, 2026. Bentley, a mother of two boys who grew up in Azerbaijan and has lived in Mankato for years, picked that exact time to honor her son, who wears jersey number 53 for the Mankato East High School football team.

The effort stands as a potential Guinness World Record. Reporting from the Mankato Free Press and KEYC News Now noted that the previous verified female world record was 4 hours, 30 minutes, and 11 seconds, set by Canadian DonnaJean Wilde. Bentley also topped an unverified 5-hour mark set by another Canadian competitor earlier in the month by 3 minutes, KEYC reported.

Beside testing endurance, the event surpassed its fundraising target by collecting $15,000 in direct donations for the Greater Mankato Area United Way, KEYC News Now reported. The Greater Mankato Area United Way reported that the money supports 75 health, education, and basic needs programs serving more than 65,000 people across Blue Earth, Nicollet, Le Sueur, and Waseca counties.

KEYC News Now reported that Bentley spoke directly to the room about the community focus behind the physical challenge: "My purpose was to build something for our community. This is your event... The only thing I want you to take from this message and platform: I'm nobody important, I just do what I love, and I use my body to make a difference in the world. But you do too have skills and assets in you." Her mother, Donette Wheelock, told KEYC that Bentley never wanted the day to center on herself, believing that people grow stronger when they lift one another up.

Getting to the five-hour mark required five years of focused training for multi-hour planks, according to reporting from the Mankato Free Press. KEYC News Now reported that Bentley has submitted witness statements, documentation, and video recordings to Guinness World Records, with review expected to take several months. The Mankato event is part of her "United We Plank" campaign, which aims to complete a one-hour plank in all 50 states to aid non-profit programs, with Washington state planned as an upcoming stop.
