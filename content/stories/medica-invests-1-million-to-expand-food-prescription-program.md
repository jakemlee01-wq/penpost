Medica announced a $1 million investment on Oct. 26 to expand food prescription and hunger-relief programs across Minnesota, partnering with Fairview Health Services and Falcon Heights-based nonprofit food hub The Good Acre.

The initiative focuses on patients managing chronic health conditions by supplying fresh food directly to clinics or doorsteps. According to reporting from the Fillmore County Journal, the program currently delivers locally sourced food to approximately 700 Minnesota households every week. The Good Acre sources those food boxes and meal packages through a regional network of more than 120 farmers and producers, working with emerging and diverse growers to supply culturally relevant produce and proteins.

The expansion comes alongside rising hunger statistics across the state. A statewide hunger study reported by the Fillmore County Journal and Minnesota Women’s Press found that 22% of Minnesota households could not afford the food they needed, and 32% of households with children experienced food insecurity. Additionally, 20% of households with children relied on emergency food resources, and Minnesota food shelf visits exceeded 9 million in 2025. That total is 2.5 times higher than in 2019 and five times higher than two decades ago.

Fairview and The Good Acre have spent more than a decade testing community food models, including Fairview's Veggie Rx and Community Cooks Meal Box programs. The new funding builds on health outcomes measured during Fairview's Fair Table produce prescription program. As reported by the Fillmore County Journal, diabetic patients in that program saw their average A1C levels drop from 8.5 to 7.9, while 55% of participants recorded lower blood pressure. In addition, 72% of participating patients reported feeling more secure about meeting monthly nutritional needs after working with a dedicated Food Resource Navigator. Over the course of that studied initiative, the partners purchased and distributed more than 37,000 pounds of locally produced vegetables and goods.

According to Food Connect and Medica, the $1 million will be deployed immediately to refine delivery logistics, increase enrollment capacity, and broaden doorstep and clinic delivery routes across Minnesota. Medica stated that food insecurity affects health long before medical care is required, calling the investment an effort to make nutritious food accessible through experienced local partners. Fairview stated that pairing healthcare with regional growers connects patients to healthy foods while supporting the local food economy.
