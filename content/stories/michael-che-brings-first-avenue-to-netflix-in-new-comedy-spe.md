Viewers pressing play on Netflix this week see black-and-white shots of downtown Minneapolis and the painted star wall outside First Avenue. The historic music venue serves as the backdrop for "Michael Che: Shot in Minnesota," which premiered on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, as reported by Bring Me The News and Netflix.

The 59-minute production marks Che's third solo hour-long special on the streaming platform, following "Michael Che Matters" in 2016 and "Michael Che: Shame the Devil" in 2021, according to MPR News and Complex. The performance was recorded over four shows across two nights on Friday, Aug. 28, and Saturday, Aug. 29, 2026, on the main stage at First Avenue, according to Ingstad Media and venue records.

The downtown shows followed an unannounced pop-up set that Che performed earlier in the summer at Sisyphus Brewing in Minneapolis, Bring Me The News reported. Beyond his performance on stage, Che told Complex that the project serves as his directorial debut, marking the first time he has directed a comedy special. The Ringer and Bring Me The News reported that Che executive produced the show alongside Matthew Vaughan of Rotten Science.

Che explained to interviewers that the special's title is tongue-in-cheek and broad, adding that it felt inclusive and could happen to anybody, according to MPR News. Che also noted his affection for performing in the Twin Cities, explaining that he loves that it is cold and that local audiences receive everything well. MPR News and The Ringer reported that Che intentionally chose Minneapolis and First Avenue to provide laughs and shared connection during tense political and social climates.

Che told Complex that the material took about two years to build, write, and polish on the road around his television schedule. During the performance, Che addressed topical subjects including race, free speech, politics, and celebrity culture, jokingly dubbing Minneapolis "the center of the fight," Bring Me The News reported. The set also includes extended routines about relationship dynamics, shopping for Valentine's Day gifts, and his Saturday Night Live Weekend Update co-anchor Colin Jost's marriage to actress Scarlett Johansson, according to UpNext by Reelgood and Netflix Is A Joke.

Che spends roughly 60 seconds of the 59-minute performance specifically discussing Minnesota, during which he delivers a joke referencing state daycare fraud allegations, MPR News reported. That brief passage drew pushback from Minneapolis stand-up comedian Comrade Tripp, who told MPR News that propagating that stigma was unhelpful and commented that a wink is a dog whistle that even fewer people can understand.

Che confirmed to The Ringer and Complex that he remains a writer and co-anchor on Saturday Night Live for the current television season, while planning to pursue additional film and television directing projects in the future.
