Minneapolis opened 12 one-day pop-up early voting sites across city neighborhoods on Monday, Oct. 19, running through Oct. 30 to broaden access ahead of Election Day.

City and state leaders gathered Monday morning at the Webber Community Center in north Minneapolis to launch the initiative, according to reporting by KARE 11. Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey and Minnesota Secretary of State Steve Simon attended the kickoff to discuss local voting logistics and accessibility.

Frey stated that the city wants to ensure full access to the ballot, saying the municipal role is to make sure every eligible voter can cast their ballot freely, safely, and confidently. Simon noted that the temporary sites rely on the same security standards used at traditional polling places, including paper ballots, secured ballot tabulators, and trained election workers.

North Minneapolis residents Patricia Danielson and Tom Bauer were among the first to cast ballots at the Webber Community Center after receiving a city mailer, MPR News reported. Danielson told MPR News that she chose to vote early in person because she did not trust her ballot in the mail.

The initial pop-up ran from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. at 4401 N. Bryant Ave., setting the standard operating hours for the neighborhood tour. Subsequent locations include the Minneapolis Institute of Art on Oct. 20, the Cedar Cultural Center on Oct. 21, Midtown Global Market on Oct. 22, and Powderhorn Park on Oct. 23. Other stops scheduled through Oct. 30 cover Higher Ground, People Serving People, the Weisman Art Museum, Little Earth of United Tribes, Lynnhurst Park Recreation Center, Sabathani Community Center, and Augsburg University's Christensen Center.

Each pop-up and central location allows registered and unregistered residents to vote. Qualified voters can register on-site with valid proof of residence, feed their completed ballot directly into an electronic tabulator, use ballot-marking devices, request curbside service, or access multi-language assistance.

Two additional permanent early voting centers opened Tuesday, Oct. 20, at the Urban League Twin Cities on Plymouth Avenue North and Bethel Lutheran Church on 17th Avenue South, KARE 11 reported. At the same time, the city expanded hours at its primary Early Vote Center at 980 E. Hennepin Ave. That facility is open weekdays from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. and weekends from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., with hours stretching to 7 p.m. on Tuesday, Oct. 27.

Turnout numbers show steady demand leading into the expansion. Minneapolis Elections and Voter Services Director Katie Smith announced that 18,132 residents had submitted early ballots by Friday, Oct. 16. That tally represents a 34.7% increase over the 13,714 ballots cast at the same point in the 2022 midterm election, and a 47.7% increase compared to 12,501 ballots at that stage in 2018.

Statewide activity shows a similar upward track. MPR News reported that Minnesotans cast more than 200,000 ballots during the first four weeks of early voting across the state, up from roughly 170,000 during the identical stretch in 2022.

Simon emphasized that Minnesota has ranked first in voter turnout in most election cycles over the last 50 years, and he praised the Minneapolis elections staff as an ethical and hardworking model for the country. Frey said that Minneapolis is taking an approach focused on ballot access while noting public concerns elsewhere regarding election interference and intimidation.

In-person early voting in Minneapolis continues through 5 p.m. on Monday, Nov. 2, the day before Election Day.
