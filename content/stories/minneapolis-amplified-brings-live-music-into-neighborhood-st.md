Live music, DJ sets, and visual art installations are moving into neighborhood storefronts across Minneapolis this fall.

The pop-up performance series, titled Minneapolis Amplified, is scheduled to run through December 2026. The program is organized by local events group Dance Agenda, also known as My Dance Agenda, in direct partnership with the Minneapolis Arts and Cultural Affairs Department.

Public backing comes through the city's Small Business Resiliency Fund and supports the Minneapolis Forward commercial corridor strategy. The initiative uses municipal financing to help local merchants recover customer foot traffic and spotlight commercial hubs. Mayor Jacob Frey stated that the events offer a way to support small businesses while watching familiar community spots come alive in new ways.

All performances in the series are free to attend and open to all ages. As reported by FOX 9 and city announcements, the showcases take place both inside and outside businesses that do not typically host concerts, including diners, auto repair garages, and plant boutiques.

Organizers planned the series partly to address recent community strain. According to reporting by WCCO, organizers aimed to encourage residents to return to public spaces following local anxieties linked to federal immigration enforcement actions under Operation Metro Surge.

Leo Himself, the founder of My Dance Agenda, told WCCO that residents were scared to go out after Operation Metro Surge occurred. He stated that the events offer people a moment to step away from pain, connect with neighbors, and dance together. Alyssa Schmidt, an attendee interviewed by WCCO, praised the neighborhood focus, stating that it makes her happy that organizers want people to come together as a community to dance and have fun.

The series launched over the weekend of Oct. 10 and Oct. 11, 2026. On Saturday, Oct. 10, organizers converted the parking lot outside Uncle Franky’s into an outdoor dance floor from 1 to 5 p.m. The following day, Sunday, Oct. 11, the series hosted a daytime performance at Band Box Diner from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m.

On Sunday, Oct. 18, the series set up inside East Hennepin Auto Service from noon to 3 p.m. for an afternoon of live band performances. Brian Harbour, the manager at East Hennepin Auto Service, told WCCO that the business shut down its gas pumps and did not earn money from hosting. Harbour stated that the shop participated simply to let community members know the business is there.

Additional events are scheduled across the autumn calendar. Minneapolis Amplified plans an evening session at TAP IN Kitchen and Cocktails on Saturday, Oct. 31, running from 8 to 11 p.m. Mother Co. Plants will host a visual art exhibition and DJ pop-up on Wednesday, Nov. 4, from 4 to 8 p.m. Organizers plan to announce additional concert dates through December.
