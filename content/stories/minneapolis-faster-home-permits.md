If you've been putting off new windows because the permit felt like a project of its own, Minneapolis just made that part shorter.

The city announced on Oct. 2 that it has dropped plan review for a handful of common jobs: flat roofs on homes and commercial buildings, siding, and replacement windows and doors. Building codes still apply. What goes away is a string of administrative steps before approval.

The city expects hundreds of permits a year to be issued, or ready to pay for, within one to two days of applying. Some may be available right away. It also says the average approval time for residential remodel permits has already dropped from about three weeks to under one week.

Contractors who qualify can opt into an "Express Limited Permit" that runs entirely online, payment included. The city says it plans to add more permit types to that list.

There's a historic preservation piece too. Routine repairs on historic properties can now go through a standard building permit, and mid-sized projects can be approved by staff without a public hearing.

Two things to keep in mind. These are the city's own figures, and the real test is whether contractors see shorter waits during busy weeks, not just in the announcement. The city's release also didn't give an effective date for the change.
