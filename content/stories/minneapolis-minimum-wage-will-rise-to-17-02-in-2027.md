The minimum wage in Minneapolis is scheduled to increase from $16.37 to $17.02 per hour on January 1, 2027, according to reporting by MinneapoliMedia News. The 65-cent hourly adjustment represents an increase of approximately 3.97 percent.

For an employee working 40 hours per week at the minimum rate, the boost amounts to an extra $26 per week in gross compensation before taxes and other payroll deductions. MinneapoliMedia News reported that Minneapolis adjusts its wage floor automatically every January 1 to reflect inflation, within set regulatory limits.

The $17.02 rate applies universally across all employers regardless of size, following the elimination of separate tiers for small and large businesses in July 2024. Coverage is based entirely on where work is physically performed. The rule applies to any qualifying employee who logs at least two hours of work within Minneapolis borders during a calendar week, including part-time, temporary, and seasonal workers. Businesses located outside the city must pay the mandated rate for any covered work performed within city lines.

MinneapoliMedia News noted that Minneapolis prohibits tip crediting, which means employers cannot count tips toward meeting the base wage. Tipped employees must receive the full $17.02 hourly rate directly from their employer in addition to any gratuities they collect. City rules also forbid deductions for mandatory uniforms, tools, or gear if the expense drops a worker's effective pay below the minimum wage. Employers are required by law to keep employee wage and hour payroll records for at least three years.

The Minneapolis wage will stand more than $5 higher than Minnesota's statewide minimum wage, which the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry reports will rise from $11.41 to $11.87 on January 1, 2027. Bring Me The News reported that the rate will match St. Paul's scheduled minimum wage of $17.02 for large employers on that same day, marking the first time base pay in both Twin Cities tops $17 an hour.

According to the City of Minneapolis, the City Council enacted the minimum wage policy in 2017 to aid low-wage workers, citing data showing that one in four local workers, or 78,000 individuals, earned less than $15 per hour before 2018. The ordinance remained in effect after the Minnesota Supreme Court affirmed the measure on January 22, 2020, following a legal challenge. The Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis is studying the economic effects of the policy through seven annual reports scheduled through 2028, monitoring shifts in hours, earnings, and hiring patterns in industries such as hospitality and retail.
