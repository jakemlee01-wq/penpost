The Minneapolis Public Housing Authority opened public applications for its Bring It Home rental voucher waitlist on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, closing the window at 11:59 p.m. on Thursday, October 15. The three-day period marks the first time since 2019 that the agency has accepted applications from the general public for any tenant-based rental assistance program.

Under the program, participating households pay 30 percent of their adjusted gross income toward rent, and the voucher subsidizes the remaining balance up to limits set by the housing authority using federal Department of Housing and Urban Development standards. To qualify, applicants must live in Minnesota, pay more than 30 percent of their monthly income toward rent, earn at or below 50 percent of the Area Median Income, and agree to lease a home inside Minneapolis city limits. Applicants cannot currently hold a federal Section 8 voucher.

The housing authority anticipates placing up to 1,500 eligible households on the new waitlist through a random lottery rather than a first-come, first-served system. Priority placement in the lottery is directed to households with children under 18 years old and families earning 30 percent or less of the Area Median Income. According to reporting from the Minnesota Star Tribune, agency staff will process the lottery and notify applicants of their official waitlist status through their online RentCafe accounts by November 1, 2026.

Families applied through the housing authority's online RentCafe portal, with staff offering language translation and accommodation support by phone and email. Before opening the process to the broader public, the agency began administering Bring It Home vouchers over the summer of 2026 by offering assistance to more than 500 eligible families who were already on the city's 2019 federal Section 8 waitlist.

The local program is supported by a two-year, $21 million grant awarded to the housing authority by Minnesota Housing, as reported by MPR News. The Minnesota Legislature established the funding in 2023 through state appropriations and a Twin Cities metro sales tax. The agency expects the $21 million allocation to finance the equivalent of 600 to 700 rental vouchers across both tenant-based vouchers, which follow the renter, and project-based vouchers, which remain tied to specific units.

Across Minnesota, approximately 400 households have leased homes through Bring It Home vouchers to date across 49 participating agencies, with St. Cloud's Housing and Redevelopment Authority being the first to launch applications in January 2026, according to MPR News. In Minneapolis, the public housing authority currently oversees nearly 7,500 federal Housing Choice Vouchers serving more than 18,500 residents. Housing authority Executive Director and CEO Abdi Warsame stated that the launch will help hundreds of low-income families this year and thousands more in the future afford a safe, stable, and affordable place to live, while thanking state leaders for championing the program.
