Families and teachers across Minneapolis now know their neighborhood school buildings will stay open. On Tuesday night, October 13, 2026, the Minneapolis Public Schools Board of Education voted unanimously to approve Resolution 2026-0078. The measure puts the district's transformation plan on pause and stops all proposed school closures and mergers for the 2027 to 2028 school year, according to reporting from WCCO-TV, KARE 11, and the Minnesota Reformer.

The vote followed more than four hours of discussion and an unexpected opening recommendation. Dr. Lisa Sayles-Adams, the superintendent, opened the meeting by withdrawing her support for closure options. She recommended that the board not move forward with any closures or mergers at this time. FOX 9 and WCCO reported that Board Chair Collin Beachy quickly backed that position. Beachy stated that as long as he sits in the chair, he will not entertain any motion proposing to close schools in 2027 to 2028, adding that the city needs to heal and take a breath.

Board directors Sharon El-Amin and Greta Callahan authored the pause resolution. El-Amin described the superintendent's reversal as a blindside move, though she noted she was grateful because her fight was to prevent closures and make sure dollars invested in northside schools stayed there. Board member Abdul Abdi supported the pause as well, telling FOX 9 that the community went through a lot during the year and needs stability.

The district's original proposals, introduced on September 15, outlined three options to close or merge up to 16 school buildings. Those closures would have affected roughly 27 percent of district facilities and displaced 12 to 14 percent of the student body, according to WCCO and FOX 9. Southwest Voices, Fight Back! News, and CBS News noted that sites on the potential closure list included Kenny Elementary, Field Elementary, Pratt Elementary, Heritage Academy, downtown's FAIR High School for the Arts, and Longfellow Alternative High School.

District leadership initially brought forward the plans to address declining enrollment and a $39.7 million budget deficit. District buildings have room for roughly 43,000 students, but current enrollment sits below 30,000. Today, only about 53 percent of school-age children living in Minneapolis attend district public schools, as reported by WCCO and Fight Back! News.

Community pushback was shaped by recent district history. Bring Me The News reported that memories of the 2020 Comprehensive District Design, which redrew attendance boundaries, and pandemic fallout coincided with a loss of about 5,000 students.

Two dozen community members addressed the board during Tuesday's meeting. WCCO and FOX 9 reported that kindergarten parent Robert Thesing called the reversal a sign of true leadership and a small step forward in establishing trust. Another parent, Jamia Pettinger, said good leadership means admitting when a plan is a mess and praised the recommendation to stop. The Minneapolis Federation of Educators, MFE Local 59, also celebrated the vote, calling the idea of closing 27 percent of the district a non-starter with city residents.

The newly passed resolution sets out clear requirements before any closures can be brought back to the table. KARE 11 reported that any future facilities plan must prioritize boosting enrollment, conduct a review of administrative spending and staffing at the John B. Davis Education Service Center headquarters, complete racial equity impact assessments, and provide transparent cost analyses.

With the pause approved, the school board canceled a vote on closures that had been scheduled for November 10, 2026. Instead, as KARE 11 and Southwest Voices reported, the district plans to redirect its work toward expanding early-childhood education, keeping neighborhood schools stable, and running marketing campaigns to bring students back.
