Minneapolis has crossed into a buyer's market, with home sellers outnumbering prospective buyers by 26.5 percent, according to a national Redfin study reported by FOX 9.

Redfin recorded 15,057 active home listings in the Minneapolis area compared to roughly 11,904 estimated active house hunters. Under the brokerage's definitions, any metro where sellers outnumber buyers by more than 10 percent qualifies as a buyer's market. Markets within 10 percent in either direction are considered balanced, while areas with over 10 percent fewer sellers than buyers are classified as seller's markets.

FOX 9 reported that recent MLS tracking shows Minneapolis residential sale prices holding near a median of $370,000, with properties spending an average of 25 days on the market before going under contract.

The national report, authored by Redfin's Dana Anderson and Senior Economist Asad Khan and published on October 14, 2026, shows the Twin Cities trend reflecting a broad national pattern. Across the entire United States, home sellers outnumbered buyers by 62.6 percent in September 2026. That margin is the widest recorded since Redfin began tracking the metric in 2013 and marks the largest inventory gap since the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis.

Nationally, active homebuyer counts fell 2.4 percent month over month to a record low of 928,892. At the same time, active sellers rose 1.2 percent to 1,510,784, representing the highest national inventory count since March 2020.

As reported by Mortgage Professional America, Khan stated that the shift is a buyer's market born out of caution, not strength. Khan noted that would-be buyers have been priced out or scared off by economic uncertainty. He pointed out the irony that supply conditions are among the best for buyers in years, but have arrived when sustained costs keep many people from affording them.

Redfin emphasized that the national inventory surplus comes from unsold properties lingering on the market rather than a sudden spike in new construction or new listings. That backlog has forced sellers to endure fewer property showings and longer waits.

Out of the 50 largest metropolitan areas in the country, Redfin found that 41 are now buyer's markets. Sun Belt metros lead the country in surplus listings, with Miami sellers exceeding buyers by 158.9 percent and Nashville seeing a 152.3 percent surplus. Only five metros remain seller's markets: San Francisco, Milwaukee, Newark, Nassau County, New York, and Montgomery County, Pennsylvania. Four others (Chicago, Boston, Providence, and New Brunswick) remain balanced.

For house hunters who hold secure jobs and adequate savings, Khan noted that the inventory gap creates rare leverage to negotiate sale prices, demand seller concessions, or walk away from offers entirely.
