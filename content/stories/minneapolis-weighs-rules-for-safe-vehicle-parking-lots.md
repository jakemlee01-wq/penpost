The Minneapolis City Council's Business, Housing & Zoning Committee has scheduled a public hearing for Tuesday, October 20, 2026, at 1:30 p.m. to review rules for safe vehicle camping. Sponsored by Council Members Jason Chavez, Soren Stevenson, Aisha Chughtai, and Aurin Chowdhury, the plan establishes an authorized regulatory category called safe outdoor parking facilities.

The proposal creates legal shelter for people experiencing homelessness who sleep in passenger vehicles. City staff reported that the designated lots are intended to prevent parking tickets, towing, and street displacement, while connecting drivers to case management and housing services. Staff reports note the model draws on similar initiatives in Duluth, Minnesota, and Denver, Colorado.

The proposal relies on two companion measures. Legislative file 2026-00348 establishes licensing standards under Chapter 296 of the Minneapolis Code of Ordinances, and file 2026-00164 updates city zoning code. As reported by Bring Me The News, the zoning amendment advanced to the council after receiving unanimous approval from the Minneapolis City Planning Commission following an April 6, 2026 hearing.

Under the zoning changes, safe outdoor parking would be allowed as an interim use, a temporary zoning permission, across all primary zoning districts, including residential neighborhoods. Participating locations must be lawfully existing off-street parking lots measuring at least 10,000 square feet. The Minneapolis Times reported that the interim use permits would remain valid for up to five years.

Running a site involves strict operational requirements. According to the Minneapolis Times, eligible operators are limited to non-profit entities, government agencies, and religious organizations. Proposed rules require continuous on-site staffing during all operating hours, approved safety and security plans, code-of-conduct rules, and permanent on-site restrooms and hygiene facilities. Lots cannot remain occupied during the day, with mandatory morning departures clearing the property daily.

Neighbors will also receive formal notification before a site opens. The Minneapolis Times reported that under draft Section 296.60, the city must attempt to mail notices to property owners and residents within 300 feet at least 10 days before licensing hearings, while also alerting local neighborhood and business organizations.

The policy arrives after years of municipal study. The Minnesota Daily reported that Minneapolis declared unsheltered homelessness a public health emergency in December 2023, following ongoing debates over encampment evictions and a 2024 study on safe outdoor spaces by the Office of the City Auditor.

Following the committee hearing, the ordinance goes to the full City Council for debate and a final vote, and then to Mayor Jacob Frey for his signature. If approved, the licensing rules are scheduled to take effect on January 1, 2027.
