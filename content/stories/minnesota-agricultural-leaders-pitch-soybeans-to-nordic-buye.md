From Sept. 27 through Oct. 3, 2026, Minnesota Governor Tim Walz led an official state delegation on a week-long trade mission across Denmark, Sweden, and Iceland to strengthen agricultural and commercial ties. According to reporting from Marshall Radio, the trip marked Minnesota's 12th international trade mission across more than 18 countries since 2019.

Minnesota Farm Bureau Federation President Dan Glessing, who farms row crops in Waverly in Wright County, traveled with the group to represent local producers. The agricultural delegation also included Minnesota Farmers Union President Gary Wertish, Minnesota Department of Agriculture Deputy Commissioner Andrea Vaubel, Minnesota Soybean Executive Director Joe Smentek, and Minnesota Soybean Growers Association President Ryan Mackethun, as reported by the Red River Farm Network.

KRWC 1360 AM reported that the delegation prioritized opening overseas channels to match Minnesota's food production capacity with international demand. The effort coincided with fluctuating export numbers. Reporting from The American Quorum noted that Minnesota goods exports rebounded 14 percent to $6.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, following a 13 percent drop in annual exports to $23.5 billion in 2025.

Glessing told the Red River Farm Network that Nordic countries face domestic production deficits. He noted that Denmark grows about 50 percent of what it needs and Sweden is not anywhere near self-sufficient. Glessing stated to KRWC 1360 AM that export markets and overseas trips are critically important to bridge gaps across the ocean when growers produce more food than the domestic market can consume.

Nordic buyers demonstrated strong interest in purchasing Minnesota-grown soybeans and collaborating on feedstocks for sustainable aviation fuel, KRWC 1360 AM reported. To navigate European Union biotech crop restrictions that limit direct food imports, the delegation promoted soybean meal and animal feed ingredients, according to KRWC 1360 AM and the Red River Farm Network.

During meetings, Glessing shared data and technical details with Nordic livestock representatives on using Minnesota soybeans to improve feed rations for European cattle. Glessing described the visit's primary objective as explaining product uses, learning what foreign partners produce, and building ties with potential buyers. He stressed that establishing face-to-face relationships across the Atlantic helps Minnesota producers secure profitable pricing rather than relying solely on saturated domestic channels.

Following the mission, Glessing reported strong optimism about the commercial connections formed abroad, KRWC 1360 AM reported.
