Minnesota election officials accepted 202,252 absentee and mail-in ballots during the first four weeks of voting for the November 3, 2026, general election, according to data from the Minnesota Secretary of State’s Office and reporting by MinneapoliMedia News.

That total reflects a 17.2 percent increase over the same four-week stretch in the 2022 midterm election, when voters had cast 172,520 ballots. Better Minneapolis and MinneapoliMedia News reported the difference represents an increase of 29,732 accepted ballots.

Of the accepted ballots counted through the close of business on October 15, voters cast 80,870 in person and returned 121,382 by mail, based on figures from the Secretary of State's Office reported by KARE 11. Across the state, local election offices distributed 519,813 absentee and mail ballots during that initial month, up from 474,935 distributed during the comparable window in 2022, according to reporting by WCCO.

Absentee voting by mail and envelope-based in-person voting first opened on September 18, MinneapoliMedia News and the Minnesota News Network reported. The four-week milestone coincided with the October 16 rollout of direct balloting, which allows voters to feed completed ballots directly into a tabulator machine.

Under a state statute taking effect this cycle, every Minnesota county must provide at least one designated in-person early voting site equipped with direct tabulators, according to FOX 9 and the Secretary of State’s Office. Paul Linnell, elections director for the Secretary of State’s Office, told FOX 9 that early reports showed successful administration and steady turnout on the first day of direct voting.

In St. Louis Park, municipal election officials reported roughly 400 voters cast ballots on October 16 alone, compared with an average of about 100 people per day during earlier envelope-based voting, according to FOX 9. Irving Thorne, a St. Louis Park resident who spoke with FOX 9, said he decided to vote early to avoid crowds on election day because he already knew who he supported.

Minnesota DFL Party Chair Richard Carlbom stated that 65 additional early voting locations had opened statewide, encouraging residents to take part in the turnout.

In Minneapolis, the early voting center at 908 Hennepin Avenue expanded its daily operating hours, according to Better Minneapolis. The city also scheduled two new early voting sites to open Tuesday, October 20, at the Urban League Twin Cities on Plymouth Avenue North and Bethel Lutheran Church on 17th Avenue South.

Early voting locations across the state have mandatory extended hours before in-person early balloting ends on Monday, November 2. The Secretary of State’s Office confirmed early voting sites must open on Saturday, October 24, from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.; Tuesday, October 27, until 7 p.m.; Saturday, October 31, from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.; Sunday, November 1, from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.; and Monday, November 2, until 5 p.m.

Although the advance voter registration deadline has passed, eligible Minnesotans can still register on-site when voting early or at their precinct on Election Day by showing valid proof of identity and residence, according to state election officials and MinneapoliMedia News.

For voters returning completed ballots, the Secretary of State’s Office noted that absentee ballots delivered by hand must reach local election offices by 5 p.m. on Election Day, November 3. Ballots returned through the mail must arrive by 8 p.m. that evening.
