An initiative called Healing Democracy is sending hundreds of clergy and lay faith leaders across Minnesota to polling sites on Election Day, Nov. 3. The Minnesota Star Tribune reported that these trained volunteers plan to wear fluorescent vests labeled "poll chaplain" outside voting locations to provide a visible, calming, and nonpartisan presence.

Organizers launched the effort to address voter anxieties over misinformation, political polarization, voter intimidation, and federal surveillance. Rev. Kelly Sherman-Conroy stated that election workers care for the voting process, while chaplains care for the people moving through it.

The volunteers are not poll watchers, election judges, or legal observers, organizers stressed to the Star Tribune. They remain outside polling facilities, do not interfere with voting operations, and do not tell anyone how to vote.

The initiative is a joint collaboration organized by the Interfaith Movement Chaplains Network alongside Minnesota Interfaith Power & Light and Multifaith Antiracism, Change & Healing. Organizers announced their final deployment plans following an October faith leaders press conference held in Minneapolis.

While Healing Democracy has trained in-person poll chaplains in Minnesota for the past five years to accompany voters and de-escalate tensions, 2026 brings an expansion into online spaces. For the first time this year, the initiative has trained digital chaplains to monitor social media and online forums during the final stretch of the election. These volunteers aim to de-escalate tensions, foster civil discourse, and steer voters toward verified voting and registration guidance.

According to organizers, the network builds directly on community relationships forged during last winter's federal immigration crackdown, called Operation Metro Surge. The effort also reflects the state's broader history of interfaith movement chaplaincy, which deploys multifaith clergy and spiritual caregivers to public demonstrations, vigils, and moments of civil unrest.

Other faith groups across the state are organizing around the vote as well. Congregations are activating local Community Care Teams to keep spaces open for community connection and de-escalation before, during, and after votes are tallied. In central Minnesota, Diocese of St. Cloud Bishop Patrick Neary and the Minnesota Catholic Conference released 2026 election guides that emphasize voting as a moral and civic responsibility.
