Local health clinics in Todd and Fillmore counties are opening up appointment slots as state health officials urge residents to check their immunization records. On Oct. 15, the Minnesota Department of Health released an urgent public health statement advising all unvaccinated residents to get immunized against measles following two new outbreaks.

State health officials confirmed 25 measles cases since mid-September across two separate, unrelated clusters, according to MPR News. FOX 9 reported that the cases are concentrated in central and southeastern Minnesota, with 19 cases confirmed in Todd County and six in Fillmore County. State health officials confirmed that every person sickened in both clusters was unvaccinated.

The median age of those infected in the recent surge is roughly 7 years old, MPR News reported. While several patients have sought care at emergency rooms, no hospitalizations have occurred in Minnesota from measles so far in 2026.

These recent cases pushed Minnesota's 2026 total to 42 confirmed infections, surpassing the 26 cases recorded in the state throughout all of 2025, according to the Grand Rapids Herald-Review. MDH records and Bring Me The News noted that this rise follows four years between 2019 and 2023 when zero measles cases were documented in Minnesota. In addition to Todd and Fillmore counties, MinneapoliMedia News reported that 2026 cases have appeared in Anoka, Hennepin, Wright, Dakota, and Polk counties.

Beth Parilla, manager of the Vaccine Preventable Diseases Section at MDH, told CBS News that each cluster began identically when an infected traveler brought measles home to local communities with low immunization rates. MinneapoliMedia News reported that neither cluster is connected to ongoing outbreaks across the border in Wisconsin, where CBS Minnesota reported at least 267 confirmed cases this year. Nationally, KARE 11 reported that the CDC and MDH are tracking more than 4,000 confirmed cases across 47 jurisdictions in 2026.

Measles symptoms include a high fever, dry cough, runny nose, watery red eyes, and a rash that typically appears 7 to 14 days after exposure, according to Bring Me The News and MinneapoliMedia News. KARE 11 noted that the virus travels through the air and can linger in a room long after an infected person leaves.

Vaccination rates have slipped in several parts of the state. MPR News and the Grand Rapids Herald-Review reported that roughly 86 percent of Minnesota kindergartners received the measles, mumps, and rubella vaccine for the 2025 to 2026 school year. Non-medical exemptions rose to 6.8 percent, giving Minnesota the third-lowest kindergarten vaccination rate in the country. In Todd County, Health and Human Services Director Jackie Och told CBS News that vaccination rates among children born in 2021 stood at 74.6 percent, but dropped to 61.6 percent for children born in 2023.

In Fillmore County, the cluster doubled from three cases earlier in October to six by mid-month, prompting Fillmore County Public Health to offer MMR vaccination appointments for uninsured and underinsured residents, CBS News and Bring Me The News reported. MDH and KDHL reported that health officials expect case numbers to grow because unvaccinated pockets exist statewide. Parilla told CBS News that stopping transmission requires immunization levels near 100 percent, and FOX 9 reported that MDH is coordinating with county health teams to trace exposures, monitor quarantines, and encourage the two-dose MMR vaccine.
