The Minnesota History Center announced a new exhibition titled “See & Be Seen: The Evolving Power of Portraits.” The installation opens to the public on Saturday, November 7, 2026, and is scheduled to run through June 13, 2027.

The exhibition draws on the Minnesota Historical Society’s fine arts collections spanning nearly 170 years, chronicling portraiture and self-representation from the 1850s to the present. Every displayed portrait maintains a Minnesota connection, incorporating diverse artists, artistic media, and community traditions.

According to the Minnesota Historical Society, the exhibition examines representation and identity to explore how Minnesotans represent themselves and their communities. The society stated that the show highlights stories that “demonstrate the power in being seen, and being seen on your own terms,” whether portraits are “displayed in a frame, tucked in an album, or stored on a phone.”

Reporting from MinneapoliMedia highlighted several specific pieces featured in the collection. These include Leslie Barlow’s 2019 painting “Venessa and Felix,” Rose Smith’s 2008 artwork “Journey to Minnesota,” Dewey Albinson’s 1925 portrait of artist Frances Cranmer Greenman, and Tom Arndt’s 1999 documentary photograph “Two guys Bde Maka Ska.”

On November 7, the museum opens at 10:00 a.m., with dedicated opening-day family and community programming scheduled between 11:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. The program includes hands-on, exhibition-inspired art-making activities designed for visitors of all ages. Time Into Pixels will also provide an interactive on-site photo booth allowing attendees to create their own portraits.

All opening-day portrait activities are included with regular museum admission, and Minnesota Historical Society members receive free entry, MinneapoliMedia reported. Funding for the exhibition is provided by private donors, society members, and the State of Minnesota’s Arts and Cultural Heritage Fund, which voters created through the Legacy Amendment on November 4, 2008.
