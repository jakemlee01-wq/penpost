Minnesota snapped a nine-month streak of a shrinking workforce in September 2026, adding 843 people to reach 3,125,305 workers. The 0.03% increase was small, but it halted the slide, according to data from the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED), Red Lake Nation News, and MPR News.

Even with more people entering the workforce, the monthly job count stumbled. DEED reported that the state unemployment rate rose one-tenth of a percentage point to 4.5%, sitting above the national rate of 4.2%, which also rose by a tenth of a point. Over the month, the number of employed Minnesotans fell by 3,268 while unemployed residents increased by 4,111. Minnesota's labor force participation rate dipped 0.1 percentage point to 66.7%, down from 68.1% a year ago. MPR News reported that state economists are puzzled by the diverging labor trends, as the national participation rate climbed 0.2 points to 61.8%.

Minnesota lost 4,600 nonfarm payroll jobs in September, a 0.1% decline, with private employers trimming 5,000 jobs. Red Lake Nation News and DEED pointed out that the monthly drop contrasted with nationwide payrolls, which grew by 29,000.

Factory floors delivered the strongest hiring. DEED and Red Lake Nation News reported that manufacturing led all expanding supersectors by adding 1,600 jobs, a 0.5% monthly jump that coincided with the state designating October as Manufacturing Month. Red Lake Nation News reported that manufacturing contributed $61 billion to the state economy last year and projects more than 92,000 job openings through 2034.

Four other sectors added jobs over the month, according to DEED: construction gained 1,100 positions, leisure and hospitality added 1,000, government added 400, and financial activities grew by 300. Mining and logging payrolls remained unchanged.

Those gains were outweighed by reductions in five other categories. DEED figures show educational and health services cut 5,700 jobs, professional and business services shed 2,100, and trade, transportation, and utilities fell by 800. Other services dropped 300 jobs, and information lost 100.

Over the broader twelve-month window from September 2025 to September 2026, Minnesota employment expanded by 1.2%, adding more than 35,000 jobs, while private sector employment grew 1.3%. DEED and Minnesota News Network noted that both annual figures beat national growth rates of 0.3% and 0.5%.

Take-home pay, however, failed to keep pace with living costs. DEED and MPR News reported that average hourly earnings in Minnesota were completely flat over the year, while regional inflation measured by the Consumer Price Index reached 3.7%, eroding worker purchasing power. Across the country, wages grew 2.9%. MPR News reported that heavy hiring in lower-paying industries, such as education, healthcare, retail, and hospitality, may be holding down the state's average hourly earnings.

Geographic performance also split sharply. KROC-AM News and DEED reported that the Twin Cities metro was the only metropolitan region in Minnesota to show annual employment growth, adding 22,404 jobs, an increase of 1.1%. Outside the Twin Cities, Duluth-Superior dropped 1,244 jobs, St. Cloud shed nearly 1,150, the Rochester area fell by more than 730 jobs, and Mankato declined by more than 300.

DEED and MPR News noted that Minnesota's total labor force currently stands 1,436 people above its pre-pandemic level in February 2020, when the participation rate was 70%, but remains down by more than 34,000 workers compared to September 2025.
