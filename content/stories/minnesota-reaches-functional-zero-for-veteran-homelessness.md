A military barracks runs on strict checklists, and Minnesota just checked off a housing ledger sixteen years in the making. Gov. Tim Walz announced on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, that Minnesota has reached "functional zero" to effectively end veteran homelessness statewide.

According to the Governor's Office, Minnesota is the fourth state to achieve this designation across all counties, following Connecticut, Delaware, and Virginia, though KARE 11 reported Minnesota as the fifth. The milestone was certified after Ramsey County met the standard on Oct. 2, 2026, completing the benchmark across all 10 Continuum of Care regions, which are regional planning networks that coordinate local homelessness assistance across all 87 Minnesota counties.

Walz gathered for the announcement at Veteran Village in Eagan alongside housing officials and veterans Omari Birts and Todd Smith. State officials told MPR News and KSTP-TV that functional zero does not mean a veteran will never lose housing again. Rather, the state built an infrastructure ensuring episodes of homelessness are rare, brief, and nonrecurring, moving people directly toward permanent shelter instead of temporary cots. Under this model, KSTP-TV reported that any veteran who falls into homelessness is now placed into permanent housing with support services within an average of 90 days.

The timeline shows how the state's approach evolved. Statewide counts recorded more than 600 unhoused veterans in 2010, missing an earlier goal set by former Gov. Tim Pawlenty to end long-term homelessness by that year, according to MPR News and KSTP-TV. Minnesota then adopted a coordinated "housing first" registry framework in 2014, an approach that prioritizes immediate permanent housing before addressing other individual obstacles. As of October 2026, between 100 and 150 veterans remain on the Homeless Veteran Registry, with every individual currently on a pathway to a permanent home.

At the event, Army veteran Todd Smith recounted how moving into a home at Veteran Village enabled him to resolve legal issues, address mental health challenges, and overcome addiction. "It's not something you just say, 'Oh, we fixed it. Veterans' homelessness is over,'" Walz said during the event, adding that the state established a safety net to ensure any loss of housing is brief.

Coordinated state agency programs and partner groups have helped more than 5,000 veterans avoid homelessness since 2019, KARE 11 reported. State funding that fueled the effort included $47 million to construct three new Minnesota Veterans Homes operating in Montevideo, Preston, and Bemidji, which serve nearly 200 veterans. The state and its partners also directed $21 million to buy and rehabilitate 161 permanent supportive housing units, while an omnibus bill in 2023 created the Veterans Supportive Housing Options program to add another 87 supportive units.

Other state programs accompanied the housing drive. KARE 11 reported that expanded mental health services helped lower veteran suicide rates in Minnesota by 20%, while the state distributed $25.7 million in expanded GI Bill benefits, pandemic relief grants to 4,300 veterans, and post-9/11 service bonuses to 26,077 veterans.

Cathy tenBroeke of the Minnesota Interagency Council on Homelessness stated that the data-sharing and housing-first systems used for veterans can act as a template to address broader unsheltered populations across Minnesota. Walz called on state lawmakers to sustain bipartisan funding to keep the housing network operating.
