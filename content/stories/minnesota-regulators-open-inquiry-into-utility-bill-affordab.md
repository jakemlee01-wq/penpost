The Minnesota Public Utilities Commission opened a statewide inquiry into utility bill affordability on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, holding its first public hearing in St. Paul. The session ran from 9:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. at commission offices at 121 7th Place East, Suite 350, with a public livestream available, according to MPR News and the Citizens Utility Board.

Titled "How Customers View Affordability," the opening meeting featured a panel examining how households, agricultural producers, and businesses experience energy expenses. MPR News reported that regulators are studying standardized measurements to define affordability. The non-profit Regulatory Assistance Project was brought in to facilitate the inquiry and lead the informational sessions.

In remarks reported by MPR News, commission chair Katie Sieben said regulators want to gather insights from local and national experts on policies that could offer relief. Sieben noted that the state must consider options from utilities, the governor, the Legislature, and federal officials to keep bills down. In a commission announcement, Sieben added that rising inflation and world events have driven up energy expenses, observing that setting reasonable utility rates does not always guarantee affordable bills for families and businesses.

The examination encompasses Minnesota's rate-regulated electric and natural gas suppliers, as detailed by the Citizens Utility Board. Electric companies under review include Xcel Energy, Minnesota Power, Otter Tail Power, and Dakota Electric Association. Natural gas providers include CenterPoint Energy, Xcel Energy, Minnesota Energy Resources Corporation, Great Plains Natural Gas Company, and Greater Minnesota Gas.

Reporting from the Citizens Utility Board and Clean Heat Minnesota noted significant financial challenges facing residents. Across 2024 and 2025, more than 181,000 Minnesota households faced utility disconnections, including nearly 60,000 natural gas customers. The groups reported that consumers carried between $145 million and $150 million in past-due electric and gas debt into 2026.

As noted in reporting by PowerLines and commission records, long-term capital investments in grid modernization and clean-energy infrastructure have added upward pressure on monthly bills. The commission noted that Minnesota's average residential electric bills ranked eighth lowest in the country in 2024. State regulators also highlighted that they have denied more than $1.5 billion in proposed rate hikes in recent years. In addition, the commission capped executive compensation that can be recovered from ratepayers, prohibited utilities from passing along executive bonuses and lobbying expenses, and expanded low-income discount programs.

The commission will hold follow-up sessions on Monday, November 2, and Tuesday, December 8, 2026, as reported by PowerLines and MPR News. A public comment window remains open for written statements from consumers and businesses. Following the hearings, the commission plans to assemble its findings into a formal report with legislative and administrative recommendations, which will go to the governor and state Legislature in early 2027.
