Annual Medicare open enrollment began on October 15, 2026, and will run through December 7, with plan changes taking effect on January 1, according to reporting from MPR News and KARE 11. Up to 390,000 Minnesota seniors, roughly one in three Medicare recipients in the state, are losing their existing Medicare Advantage coverage or facing major plan restructurings, according to the Minnesota Board on Aging, MPR News, and CBS News Minnesota. The Minnesota Star Tribune reported that more than 320,000 Minnesotans are losing their current Medicare Advantage plans entirely as insurers eliminate options or exit county markets.

Major carriers including Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Minnesota, HealthPartners, UnitedHealthcare, Medica, and Humana are reducing footprints or leaving sections of Minnesota, according to MPR News and Becker's Payer Issues. Becker's Payer Issues reported that the total number of Medicare Advantage plans offered across the state is falling 22 percent, from 68 plans down to 53, leaving 18 rural counties without a single standard Advantage option. Standalone Medicare Part D prescription drug options are also shrinking, dropping from 11 available plans to 9, the Minnesota Star Tribune reported.

Becker's Payer Issues and CareRoute reported that Humana is completely exiting Minnesota's individual Medicare Advantage market, affecting about 9,500 enrollees. Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Minnesota is dropping all but one of its individual Advantage options and pulling out of 15 counties, Becker's and the Minnesota Star Tribune reported, following more than $500 million in Medicare losses over three years tied to higher care and drug costs. Blue Cross chief executive Dana Erickson stated that while the company's financial foundation remains strong, it cannot responsibly continue an unsustainable path of negative performance that could affect its broader membership of three million people.

For seniors who retain plan choices, prices and cost-sharing levels are climbing. Becker's Payer Issues reported that Minnesota will have the second-highest average Medicare Advantage premiums in the nation at $59.40 per month, behind only Wyoming. The Minnesota Star Tribune reported that the median in-network out-of-pocket maximum across state plans is jumping from about $5,000 to $6,900. On some popular Twin Cities metro plans, out-of-pocket maximums are increasing from $3,800 to $5,900, and nearing $10,000 in Hennepin County, CBS News Minnesota reported. The same outlet reported that an illustrative five-night hospital stay will cost $2,250 under newly restructured terms, up from $400 previously.

Seniors whose plans are discontinued will automatically revert to Original Medicare on January 1 if they take no action, CBS News Minnesota and Minnesota Aging Pathways reported. That shift leaves beneficiaries responsible for standard Part A and Part B deductibles and 20 percent uncapped coinsurance, with no Part D prescription drug coverage. Beneficiaries leaving discontinued plans receive a Special Enrollment Period through February 2027, along with guaranteed-issue rights to purchase a Medigap supplemental policy without medical underwriting, according to CareRoute and the Minnesota Star Tribune.

Minnesota Board on Aging Chair Maureen Schneider urged seniors not to delay reviewing their options, noting that state help lines expect record call traffic, MPR News reported. Laurel Heppner told CBS News Minnesota that seniors are confused and distressed after receiving notice that their plans are ending. Charlie Forbush warned via CBS News Minnesota that coverage changes could separate patients from doctors they have visited for a decade. Free guidance is available through the state-supported Minnesota Aging Pathways line at 800-333-2433 on weekdays from 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., or online at Medicare.gov, according to the Minnesota Department of Human Services.
