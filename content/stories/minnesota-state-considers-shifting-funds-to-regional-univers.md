The Minnesota State colleges and universities system is considering a plan to shift funding internally from two-year community and technical colleges to four-year regional universities, according to reporting by MPR News.

Under the proposal, up to $15 million per year would be redirected to stabilize struggling university budgets. The Minnesota State Board of Trustees is reviewing these funding formula adjustments as part of its ongoing budget planning and allocation evaluations for upcoming fiscal years.

The shifted funds would specifically support St. Cloud State University and Bemidji State University. Both institutions have faced persistent budget deficits brought on by multi-year declines in student enrollment.

At St. Cloud State University, administrators have already carried out substantial academic program cutbacks and faculty restructuring during recent budget cycles in an effort to align operating costs with a reduced student headcount.

The broader Minnesota State system spans 54 campuses, encompassing 26 two-year colleges and seven state universities. Leaders and representatives from the two-year colleges have expressed concern over losing financial resources under the proposed reallocation, noting that their institutions are also navigating enrollment challenges and workforce training pressures.
