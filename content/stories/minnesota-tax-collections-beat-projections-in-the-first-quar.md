State budget forecasters usually prefer pleasant surprises, and October delivered one. Minnesota collected $7.624 billion in net general fund revenue during the first quarter of fiscal year 2025, outperforming its February forecast by $234 million, or 3.2 percent.

The extra cushion came largely from paychecks. According to Minnesota Management and Budget, individual income tax withholding led the pack, landing $146 million, or 5.1 percent, ahead of expectations. Business owners helped too, with partnership and S corporation payments beating estimates by $21 million. Meanwhile, state income tax refunds ran $7.3 million lower than anticipated, keeping more cash in state coffers.

Not every line item sparkled. Corporate franchise taxes and general sales tax collections both lagged behind expectations for the quarter, hinting at cautious shoppers and uneven business profits. Think of it as a solid household budget where wages look great, even if the weekend garage sale brought in less than hoped.

The broader economic backdrop is holding steady for now. In the update, Minnesota Management and Budget noted that forecaster S&P Global Market Intelligence raised its near-term outlook, projecting real gross domestic product growth of 2.7 percent for 2024 and 2.1 percent for 2025.
