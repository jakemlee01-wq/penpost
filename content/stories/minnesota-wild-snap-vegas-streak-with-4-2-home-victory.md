A five-minute scoring flurry can change an entire evening before the ice even gets resurfaced. On Monday night, Oct. 12, 2026, the Minnesota Wild jumped out early and held on to defeat the Vegas Golden Knights 4-2 at Grand Casino Arena, halting Vegas' four-game winning streak. The Associated Press and NHL.com reported the victory pushed Minnesota to a 4-1-1 record, while the Golden Knights fell to 5-2-0.

The decisive stretch arrived late in the opening period, when Minnesota produced three goals within 5 minutes and 7 seconds. Matt Boldy initiated the scoring with 8:10 remaining in the first frame by gathering his own rebound off a stopped one-timer and sliding a snap shot past Vegas goalie Carter Hart, according to Yardbarker.

Max Shabanov stretched the lead to 2-0 at 14:32 on a power play, firing a wrist shot from the top of the left circle that went in off Hart's blocker while Joel Eriksson Ek screened the crease, Yardbarker and the Las Vegas Sun reported. Danila Yurov pushed the margin to 3-0 at 16:57, converting a pass across the slot from Bobby Brink. Wild rookie Rieger Lorenz earned his first career NHL assist on Yurov's tally, according to CBS News.

Vegas pushed back during the remaining 40 minutes. Defenseman Rasmus Andersson scored 2:37 into the second period with a long slap shot from near the blue line after Tomas Hertl passed from behind the net, NHL.com reported. Vegas forward Raphael Lavoie picked up the secondary assist for his first career NHL point. William Karlsson later cut Minnesota's advantage to 3-2 with 10:35 left in the third period, scoring on an odd-man rush off a feed from Brett Howden set up by Mitch Marner.

Eriksson Ek sealed the outcome by sliding in an empty-net goal with 1:22 remaining, according to the Associated Press. Wild goaltender Jesper Wallstedt made 21 saves on 23 shots across his sixth straight start of the season, while Hart finished with 24 saves on 27 shots for Vegas.

Special teams gave Minnesota a distinct edge all night. The Associated Press reported that the Wild matched a franchise record by scoring a power-play goal in each of their first six games, lifting their league-leading power play to 7-for-17 (41.2 percent). Minnesota's penalty kill denied all five Vegas opportunities, improving to 21-for-22 on the season, which also ranks first in the NHL. Minnesota outshot Vegas 23-11 through the first two periods, including a 6-1 edge in shots while playing short-handed.

The contest also included roster strain and physical play. CBS News reported that rookie Lorenz dropped his gloves for his first career NHL fight against Vegas defenseman Jeremy Lauzon in the third period. Minnesota played without defenseman Brock Faber, who is sidelined with a skull fracture, as well as forward Nick Foligno on injured reserve. General manager Bill Guerin announced that forward Marcus Foligno was placed on injured reserve with a mid-body injury, leading to the recall of Tyler Pitlick from the Iowa Wild, according to Pro Hockey Rumors. Forward Nico Sturm exited in the first period and did not return after a stray slap shot from teammate Jared Spurgeon hit the right side of his helmet, CBS News noted.

Both teams play again on Tuesday, Oct. 13. The Wild travel to face the Winnipeg Jets, while Vegas heads south to visit the Nashville Predators.
