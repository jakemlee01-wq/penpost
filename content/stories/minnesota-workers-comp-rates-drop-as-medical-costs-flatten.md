Across most of the country, medical costs for workplace injuries kept climbing, but Minnesota held the line in 2025. In 26 other states analyzed by the Workers Compensation Research Institute (WCRI), medical payments per claim rose between 4% and 8% annually from 2022 through 2025. In Minnesota, those same medical payments held flat in 2025 after two straight years of increases.

Those numbers come from the study CompScope Medical Benchmarks for Minnesota, 2026 Edition, authored by Rebecca Yang, which examined non-COVID-19 claims through 2025. Sebastian Negrusa, vice president of research at WCRI, stated that Minnesota was unusual among the benchmarked states for keeping per-claim medical expenses stable in 2025. Negrusa pointed to state fee schedule reforms, shifting claim mixes, and broader economic factors as key reasons for the pause.

The WCRI report specifically credited state rules that held prices in check, notably reforms to Minnesota's hospital outpatient fee schedule that took effect in October 2023. The Legislature followed up with House File 2988, which enacted an additional 4% reduction to hospital outpatient fees on October 1, 2025.

That pause on medical bills is now steering baseline insurance costs lower. The Minnesota Workers' Compensation Insurers Association (MWCIA) presented an overall 4.6% decrease in pure premium base rates (the portion of an insurance rate set aside to pay anticipated claim losses) for 2026 to the state's Workers' Compensation Advisory Council.

An actuarial analysis by Andrea Everling of the MWCIA mapped out that 4.6% net drop. The decline was fueled by a 5.2% reduction in experience-only indications and a 0.5% drop attributed to House File 2988. Those cost reductions offset a 0.4% bump from statewide average weekly wage adjustments and a 0.7% increase in loss adjustment expenses.

The rate decrease continues a longer downward slide. According to the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, the average pure premium rate for 2026 is 34% lower than in 2016 and 44% below 2004 levels. The MWCIA also announced that average premium levels for employers in the Minnesota Workers' Compensation Assigned Risk Plan will drop by 3.8% on January 1, 2027.

Even with flat medical bills, one cost category is still climbing. MWCIA figures show that indemnity severity, which measures wage replacement costs per claim, jumped 24% in Minnesota over the past five years.
