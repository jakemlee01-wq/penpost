Riverbend Freight, a trucking company based in Montevideo, says it will start twice-weekly delivery runs to a dozen smaller towns in western Minnesota next month, including Dawson, Madison, and Appleton. (Demo story; company and details are examples.)

The change is about something called less-than-truckload freight: shipments too big for a parcel service and too small to fill a whole trailer. Think of a few pallets of auto parts, or a replacement walk-in cooler. Small-town businesses often wait longest for this kind of delivery, because carriers bundle routes to save fuel.

Riverbend says it bought four new trucks and hired six drivers to cover the routes. It describes the expansion as a response to customer requests, which is a company claim, not something independently verified.

A predictable delivery day can matter more than a fast one. A hardware store or a farm supply shop can plan inventory around Tuesdays and Fridays. It is harder to plan around "sometime next week."

The comparison is worth keeping modest. This is one carrier adding routes, not a fix for rural freight costs. But for businesses at the edge of the delivery map, steady service is the kind of change that tends to show up slowly, in fewer empty shelves.
