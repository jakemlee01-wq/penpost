Center Avenue and 11th Street in Moorhead reopened to vehicular, bicycle, and pedestrian traffic by early Thursday evening, Oct. 15, 2026, according to reporting by KFGO and the Minnesota Department of Transportation. The opening marks the substantial completion of the $171 million 11th Street Grade Separation project, which the City of Moorhead described as the largest transportation undertaking in municipal history.

The project moved 11th Street beneath two separate BNSF Railway lines, known as the Prosper and KO subdivisions, using newly built rail bridges. Moorhead is split by rail lines carrying roughly 70 freight trains daily. Reporting from KFGO and InForum noted that those trains previously blocked surface crossings for more than five cumulative hours each day, an interruption projected to reach 90 trains and seven hours of stoppage daily by 2045.

Ames Construction managed the primary physical work under a $114.8 million contract after breaking ground in March 2024. Crews laid concrete paving, added pedestrian sidewalks meeting Americans with Disabilities Act standards, and installed modern traffic signals. Below ground, workers replaced aging utility mains and drainage lines, installing a new stormwater lift station, a pumping facility that moves surface water to a higher elevation, at 1st Avenue and 11th Street to pump runoff toward the Red River.

Work unfolded across three distinct phases, according to project records noted by MnDOT and Up North Real Estate. Stage 1 in 2024 covered the rail bridges, First Avenue, and northern 11th Street. Stage 2 in 2025 focused on Main Avenue and 11th Street south. Stage 3 in 2026 finished Center Avenue and the roadway running beneath the bridges. Ames Construction and MnDOT workers will continue final cleanup, punch-list repairs, and finishing work through November 2026, InForum reported.

The grade separation creates a clear route to Moorhead's south side for emergency vehicles. Local officials pointed out immediate operational benefits for nearby emergency headquarters, including the Moorhead Fire Department, the Clay County Sheriff's Office, and the Moorhead Police Department. MnDOT District Engineer Shiloh Wahl told KFGO that the investment will ease daily commutes while improving safety, accessibility, and emergency response times.

Grade-separating the downtown rail corridors had been a local objective since initial planning concepts in the 1970s, according to city officials. Financing drew from several levels of government, including $120 million in state legislative appropriations, $26.3 million in federal grants, $21.5 million from MnDOT, and $3.8 million in local funds from Moorhead and Clay County. State Sen. Rob Kupec stated that a $10 million state bonding allocation during the 2023 legislative session closed a project shortfall, protecting neighboring property owners from special tax assessments.

Local leaders held a formal ribbon-cutting ceremony on Wednesday, Oct. 7, 2026, at 11th Street and Center Avenue, as reported by KFGO and the city. The opening was commemorated with a downtown block party featuring food trucks along with performances by the Pat Lenertz Band and the Swing Barrel Brewery Band.
