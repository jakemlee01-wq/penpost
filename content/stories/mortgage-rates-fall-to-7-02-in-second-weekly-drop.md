Borrowing costs eased for a second straight week as the national average rate for a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage declined to 7.02%, down from 7.09% the week before, according to Freddie Mac.

The benchmark rate had previously reached a 2024 high of 7.22% in early May. During this recent pullback, daily rate trackers recorded the 30-year average briefly slipping under the 7% threshold to 6.99% on Wednesday, May 15.

Shorter-term loans followed the same downward trend. Freddie Mac reported that 15-year fixed-rate mortgages averaged 6.28%, falling from 6.38% the prior week. Financing remains notably higher than it was during the corresponding week in 2023, when the 30-year average stood at 6.39% and the 15-year rate sat at 5.75%.

Even a slight dip delivers critical assistance to shoppers facing a national median home sale price that hit an all-time high of $386,951, according to Dana Anderson. For the rest of the year, Freddie Mac expects mortgage rates to remain elevated, forecasting one Federal Reserve rate cut late in 2024.
