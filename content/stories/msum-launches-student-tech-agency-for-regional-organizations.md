College computer labs usually produce homework, but a new agency at Minnesota State University Moorhead is turning that student coursework into technical support for local neighbors.

On Monday, October 26, 2026, MSUM's Computer Science and Information Systems Department officially launched Dragon Ember Solutions, as reported by AM 1100 The Flag. The student-run agency matches regional small businesses and nonprofit organizations with teams of MSUM students to handle essential, entry-level technical needs.

Dr. Juan Cabanela, a professor in the Computer Science and Information Systems Department, serves as the program coordinator, AM 1100 The Flag and MinneapoliMedia News reported. Cabanela stated to WDAY Radio that the department is thrilled to introduce the agency as a new avenue for community engagement and experiential learning, adding that he looks forward to seeing how the first wave of student teams applies their skills to support local organizations.

Project matching targets three core service areas, according to reporting from WDAY Radio and MinneapoliMedia News: web support, basic data administration and migration, and IT documentation and assessments. MinneapoliMedia News reported that specific tasks assigned to students include troubleshooting websites, updating web pages, cleaning up spreadsheets, reformatting data, compiling basic reports, taking technology inventories, and conducting hardware or software assessments.

Rather than serving as an urgent professional IT contractor, the agency operates as a supervised learning lab, according to WDAY Radio and MinneapoliMedia News. Faculty members oversee all student work on a "best-effort" educational basis, tailoring client deliverables to the evolving skills of the students handling the job.

MinneapoliMedia News reported that client applications for the upcoming spring term are due by 5:00 p.m. on Friday, November 20. Selected projects for this inaugural cohort will run across the spring semester, from mid-January through early May, according to WDAY Radio and MinneapoliMedia News. AM 1100 The Flag and WDAY Radio noted that client capacity for this first spring cohort will be strictly limited due to the pilot nature of the rollout.
