The University of Minnesota Medical School and CentraCare have partnered to launch a new, one-year advanced family medicine obstetrics fellowship. The initiative is designed to train family physicians to handle high-risk and complicated deliveries, with the explicit goal of preventing labor and delivery units from closing across Greater Minnesota.

As reported by MPR News and KNSI, the program will select two board-certified family medicine physicians each year. The inaugural cohort of two fellows is scheduled to begin clinical rotations in July 2027.

Training will take place across central Minnesota at CentraCare St. Cloud Hospital and affiliated rural critical access hospitals, which are small facilities certified to offer essential local inpatient care. Fellows will train alongside obstetrician-gynecologists and family physicians. The curriculum focuses on high-volume surgical obstetrics, requiring each fellow to perform 100 or more cesarean sections as primary surgeon. Rotations also cover neonatal resuscitation, high-risk obstetric services, neonatal intensive care, and perinatal mental health.

Physicians involved in the program pointed to persistent workforce constraints in smaller communities. Program director Dr. Christine Goudge stated that the state urgently needs to train more doctors who can provide maternity, pregnancy, and postpartum care in its smallest towns. Dr. Goudge noted that while adding two skilled physicians each year may not be the entire solution, it represents an important step toward stabilizing rural maternity staffing needs.

Dr. Keri Bergeson explained that small hospitals delivering roughly 100 babies per year cannot sustain full-time dedicated obstetrics and gynecology staffs. In comments cited by CentraCare and MPR News, Dr. Bergeson emphasized that versatile family practitioners are essential as rural communities confront disappearing health services and seek to keep high-quality care close to home.

The partnership addresses a documented decline in rural labor wards. Research from the University of Minnesota Rural Health Research Center found that 29 hospitals across Minnesota eliminated their obstetric services between 2010 and 2024. Reporting by MPR News and KNSI noted that only 56 percent of hospitals statewide still maintain active labor and delivery units.

That loss of care affects a wide geographic area. University of Minnesota Rural Health Research Center data shows that 37 of Minnesota’s 87 counties currently have no hospital-based obstetric services. That leaves roughly 700,000 residents living in counties without local hospital birth options.

Before this partnership, the state’s only other rural obstetrics fellowship was based at Essentia Health in Duluth, which also operates in partnership with the University of Minnesota, according to MPR News and Wisevoter.

The new fellowship also ties into a broader academic expansion in central Minnesota. In summer 2025, the University of Minnesota Medical School and CentraCare are launching a new regional campus in St. Cloud. That facility marks the medical school's first regional campus expansion in more than 50 years, following the opening of its Duluth campus in 1972.
