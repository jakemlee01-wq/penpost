The New Ulm Career and Technical Education Center has been selected to receive a $300,000 corporate grant from 3M to renovate and modernize its classroom and lab spaces.

The award was presented to city leaders on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, during a regular meeting of the New Ulm Economic Development Authority, as reported by The Journal. The grant is part of the 3M Community Impact Future Makers Lab initiative, which selects only four communities nationwide each year.

The program operates in partnership with the nonprofit organization Heart of America, according to program materials from 3M and Heart of America. The initiative updates learning environments in communities near 3M facilities, adding flexible workstations, updated building infrastructure, and equipment for robotics, mechatronics, and advanced manufacturing.

In New Ulm, the funding will supply updated design tooling, machining equipment, and fabrication tools to match current manufacturing standards. Construction on the transformed lab space is currently underway.

Once the work is completed, New Ulm High School will move its "Intro to Trades" course directly into the center. Steve Brown, chief executive of the New Ulm Business Resource Innovation Center, stated at the meeting that the facility's grand opening is scheduled for November 2026, and that 3M's chief executive plans to attend.

Local economic development board members noted that the investment reinforces public-private partnerships designed to address rural labor shortages in Brown County. The center offers semester-long elective courses and industry credentials to students from New Ulm High School and surrounding rural school districts.

According to records from GreenSeam and the ISD 88 Foundation, the technical center first opened in August 2021 under New Ulm Public Schools. Located in the former Windings building at 208 North Valley Street, the facility was established to provide student training in welding, automotive repair, carpentry, machining, and manufacturing.
