County officials from Itasca, St. Louis, and Aitkin counties have partnered with representatives from the Leech Lake Band of Ojibwe to create a unified 10-year regional economic development plan.

The group held its first planning meeting on October 7, 2026, in Grand Rapids, Minnesota. As reported by KAXE, Itasca County Commissioner John Johnson spearheaded the collaborative gathering. Details on the group's agreed priorities were subsequently announced on October 19, 2026.

More than 20 organizations sent staff to take part in the Grand Rapids session. Participants included regional electric utility Minnesota Power and the Department of Iron Range Resources and Rehabilitation, a state agency.

The regional effort was organized after several major industrial and economic development projects in northeastern Minnesota faced delays or fell through in recent years. KAXE reported that those lost opportunities prompted local leaders to coordinate their efforts rather than compete against each other for incoming investments.

Attendees settled on three core priorities to anchor the decade-long strategy: reforming environmental and regulatory permitting, expanding public-private partnerships, and strengthening local workforces. A public-private partnership is a formal collaboration between government agencies and private companies to finance or run community projects.

Facilitators Dave Webb of the consulting firm Homerun Leadership and Flaherty & Hood, a St. Paul-based policy and legislative law firm, guided the planning discussions.

Derrick Miedaner stated that the multi-county effort is designed to integrate local economies and figure out ways neighboring counties can help one another rather than compete. 

Jonathan White described the tribe's participation as a natural collaboration with Itasca County and Commissioner Johnson. White noted that it feels like a good thing to build partnerships where the relationship historically was not great, and stated that being part of the broader regional effort feels awesome, according to KAXE.

Working with their facilitators, the coalition is now using the initial priorities established at the Grand Rapids meeting to draft actionable objectives and shared policy positions for the 10-year roadmap.
