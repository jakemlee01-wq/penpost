Hearing aids and wearable sensors could soon run on microbatteries printed in Oakdale that pack twice the power into tiny spaces.

The Minneapolis / St. Paul Business Journal reported that local hardware startup Motif Materials has engineered a 3D-printed microbattery designed to provide roughly double the power density of traditional lithium batteries. Power density measures how much energy a battery holds relative to its size.

To back the design, the Oakdale-based startup is working to raise $1.7 million in an initial pre-seed funding round. The team plans to use that money to finance a 12-month proof-of-concept initiative and validate performance data for potential commercial clients.

Motif Materials is focusing first on hearing aids before expanding into wearable sensors and implantable medical devices. Hearing aids face lower regulatory hurdles than other medical equipment, but their designs have long been limited by standard power cells. Chief Operating Officer Zeiter Farah noted that the shape of hearing aids has been dominated for years by the shape of the battery.

The startup is led by six co-founders, including Farah, Chief Executive Officer John Lees, and Vice President of Business Development Gary Johnson. Four of the six co-founders worked together at Minnetonka-based additive manufacturer Evolve Additive Solutions, a 2018 spinoff of 3D-printing firm Stratasys Ltd. that wound down operations in September 2026.

The concept took shape after Lees and Farah attended the MD&M Midwest manufacturing trade show in October 2024 to gather industry feedback. At the event, Johnson introduced them to co-founder Brian Hayden, a retired chemistry professor and former founder and chief scientific officer of U.K.-based solid-state battery maker Ilika. Hayden was preparing to retire and move to Minnesota at the time.

The partners officially founded Motif Materials in July 2025 and submitted core patent applications later that year. Describing the process, Farah stated that partnering with Hayden brought together deep battery knowledge with additive manufacturing, laser printing, and electrophotography.

Inside Motif's workspace, prototype 3D printers build substrate layers and electrode components. Lees stated that the equipment can produce 200 sets of electrodes for 200 batteries in about half an hour.

Conventional lithium-ion battery manufacturing typically requires huge factories and overseas supply chains that are largely concentrated in Asia. Motif projects it can set up a vertically integrated domestic microbattery facility for about $5 million. Lees noted that this lower-cost model will allow the company to produce high volumes of batteries.

Lees also noted that early customer conversations are already underway. He stated that Hayden's background in validating battery technology helps the startup know how to test its cells and understand what data commercial buyers expect to see.

Alongside raising its $1.7 million pre-seed round, the Motif Materials team plans to showcase its microbattery technology directly to prospective clients and investors at the MD&M Midwest trade show in October 2026.
