Community Pathways of Steele County and the local Owatonna Knights of Columbus have opened their annual winter coat collection and distribution, with organizers expecting to make between 1,400 and 1,500 coats available to area families ahead of the coming winter.

As reported by the Owatonna People’s Press and Southernminn.com, the expanded effort follows a previous winter season in which the charity handed out more than 1,200 new and gently used coats.

The distribution opened Monday evening at the Community Pathways building at 155 Oakdale St. in Owatonna with a dedicated session for children's outerwear. Knights of Columbus volunteer Dean Schuette estimated that roughly 170 brand-new children’s coats were prepared for the event, secured through the national KC Coats for Kids program.

To take part in the Monday giveaway, recipients needed to be Steele County residents and registered shoppers with Community Pathways, Executive Director Dom Korbel explained. Qualifying families could pick up one coat per child and up to three per household at no charge, without using up any of their monthly clothing stipend, an allowance provided to registered clients for store goods. Parents were encouraged to bring their children to ensure a proper fit, though children did not need to be present if they were already listed on the family’s registered account.

Organizers relied on community spaces and volunteers to store donations ahead of the distribution. Schuette and his wife kept around 100 boxes and tubs of coats at their home over the summer so donations would not be turned away. Year-round storage was also supported by the newly opened Oak Hills Community Connections. In the days before Monday's event, Schuette reported that donation bins placed at two local Catholic churches were overflowing before the final collection run.

Beginning Tuesday, donated coats for all ages will be stocked on the racks at the Unique Finds thrift store. Korbel stated that shoppers can buy them for $5, paid either in cash or with their monthly shopping stipend.

Supply gaps remain for other cold-weather items. Korbel stressed an ongoing shortage of gloves, mittens, winter boots, and children's snow pants, noting that local elementary schools require snow pants for outdoor recess.

Schuette stated that many families have no other place to turn for winter clothing and are grateful to receive coats at low or no cost. Korbel noted that people need help, and resources provided through the community and the Knights of Columbus help make their lives better.
