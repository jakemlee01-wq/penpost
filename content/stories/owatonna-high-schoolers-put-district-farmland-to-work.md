The Owatonna School District is using roughly 30 acres of district-owned farmland along Turtle Creek for student instruction, according to reporting from Southernminn.com and the Owatonna People's Press.

For the past four years, Owatonna High School agriculture instructor and Future Farmers of America adviser Liz Tinaglia has directed operations on a half-acre garden plot at the site. The outdoor program provides hands-on farming and soil-management lessons for high school students in grades 9 through 12 who are enrolled in Tinaglia's greenhouse and introductory plant science courses. Tinaglia reported that the plot serves as a working outdoor lab where students gather soil samples, conduct soil tests, plan plots, till, plant, and weed.

The district stated the project is intended to directly train and inspire the region's next generation of agricultural professionals. On the half-acre parcel, students grow crops completely free of pesticides and herbicides. Past harvests have produced pumpkins, ornamental corn, tomatoes, and peppers. Produce from the plot has previously been sold through student-run sales, and any unsold items were given away to the community.

The remaining 29 acres of the property are sublet to the Owatonna FFA chapter, Southernminn.com and the Owatonna People's Press reported. A local partner farmer carries out custom planting and harvesting on that acreage, raising primarily corn and soybeans on behalf of the school. That farmer also works directly with students in the field, teaching them about combine operations, crop yields, irrigation, and grain marketing.

The 29 commercial acres generate roughly $30,000 per year after operating expenses. All of that revenue goes into a dedicated reserve named the "Our Farm" account, which the district keeps separate from general FFA chapter funds. The money is being saved to build a dedicated, permanent Owatonna High School student farm, and district staff are currently in active planning and discussions with the Steele County Free Fair.
