Stillwater native Tim Brown took first place in the annual Pumpkin Regatta on the St. Croix River, securing his third regatta title during the 21st annual Stillwater Harvest Fest, according to reports from MPR News, WCCO, and KLFD Radio. Brown has raced in the event every year for the past decade. MPR News reported that Brown, who thinks his first victory came in 2016, noted that winning in his mid-40s is pretty cool.

Brown faced three other competitors in head-to-head river heats: Hannah Brown, Laura Fairchild, and his cousin Matthew Schmidt, who is a former regatta winner. MPR News reported that Brown joked his top goal was simply beating his cousin. Brown also commented on the difficulty of navigating the water in a vegetable, stating that it is not as easy as it looks and questioning who would have thought a pumpkin could float and be paddled.

First-time racer Laura Fairchild entered the river competition to honor the memory of her son, U.S. Army veteran Justin Ross Fairchild, who died in 2007, MPR News reported. To prepare the vessels, festival organizers hollowed out massive gourds weighing hundreds of pounds each to turn them into functional boats. Stillwater Harvest Fest organizers scheduled a Sunday noon contest called the Battle to Paddle, where racers competed to win first choice of the hollowed-out gourds before launching onto the river around 2:30 p.m. Race organizers told MPR News, WCCO, and KLFD Radio that mild fall weather aided the paddlers and the hundreds of spectators lining the riverbanks.

The weekend regatta was held alongside giant pumpkin weigh-offs and heavy-equipment drops at Lowell Park. At 5:30 p.m. on both Saturday and Sunday, massive pumpkins were hoisted by a 100-foot crane and dropped onto targets below, smashing in front of festival spectators.

In Saturday's Great Pumpkin Commonwealth-sanctioned weigh-off, Jim Ford of Eldorado, Wisconsin, won first place with a 2,491-pound pumpkin, according to BigPumpkins.com. Shannon Leer of Whitehall, Wisconsin, took second place with a 2,263-pound entry, and Charlie Bernstrom of Lancaster, Minnesota, placed third with a gourd weighing 2,228 pounds. MPR News, WCCO, and KLFD Radio reported that the largest competition gourd displayed at the festival weighed 2,305 pounds.

Cassie McLemore told MPR News that the event began strictly as a pumpkin weigh-off more than two decades ago before expanding into a multi-day regional festival. McLemore noted that the festival earned global recognition for the heaviest top-10 combined pumpkin weigh-off in 2015, 2018, and 2023.

According to festival organizers, the free two-day community gathering also featured live bluegrass music, a Ferris wheel overlooking the river, a chili cook-off, an artisan market, a Sunday children's costume parade, and a beer garden sponsored by the Bayport Legion.
