Paperback Exchange spent nearly half a century near 50th Street and Penn Avenue South before a ruptured pipe wrecked its home. The Minneapolis used bookstore finalized the purchase of an existing single-family house at 5000 Oliver Avenue South to serve as its permanent storefront, roughly 100 feet from its former location at 2227 W. 50th Street, according to reporting from Axios Twin Cities, KARE 11, and CBS News.

The purchase comes 20 months after disaster struck the shop, which was founded in 1975. On February 13, 2025, a City of Minneapolis water main ruptured beneath the street, sending water rushing under the sidewalk and blowing inward through a basement wall, as KARE 11 reported. The flood destroyed roughly 100,000 books, which were largely stored in the basement. That wiped out between 70% and 80% of an inventory that had topped 150,000 volumes, according to Axios Twin Cities and WCCO.

Private insurance covered only a small fraction of the shop's financial losses. Meanwhile, the City of Minneapolis claimed municipal immunity, stated it was not liable for the damage, and paid no restitution, WCCO and CBS News reported. An ongoing community GoFundMe campaign helped close the gap by raising more than $62,000, which kept the business afloat and helped finance the residential property purchase.

Without a permanent storefront for 20 months, owner Hersey sustained operations through pop-up sales at local book fairs, CBS News and KARE 11 reported. Those road trips included the Bloodcurdling Book Bash in Bloomington in October 2026 and the Rain Taxi Twin Cities Book Festival in St. Paul in November 2026.

The real estate opportunity arrived through a personal connection. A friend visited after the disaster and mentioned she was planning to sell her Oliver Avenue house, wanting to help and believing the bookstore would be great for the neighborhood, according to CBS News and KARE 11. Hersey told CBS News that they wanted to keep the store in the neighborhood, could not have asked for a closer spot, and found hope in the immediate community support.

The interior of the converted house offers square footage comparable to the old commercial storefront, Axios Twin Cities reported. Paperback Exchange will also retain its signature trade model, allowing readers to exchange used paperbacks for store credit. Crews are currently renovating the residential structure to convert it into a retail shop and improve accessibility. As reported by WCCO, CBS News, and Axios Twin Cities, the bookstore plans to reopen in spring 2027, timed for Independent Bookstore Day in April.
