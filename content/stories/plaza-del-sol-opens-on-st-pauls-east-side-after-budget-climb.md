Plaza del Sol has launched soft opening operations at 990 Payne Ave. on St. Paul’s East Side, converting a 1910 building into a multi-use Latino food hall, commissary kitchen, and event center. The Minneapolis / St. Paul Business Journal reported that the final cost for the facility reached $11 million, rising from an earlier projected budget of $7 million.

The project was spearheaded by the Latino Economic Development Center (LEDC), a St. Paul-based nonprofit organization that purchased the former furniture store out of foreclosure. KARE 11 reported that LEDC is moving its central headquarters into the Payne Avenue property and leasing additional office suites to other community nonprofits. The organization built the venue to operate as a cultural anchor, an event space, and an incubator for culinary entrepreneurs.

The layout includes a ground-floor food hall with two full-kitchen restaurant spaces and three turnkey stalls, according to KARE 11 and the Plaza del Sol website. The building houses a 24/7 shared commercial commissary kitchen for caterers and food truck operators, along with an upper-level event hall that accommodates 250 to more than 300 guests. The event space includes a dance floor built using salvaged wood from the original 1910 structure.

Several food and drink businesses have already begun serving customers during limited soft-launch hours. Bien Dorados, owned by Medardo Gallardo Leon and Isaac Josue Mendez Coyote, expanded into the space after starting at the Currie Kitchen ghost kitchen in Minneapolis in 2024. The Business Journal reported that Bien Dorados serves quesabirria tacos, birria pizza, mulitas, crunchwraps, taquitos dorados, corn ribs, chilaquiles, burritos, and churros. The owners told the publication that the location fits their vision for gathering entrepreneurs and community, and noted their interest in serving late-night diners.

Husband-and-wife team Heriberto Gonzalez and Erika Garcia opened two concepts inside the hall. Valentina’s serves Mexican-Italian-American fusion, including build-your-own pasta, chilaquiles, wings, and basket tacos. Their second business, La Moreliana, serves Mexican ice cream, paletas, and crepes. Gonzalez and Garcia told the Business Journal that they take the most pride in building a family business that represents their culture, passion, and hard work.

The building also includes an on-site bar named Uno Dos Tres. Deli Cheese Coffee, LLC is scheduled to open later in the month with coffee and breakfast service. LEDC and the Business Journal reported that once Deli Cheese Coffee opens, only one small food stall will remain available for lease.

The project reached its opening following recruiting disruptions earlier in the year. The Business Journal and KARE 11 reported that LEDC originally received inquiries from more than 50 prospective businesses, but federal immigration enforcement under Operation Metro Surge caused many immigrant entrepreneurs to go into hiding. LEDC Executive Director Alma Flores briefly weighed opening vendor spots to non-Latino operators before deciding to maintain the focus on supporting Latino business owners.

Plaza del Sol is gradually widening its hours and menus as operations ramp up. According to LEDC and the Community Reporter, the venue will host the Minnesota Public Relations Society of America DEI Forum on Monday, Oct. 26, featuring Flores as keynote speaker, before holding official grand opening celebrations.
