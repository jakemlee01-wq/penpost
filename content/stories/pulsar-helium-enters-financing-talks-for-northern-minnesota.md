Deep underground near Babbitt, gas reservoirs hold rare helium that could soon supply hospital scanners and advanced electronics. To process that gas on a commercial scale, Pulsar Helium Inc. has entered the active financing phase for its planned Rare Gas Hub in northeastern Minnesota.

The Mesabi Tribune reported that the company opened discussions to assemble private and public funding packages. Pulsar is advancing talks with the Eveleth-based Minnesota Department of Iron Range Resources and Rehabilitation, known as the IRRR, for low-interest direct financing or bank-participation support. The prospective site sits inside the agency's designated Taconite Assistance Area, a regional economic zone funded by taconite production taxes paid by northeastern Minnesota iron mining operations.

The Mesabi Tribune also reported that parallel financing discussions are underway with the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development, Michigan-based University Bank, and USDA Rural Development. Pulsar confirmed that the location qualifies for the USDA's Business & Industry Guaranteed Loan Program, which provides federal guarantees to commercial lenders.

Pulsar Chief Executive Officer Thomas Abraham-James said the company's preferred strategy is to secure debt and public financing alongside existing cash to avoid issuing new shares, which would dilute the ownership stake of current investors. Pulsar reported holding about $24.6 million in unaudited cash reserves as of the announcement, with part of that reserve allocated toward project development. Reports from GlobeNewswire and ChemAnalyst noted that discussions with lenders and public agencies remain non-binding, with credit approvals and formal underwriting still pending.

The hub is planned to process gas from Pulsar's Topaz project in Lake County. Tests there showed helium concentrations averaging 8.1 percent, with historical tests reaching 10.5 percent, alongside rare helium-3. The project aims to serve domestic demand in medical imaging, aerospace, semiconductor manufacturing, and quantum computing. IRRR Commissioner Ida Rukavina said northern Minnesota's resources have long powered national industry, noting that developing the resource could diversify the regional economy and create local jobs.

According to reporting from gasworld and Kalkine Media, the financing package supports an $85.5 million equipment-supply deal signed with Chart Energy & Chemicals, a subsidiary of Baker Hughes. Pulsar made an initial payment of roughly $5.025 million, with a second milestone payment of $8.55 million scheduled for January 31, 2027, depending on financing availability.

Once that payment is made, Chart will begin procuring specialized equipment over an estimated two-year period. While that work unfolds, Pulsar is carrying out site and utility due diligence, picking an engineering, procurement, and construction contractor, and working through Minnesota Department of Natural Resources permitting toward a final financial close.

Reports from Kalkine Media and gasworld stated that the plant is engineered to liquefy 861 liters of helium per hour, or roughly 7.5 million liters annually at 99.999 percent purity. The design also features a carbon dioxide capture system designed to process 300 metric tonnes per day.
