On Sunday night, Quinn Hughes stepped onto the ice at Rogers Arena in Vancouver wearing a Minnesota Wild sweater for the first time. By the end of the evening, the defenseman had reached 500 career points and secured a 4-2 victory over the Vancouver Canucks, the club he previously captained.

According to reporting from the Associated Press, TSN, and The Minnesota Star Tribune, the Vancouver crowd heavily booed Hughes during pregame warmups, the national anthem, and whenever he touched the puck. Bring Me the Sports reported that fans chanted "He's not signing." During a first-period video tribute, Hughes also received a mix of cheers and a partial standing ovation. Hughes spent six and a half seasons with Vancouver before being traded to Minnesota on Dec. 12, 2025, in exchange for forwards Marco Rossi and Liam Öhgren, defenseman Zeev Buium, and a 2026 first-round draft pick.

The milestone arrived 12 minutes and 55 seconds into the second period during a power play, a game situation where a team has a skater advantage due to an opponent's penalty. As detailed by NHL.com and Field Level Media, Hughes started the play behind Vancouver's net by feeding a pass to Joel Eriksson Ek in the low slot. Hughes then skated around to the right circle and swept the rebound into an open net, giving Minnesota a 3-0 lead. The goal stood as the game-winner and marked his third goal of the season.

With that play, Hughes reached 500 career regular-season points in his 520th game. NHL.com and Field Level Media reported that he became the seventh-fastest defenseman in NHL history to reach the mark, and the second-fastest American-born defenseman, trailing only Brian Leetch, who reached it in 496 games.

Minnesota had built its early lead with a first-period power-play goal from Maxim Shabanov, followed by Bobby Brink's first goal of the season in the second period off a pass from Michael McCarron, according to The Canadian Press and Field Level Media. In the third period, Hughes assisted Kirill Kaprizov at 3:38 for Kaprizov's league-leading 13th goal of the season. That assist brought Hughes to 501 career points, with 69 goals and 432 assists. Through 13 games this season, Hughes has 16 points.

Vancouver answered later in the third period with goals from Max Sasson and Brock Boeser, but could not close the gap. The Canadian Press and the Associated Press reported that the loss was Vancouver's 10th consecutive defeat, dropping the Canucks to a 2-10-1 record. Wild backup goaltender Calvin Pickard stopped 15 of 17 shots in his second start of the season to earn his first victory of the year. Canucks goaltender Leevi Merilainen made 30 saves.

"The passion with the fanbase is what makes it special to play here," Hughes told NHL.com after the game. "So for me, it was cool to be back here, and exciting. And I'm glad we got the win." Hughes also noted that Vancouver is a "competitive market" and told teammates the win "meant a lot," according to Bring Me the Sports. Wild President of Hockey Operations and General Manager Bill Guerin praised Hughes to The Minnesota Star Tribune, stating, "He's better than I thought he was. When you get him and then you start seeing him every night and then you see him in practice, everything that he does... there's so many little things he does."

Field Level Media and Bring Me the Sports reported that the victory completed a weekend road sweep following a 5-1 win over the Seattle Kraken on Saturday. The outcome improved Minnesota to 8-3-2 and placed the team in first place in the Central Division, two points ahead of the Utah Mammoth. The Associated Press noted that the Wild travel to Salt Lake City to play the Mammoth on Tuesday, while the Canucks visit the Anaheim Ducks.
