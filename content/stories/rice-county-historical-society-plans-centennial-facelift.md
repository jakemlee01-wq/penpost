A century after its founding, the Rice County Historical Society Museum in Faribault is preparing for an extensive internal makeover, the Faribault Daily News reported. The museum is celebrating its 100th anniversary through December 2026 before closing its doors from December 2026 until April 2027 to finish construction.

The historical society was established in 1926 through a merger between the Old Settlers Association of Rice County and the Daughters of the American Revolution. The organization initially operated out of the Rice County Courthouse, moved into Buckham Memorial Library in 1930, and relocated to North Alexander Park in 1976, marking 50 years at that location in 2026. Beyond its park museum, the society maintains eight other historic properties in Faribault, including seven buildings on the Rice County Fairgrounds and the 1853 Alexander Faribault House at 12 First Ave. NE.

The planned renovation will modernize the main museum gallery, staff offices, and the gift shop. Architectural updates will create dedicated room for five rotating exhibits, alongside a permanent gallery for traveling collections from partners like the Smithsonian Institution, the Minnesota Library Association, and the Minnesota Historical Society. Executive Director Dave Nichols told the Faribault Daily News that the board and organization are pleased to finally carry out changes they have wanted for a long time.

The new layout will incorporate expanded hands-on educational stations for visiting children. Nichols confirmed the remodel will also allow the society to exhibit pieces from its 120,000-item permanent collection that currently cannot fit on the display floor. A Minnesota Historical Society fur trade exhibit will be the first traveling installation after reopening, followed the next year by a Department of Natural Resources exhibit partnered with the River Bend Nature Center.

Centennial projects also reach into local kitchens with a commemorative cookbook filled with regional recipes from across Rice County. Meanwhile, the society has raised about $170,000 toward a $200,000 centennial endowment campaign. An endowment works like a community savings nest egg, an invested fund that produces annual operating returns. Reaching the $200,000 finish line will unlock a dollar-for-dollar match from a partner donor group, delivering between $20,000 and $25,000 in yearly operating revenue.

The society will mark the full milestone during its Centennial Annual Meeting on October 22, 2026. Scheduled from 5 to 7 p.m. at The Inn at Shattuck-St. Mary's, the evening includes dinner, a business meeting, and a 100-year retrospective.
