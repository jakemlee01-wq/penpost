rms Company has begun an $11 million expansion of its Coon Rapids manufacturing facility, expanding its production capacity and installing advanced machinery.

According to reporting from HometownSource and the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED), the project adds a 20,000-square-foot manufacturing addition to the southeast side of the existing building at 8600 Evergreen Blvd. in the Evergreen Business Park. It also includes purchases of new machinery and equipment. Coon Rapids Community Development Director Matt Brown confirmed that construction work is already underway.

The expansion is projected to create at least 75 new jobs over the next two to three years, or within three years of the project's completion, according to DEED and city documents. The company manufactures precision-machined components used primarily in implantable medical devices. A subsidiary of Elk River-based Cretex Companies Inc., rms has operated at the site since 1992 and serves as the largest private employer in Coon Rapids, employing more than 1,100 people across a 335,000-square-foot campus. The facility previously underwent expansions in 1998, 2006, 2011, and 2016.

State economic development programs are providing $1.4 million in assistance for the project. That total includes an $800,000 allocation from the Job Creation Fund, which will be disbursed directly to rms as jobs are created and retained. The state also awarded a $600,000 five-year, zero-interest forgivable loan through the Minnesota Investment Fund. That loan will pass through the city and is contingent on rms meeting specified wage and job targets.

The Coon Rapids City Council voted unanimously on Oct. 20 to approve the formal grant agreement with DEED to accept and pass the state funds through to the company. HometownSource reported that Council Member Brian Armstrong stated that rms has been a great business partner and brought a lot of jobs to the city, while Council Member Brad Greskowiak stated that rms has been a great employer in the community and contributes heavily to the local economy.

To satisfy state matching requirements, Coon Rapids plans to provide a $100,000 forgivable loan from the city Housing and Redevelopment Authority commercial-industrial revolving fund, with terms mirroring the state investment loan. According to Brown, the city council is scheduled to hold a required public hearing and formally review the city loan documents within the next two months.
