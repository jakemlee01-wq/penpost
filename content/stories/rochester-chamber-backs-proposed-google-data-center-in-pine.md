The Rochester Area Chamber of Commerce announced its formal backing on Tuesday, Oct. 6, 2026, for Project Skyway, a proposed large-scale Google data center development in Pine Island, as reported by the Post Bulletin and MPR News. The chamber is the second-largest chamber of commerce in Minnesota and represents employers throughout Olmsted County, according to MPR News.

As reported by KTTC and the Post Bulletin, Minneapolis-based Ryan Companies is spearheading development on a 482-acre parcel located just north of Pine Island, about 15 miles north of Rochester. Google is partnered on the proposed $1 billion facility as an anchor tenant and initial investor. The technology campus is planned to span roughly 3 million square feet across up to three stories, with Google initially occupying roughly 88 to 100 acres.

Chamber President Ryan Parsons projected that construction will generate roughly 700 skilled-trades building jobs in the near term and establish a little over 100 permanent, full-time positions once operational, according to MPR News and KROC-AM. Parsons described the development as an outstanding opportunity for the community. MPR News reported that the facility would make Google the second-largest employer in Pine Island, behind only the Land O'Lakes dairy processing plant.

KROC-AM reported the chamber's projection that the development will generate about $130 million in city property tax revenue over its first 28 years of operation, along with more than $24 million directed to Pine Island Public Schools. Jonathon Krull, the chamber's vice president of public affairs, told KTTC that the capital investment will provide regional tax base growth, employment, and infrastructure upgrades extending well beyond city borders.

Power for the site is slated to come from Xcel Energy under a Clean Energy Accelerator Charge model. Google and Xcel have outlined plans to add 1,400 megawatts of wind, 200 megawatts of solar, and 300 megawatts of Form Energy iron-air battery storage to the power grid, alongside $50 million from Google to support Xcel's Capacity*Connect program. Engineering filings estimate direct water consumption for the proposed facility at approximately 152,000 gallons per day.

The chamber's endorsement arrives during intense local debate. MPR News and KROC-AM reported that area residents formed opposition groups and placed "NO DATA CENTER" signs on lawns across Pine Island, raising concerns over groundwater depletion, energy demands, and noise. Construction had originally been scheduled to break ground in July 2026, according to MPR News.

On May 22, 2026, Goodhue County District Court Judge Patrick Biren issued a temporary restraining order that halted all site work, MPR News reported. That court order followed a lawsuit by the Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy against the city and Ryan Companies, alleging the environmental review was inadequate. The project remains paused while the district court reviews the environmental litigation, with formal municipal development filings and city permitting still required before construction can proceed.
