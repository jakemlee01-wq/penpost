People stopping by Pinnacle Catering in southwest Rochester this week walked out with warm soup, fresh bread, and a handmade bowl to keep. Those pickups marked the return of Channel One Regional Food Bank's annual Empty Bowls fundraiser, now in its 22nd year.

The to-go event ran on Tuesday, October 13, and Wednesday, October 14, 2026, at 2112 2nd Street SW. Channel One organized the fundraiser as a pre-order system, taking orders online through October 7 without walk-up sales. Ticket holders scheduled pickup slots during windows from 11:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m., arriving to collect their meals and browse display tables to select a handcrafted bowl on a first-ordered, first-served basis.

Each 16-ounce pint cost $25 and came with bread from local bakeries. According to reporting from 106.9 KROC and Channel One, eight area restaurants and caterers donated the soups: Bleu Duck Kitchen, Canadian Honker, Chester's Kitchen & Bar, Five West Kitchen + Bar, The Hubble House, Pinnacle Catering, Thai Pop, and Victoria's Ristorante & Wine Bar.

The bowls that came with the orders were donated by regional potters, woodworkers, and arts groups. Contributors included Sunny Slopes Pottery, Zumbro Valley Woodturners, Mago Studio Art, Carey Ceramics, Aveli Pottery, and 125 LIVE. Community groups and local businesses also painted bowls and provided sponsorships, including Think Bank, Jessica Spy Optical to Go, Gloria Dei Lutheran Church, Rochester Civic Theatre, and the EVEN Hotel. Think Bank returned as presenting partner for more than a decade, alongside Diamond Sponsor Post Consumer Brands and Platinum Sponsors Knutson Construction, the Rochester Athletic Club, and Parkplace Motors.

Channel One Director of Development and Communications Jessica Sund noted that the bowls serve as a lasting reminder of neighbors who do not know where their next meal will come from. In reporting by KTTC, Sund stated that each single soup order provides 45 meals for people facing hunger.

The fundraiser has grown steadily. In 2025, Empty Bowls crossed the six-figure mark for the first time, bringing in more than $100,000. All proceeds from the sales go directly toward food purchasing and distribution. Channel One operates across a 14-county network in southeastern Minnesota and western Wisconsin, distributing more than 15 million pounds of food each year.
