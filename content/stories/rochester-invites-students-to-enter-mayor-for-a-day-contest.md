The City of Rochester announced on Thursday, Oct. 15, 2026, that local fourth, fifth, and sixth graders are invited to submit entries for the statewide Mayor for a Day Essay Contest. The competition is run in partnership with the League of Minnesota Cities, and regional broadcaster ABC 6 News circulated the city's announcement the same day.

To participate, students who entered fourth, fifth, or sixth grade in September 2026 must write an original essay between 125 and 300 words. The assignment asks students to answer either what they would do if they were mayor for a day, or what they would change in their community. Submissions are free, limited to one per student, and require parent or guardian consent.

The League of Minnesota Cities evaluates essays on creativity, originality, and the writer's ability to demonstrate a basic understanding of city services. Submissions are accepted through an online form, by email as a PDF attachment to mayorforaday@lmc.org, or by postal mail. The deadline to submit is Friday, Dec. 11, 2026.

Three statewide winners will each receive a $100 cash prize, and their essays will be published in Minnesota Cities magazine. Contest officials will select and notify winners by email in early January 2027. Winning essays will also be sent to each winner's home city mayor and distributed to local media outlets.

The contest is in its 15th year, after the League of Minnesota Cities established it in 2013 to teach young residents about municipal government. Rochester Mayor Kim Norton stated that young people have great ideas and described the contest as a chance for students to use their imaginations and share what matters to them in Rochester. League President and Carver Mayor Courtney Johnson stated that children view their communities from a distinct perspective and do not need to be old enough to vote to care about where they live.

Last year, the contest selected winning essayists from Minnetrista, Silver Bay, St. James, and Woodbury, and awarded honorable mentions to students from Duluth, Lakeville, Blaine, and Spring Valley.
