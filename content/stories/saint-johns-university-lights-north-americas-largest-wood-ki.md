The Johanna Kiln at Saint John's University in Collegeville begins a 10-day continuous wood-burning session on Friday at 4:00 p.m. As reported by AM 1240 WJON, the structure is recognized as the largest wood-burning pottery kiln in North America.

Operating the massive kiln requires extensive preparation, which is why it is fired only once every two years. Stacking and preparing the 12,000 ceramic sculptures and pottery pieces inside took up to nine weeks to complete. This run marks the 18th firing for the kiln, which first operated on October 13, 1995.

Master potter Richard Bresnahan founded Saint John's Pottery in 1979 following a multi-year apprenticeship in Karatsu, Japan. Bresnahan designed the kiln, named it in honor of art historian Sister Johanna Becker, OSB, and constructed it alongside apprentices and volunteers. This firing showcases ceramic work from 27 artists, including three current apprentices, craftspeople from Japan, and artists from Minnesota, Colorado, New Mexico, Maine, and Texas.

Keeping the fire burning continuously across the 10-day run requires teams of volunteers working around the clock in stoking shifts. The burn is scheduled to wrap up on October 25.

The public lighting ceremony at the Saint John's Pottery Studio opens with a short prayer and a traditional Japanese purification ritual using rice, salt, and sake. Musician, songwriter, and Saint John's alumnus John McCutcheon is scheduled to perform during the event. Lighting duties fall to five grandsons of the late Kevin Flicker, who will ignite the kiln using a handmade torch. Flicker, a former studio apprentice and longtime ceramics professor at the University of Minnesota Morris, died in the past year.

The 18th firing is dedicated to Flicker, studio architect James Dean, and artist Catherine Mulligan, who all died within the past year. Dean designed the Saint John's Pottery Studio. Mulligan participated in every firing since the kiln debuted in 1995, and her final ceramic works were placed inside to be fired posthumously. According to reporting from the College of Saint Benedict and Saint John's University, Mulligan's family noted she maintained a unique bond with Bresnahan.

When the burn finishes on October 25, volunteers will remove a piece of hot pottery from the kiln to light candles inside three spirit boats commemorating Dean, Flicker, and Mulligan. Those boats will be paddled by canoe into Stumpf Lake below the studio and released onto the water. Crews will then seal the kiln shut with recycled clay, leaving it to cool for two weeks before the 12,000 pottery pieces are unloaded, cleaned, and prepared for use.
