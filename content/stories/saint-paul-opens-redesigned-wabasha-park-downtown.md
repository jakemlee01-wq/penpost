Downtown Saint Paul has remade one of its central public squares. The City of Saint Paul scheduled the grand opening and ribbon-cutting ceremony for the newly redesigned Wabasha Park for Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, at noon.

The park sits at 376 Wabasha St. N. in the downtown core. The city has owned the site since 1979, and it was previously known as Capital Centre Plaza, Ecolab Plaza, and Osborn Plaza. The Saint Paul City Council and Parks Commission renamed the plaza Wabasha Park after community surveys to clarify that the space belongs to the public, as reported by the St. Paul Voice and Hoodline. Parks and Recreation Director Andy Rodriguez noted in a Downtown St. Paul Fall 2026 report that passersby often mistook the space for the front steps of neighboring private towers, saying, "A lot of people didn't know this is parkland."

As reported by Hoodline, the $4.1 million project converted a deteriorating concrete plaza into an accessible civic space. Funding came from Saint Paul's voter-approved 1% "Common Cent" local option sales tax, which was enacted in May 2024 to support municipal streets, park infrastructure, and deferred maintenance. The St. Paul Voice reported that the overhaul addresses part of a $100 million deferred maintenance backlog across the city's park system.

Crews removed stairs to create a single continuous grade across the site. According to Hoodline and the Saint Paul Parks and Recreation Department, this design established full Americans with Disabilities Act accessibility, flat pedestrian walking paths, expanded seating, upgraded lighting, expanded planting beds, and a pet relief area. Trees removed to level the grade are being replaced with a diverse selection of new species on a one-to-one ratio.

The project also added a custom wave-shaped pergola and shelter structure in the central plaza. At the corner of 5th and Wabasha streets, workers fully restored "Skygate," a landmark public art sculpture originally installed in 2000.

The overhaul was designed in partnership with Aune Fernandez Landscape Architects, and Sheehy Construction Company served as the prime contractor, according to the Minneapolis/St. Paul Business Journal.

Wabasha Park serves as a central entrance and skyway connection for several neighboring buildings. These include the Osborn370 building, the Alliance Bank building, and the Stella building, a 16-to-17-story former office tower converted into downtown residential apartments.

The grand opening celebration includes scheduled remarks from Rodriguez, Saint Paul Downtown Alliance President Joe Spencer, and local elected officials. The event features live music performed by the Will Effertz Collective, lawn games, food for purchase from downtown businesses Ruam Mit Thai + Lao Cuisine and Hepcat Coffee, and prize drawings supported by the Saint Paul Downtown Alliance.
