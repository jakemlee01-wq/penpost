Sanford Health Bemidji marked Indigenous Peoples Day on Monday, October 12, 2026, with a blessing ceremony, a traditional feast, and cultural teachings at its medical center campus on Anne Street Northwest, according to Sanford Health News.

Darryl Kingbird opened the gathering at 10:00 a.m. with a traditional prayer and blessing in the hospital's East Lobby, as reported by Lakeland PBS. Sanford Health staff and visitors were encouraged to wear tribal nation attire throughout the day, and hospital communications listed Rebekah Fineday as the community liaison for the gathering.

Alongside the morning blessing, an artist vendor showcase ran from 10:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. in the East Lobby. The showcase featured work from regional Indigenous artists Brian Dow and Rob Fineday, according to Minnesota Daily News.

The medical center cafeteria served a community lunch between 11:00 a.m. and 1:30 p.m., Lakeland PBS reported. The menu included bison roast with mushroom sauce, mashed rutabaga, wild rice salad, and berries. The Walker Pilot Independent reported that the meal was free for Sanford employees and cost $7 for public visitors.

At 1:00 p.m., the hospital hosted a teaching session on the history and spiritual significance of the Anishinaabe jingle dress in room Education A/B, according to MPR News. Sanford schedules listed Riley Roy to lead the session, while Lakeland PBS reported that Vicky Fineday presented on jingle dress history and traditions.

Hospital patients, healthcare staff, and local community members attended the events, according to Lakeland PBS. Sanford Health Bemidji runs similar outreach events annually to maintain ties with local Anishinaabe residents and regional tribal communities.

Both Lakeland PBS and KAXE reported that the hospital gathering took place alongside wider Indigenous Peoples Day observances across Bemidji, Beltrami County, and northern Minnesota.
