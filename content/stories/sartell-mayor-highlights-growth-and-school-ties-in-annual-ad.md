Sartell Mayor Ryan Fitzthum delivered the city's 2026 State of the City address on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, focusing on commercial growth, municipal finances, and local institutional partnerships.

According to reporting from WJON News and the Sartell Area Chamber of Commerce, the annual luncheon took place at 12:00 p.m. at The Waters Church in Sartell, organized as a joint event with the chamber. St. Cloud Live reported that more than 50 local business and community leaders attended the presentation.

WJON reported that the city organized the presentation around the guiding theme and question "Why Sartell?" to showcase the community's appeal to incoming residents and developers. Fitzthum pointed to the local school system, stable property tax rates, ongoing commercial expansion, and sustained economic growth as primary reasons families and businesses choose Sartell.

Fitzthum specifically highlighted the working relationship between Sartell municipal staff and the Sartell-St. Stephen School District, led by Superintendent Dr. Michael Rivard. Fitzthum noted that the collaboration over the last three years represents the strongest partnership he has seen during his ten years serving in elected office.

Addressing safety, Fitzthum emphasized that school incidents cannot be viewed in isolation from the broader city. As reported by WJON, Fitzthum remarked that when an incident occurs, "it's not a school problem, it's not a city problem, it's a community problem."

Fitzthum also outlined the geographic division of Sartell, explaining that single-family residential homes are predominantly clustered on the north side, while commercial developments and higher-density residential housing are concentrated on the south side. Fitzthum explained that this structure creates a natural separation between commercial and residential areas, but added that maintaining a small-town atmosphere requires neighbors to get out, meet each other, and watch out for one another.

According to WJON, city leaders stated that maintaining stable property taxes, delivering quality municipal services, pursuing regional business partnerships, and executing long-term planning remain the city's key priorities to attract newcomers.
