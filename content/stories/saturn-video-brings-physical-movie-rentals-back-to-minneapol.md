Browsing physical shelves for a weekend movie rental has returned to Minneapolis. Nonprofit Saturn Video is holding the grand opening for its first permanent storefront on Saturday, October 17, 2026, at 3201 East 25th Street in the Seward neighborhood.

Minneapolis has lacked a dedicated brick-and-mortar video rental shop since South Minneapolis staple Movies on 35th in Powderhorn closed in 2018, as reported by MPR News, Axios Twin Cities, and Racket. The wider Twin Cities metro lost its last commercial video rental outlet in May 2023, when Video Universe in Robbinsdale shut its doors.

Founder Sierra Parham decided to launch Saturn Video after Video Universe closed and after she was laid off from her job. Parham told Racket that hearing that the Robbinsdale location was the last one broke her heart. MPR News reported that Parham has been collecting VHS tapes and DVDs for roughly 16 years, an interest that started during an internship at Intermedia Arts through the Minneapolis Step Up youth employment program. Parham discussed the opening during an October 15 broadcast of MPR News' Minnesota Now.

Saturn Video operated for three years as a mobile pop-up, setting up physical media sales and rental tables at Twin Cities markets, community events, and local theaters, according to Axios Twin Cities and FOX 9. On September 1, 2026, Saturn Video moved into a shared creative hub alongside the 3201 Collective and Dream Chambers Public Access, according to the shop and Racket.

The store's catalog grew past 1,000 titles across DVD, Blu-ray, and VHS formats heading into opening day, FOX 9 and MPR News reported. The inventory was assembled through community donations and acquisitions. Parham told Axios Twin Cities and Hoodline that while the shop stocks mainstream releases, she focuses the collection on indie cinema, cult favorites, and films directed by women, LGBTQ+, and BIPOC filmmakers.

Saturn Video offers a membership model priced at $10 a month, which gives patrons unlimited movie swaps with one title checked out at a time, according to Axios Twin Cities and MPR News. Non-members can rent individual movies for a few dollars. The project operates as a nonprofit under the 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsorship of St. Paul-based Springboard for the Arts. It gathers community donations through GiveMN, Racket reported.

Racket reported that the opening weekend features a free public celebration and sidewalk sale on Saturday from 2 p.m. to 8 p.m. That is followed by an after-hours party on Sunday, October 18, from 5 p.m. to 10 p.m., which includes DJ sets, zine-making, tarot readings, and a curated film program with a $10 suggested donation. Over the long term, Parham told MPR News and Racket that she envisions expanding the Seward space into a hybrid video rental store and late-night café where film lovers can gather and talk.
