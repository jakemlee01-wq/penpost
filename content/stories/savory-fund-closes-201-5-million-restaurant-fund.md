Savory Fund has closed its third investment pool at $201.5 million, topping its original $200 million goal. The Salt Lake City private equity firm, an investment manager that pools capital to buy stakes in private companies, announced the final closing on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026.

As reported by PR Newswire and Fast Casual, the firm has already deployed 35% of Fund III across four regional restaurant chains. Savory plans to build the fund into a stable of eight to nine concepts across quick-service, fast-casual, and casual-dining formats, leaving four to five additional brand investments to come, according to Dealroom and Nation's Restaurant News.

The four concepts currently backed by the fund cover distinct dining sectors. Nation's Restaurant News reported that the portfolio includes South Block, an acai bowl and smoothie chain based in Arlington, Virginia, and the Washington, D.C., area that is expanding beyond its home market. It also includes Bonrue Bakery, a European-style artisan patisserie operating in St. George, Utah.

The other two initial investments focus on Asian cuisine. Hawkers Asian Street Food, based in Orlando, Florida, brings a Michelin Guide recommendation alongside high guest frequency and unit volumes, according to Fast Casual and Nation's Restaurant News. Zao Asian Grill, a pan-Asian fast-casual chain based in Salt Lake City, operates 23 locations and has generated roughly 18% to 20% same-store sales growth, which tracks revenue gains at locations open at least a year, according to Nation's Restaurant News.

Savory pairs its capital with operational backing in real estate, supply chains, and technology, Fast Casual reported. Platform chief executive officer Clay Dover, who was appointed to the role in January 2026, leads that operational strategy alongside co-founders and managing directors Andrew K. Smith and Shauna K. Smith.

Shauna K. Smith claimed that capital is not what sets the firm apart, pointing instead to an operational team tasked with scaling brands without diluting what made them work. Andrew K. Smith called the fund close a vote of confidence, pointing to support from major commercial banks and returning investors.

According to Dealroom and PR Newswire, limited partners, the outside investors who supply money to private funds, consisted of both new commercial institutions and returning backers from earlier rounds. Savory closed Fund I at $100 million in October 2020 and Fund II at $100 million in 2021, followed by an interim close of $110 million for Fund III in December 2025, according to Fast Casual.

Founded in 2018, Savory manages more than $750 million in assets across 13 restaurant brands and over 400 locations in 27 states, Dealroom and PR Newswire reported. The firm previously built its record by scaling beverage brand Swig from 17 to 46 units before selling a majority stake to The Larry H. Miller Company in 2022, and expanding Hawaiian barbecue chain Mo' Bettahs from six to 56 units before selling majority control to Blue Marlin Partners and Trive Capital in 2024. Savory kept minority stakes in both companies.
