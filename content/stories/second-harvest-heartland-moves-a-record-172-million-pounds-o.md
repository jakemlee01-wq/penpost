Pounds of food, like freight trucks, are heavy things to move when diesel is north of six dollars a gallon. On Monday, Oct. 26, 2026, Second Harvest Heartland announced that it distributed a record 172 million pounds of food across Minnesota over the past fiscal year, running the massive effort out of its Brooklyn Park warehouse.

That total marks a 500 percent increase over the last two decades and a 20 percent jump in just four years, according to Chief Supply Chain Officer Toni Scott in reporting by MPR News. Scott noted that the old rhythm of peaks and valleys has vanished. The seasonal dips are gone, replaced by a steady stream of demand. A crisis usually comes with an endpoint, Scott told KARE 11, but the current strain feels like a continued need where agencies stretch their hours and still feel behind.

The pressure on household budgets is clear. Scott highlighted that one in five Minnesotans cannot afford groceries, as reported by KARE 11 and MPR News. U.S. Inflation Calculator metrics reported by KARE 11 show grocery prices climbed 2.5 percent this year, with fruit and vegetable prices rising nearly 4 percent.

At the same time, moving supplies has become its own financial hurdle. Shipping food across the region added an extra $20,000 every month to Second Harvest Heartland's diesel fuel budget alone, according to KARE 11 and MPR News. Scott told MPR News that diesel costs jumped 70 percent since March, with AAA reporting Minnesota diesel averaging around $6.18 per gallon by mid-October.

Those climbing freight rates forced Second Harvest Heartland to scale back on certain long-distance shipments, such as citrus from California, and the group warned that off-season fresh produce will be tougher to secure, MPR News reported.

The crush at the door reflects multiple overlapping hits. MPR News reported that the surge in food shelf visits stems from federal cuts to SNAP and Medicaid, persistent inflation, higher transportation costs, government shutdowns, and Operation Metro Surge. The numbers tell the scale. Minnesota food shelves logged a record 9 million visits in 2025, which is more than 2.5 times the 2019 total, and recorded three of the four busiest months in state history during 2026, according to hunger-relief partner The Food Group.

Inside the Brooklyn Park distribution center, sorting and packing goods falls to workers and regular volunteers. Julia Traynor, a restaurant worker who donates her time two to three times a week, told KARE 11 that her industry work exposed her to food insecurity, adding that meeting hungry people asking for free meals breaks your heart. On the warehouse floor, team member Timothy told KARE 11 that it is really nice to help a lot of folks who need food.

Keeping the operation rolling requires outside help. Nonprofit organizers told KARE 11 that steady donations and volunteer shifts remain vital. To meet the load, Second Harvest Heartland is using one-time state funding approved during the 2026 legislative session to distribute an additional 3.4 million pounds of food, while preparing further policy advocacy for the 2027 session.
