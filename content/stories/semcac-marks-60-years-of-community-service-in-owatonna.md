Many residents only discover the Southeastern Minnesota Community Action Corporation when hardship strikes, according to Executive Director Jennifer Hengel.

SEMCAC marked 60 years of community service on Thursday with an open house at the Steele County Dining site in Owatonna, as reported by the Owatonna People’s Press. Hengel spoke at the gathering to outline the nonprofit's history, scope, and mission, noting that raising awareness was a primary goal because the agency wants neighbors to know what help exists before they face an emergency.

The agency's core focus is going after the causes and conditions of poverty and offering programs designed to help people become self-sufficient, according to reporting from Southernminn.com.

SEMCAC was formed in 1966 under the federal Economic Opportunity Act of 1964. It now serves as the designated community action agency for seven southeastern Minnesota counties, making it one of 24 community action agencies operating across the state.

Managing those regional programs involves 231 staff members statewide, including 27 workers based directly in Steele County. The agency also relies on 1,045 community volunteers, who contributed 36,690 service hours over the past year to support efforts like Meals on Wheels.

That network delivered substantial support across Steele County in 2026. SEMCAC provided direct financial assistance to 604 Steele County households to cover utility bills, and assisted 79 households with rent or mortgage payments to prevent eviction, foreclosure, and homelessness.

Nutrition was another central piece of the county workload. SEMCAC served 33,482 senior meals in Steele County during 2026 through home-delivered services and weekday dining sites.

SEMCAC also arranged 649 transit trips for Steele County residents through its volunteer driver program, and educated and supported 34 children and their families through local Head Start early childhood education programming.
