U.S. employers added 29,000 jobs in September, the Labor Department reported Oct. 2. Economists surveyed by Bloomberg had expected about 90,000, according to Yahoo Finance.

The unemployment rate rose from 4.1% to 4.2%. Job gains for July and August were also revised down by a combined 60,000, which means the summer was a little softer than first reported.

Most of the hiring came from health care, which added 17,000 jobs, below its recent monthly average. Construction added 11,000. The financial sector lost 7,000 and is down 129,000 jobs from its peak in May 2025, with many of the losses at insurance companies.

Paychecks grew slowly. Average hourly earnings rose 0.1% from August and are up 3% from a year ago, which Yahoo Finance noted is likely below the current rate of inflation.

How worried to be depends on who you ask. Vanguard economist Adam Schickling called it "a low-hire, low-fire labor market." Economists at the St. Louis Fed said the rise in unemployment was very small and that the job market still looks healthy by historical standards.

The practical upshot: jobs aren't disappearing quickly, but they aren't easy to find either. Yahoo Finance reported the weak number may lead the Federal Reserve to hold interest rates steady this month.
