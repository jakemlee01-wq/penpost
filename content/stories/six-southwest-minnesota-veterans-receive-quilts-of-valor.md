Six southwest Minnesota military veterans were presented with handmade Quilts of Valor at 10 a.m. on Saturday, Oct. 24, at the Marshall Adult Community Center, the Marshall Independent reported.

The honorees included Kenneth Schultz, Curtis "Curt" Fried, Barry Nauman, and Lee Steven Simmons, all of Marshall, along with Emmet Bakke and David Erp of Hazel Run, according to coverage from the Marshall Independent and Marshall Radio. The veterans represented multiple service branches, including the Air Force, Navy, Naval Reserve, Army, and Army National Guard.

The quilts were made and presented by Pieces for Heroes, a local chapter of the Quilts of Valor Foundation that gathers twice a month in Marshall, the Marshall Independent reported. The volunteer group counts about 20 core members who meet regularly to sew alongside additional home quilters. Pieces for Heroes has awarded more than 500 quilts across southwest Minnesota since 2019, according to the Marshall Independent and Marshall Radio. Nationwide, Blue Star mother Catherine Roberts founded the Quilts of Valor Foundation in 2003 while her son was deployed in Iraq, and the organization has since awarded over 400,000 quilts.

Bruce Fuhrmann of the Marshall American Legion post served as master of ceremonies, reported the Marshall Independent. Fuhrmann has worked with the quilting group since 2017 to interview nominees and share their backgrounds. He called each veteran ahead of time to gather their service histories, noting, "Some of the stories they've shared with me really makes the job enjoyable."

Each recipient brought a distinct record of service to the ceremony, as detailed by the Marshall Independent. Kenneth Schultz served in the Navy from 1960 to 1964 as a storekeeper managing supplies and parts aboard the submarine USS Queenfish at Pearl Harbor until its decommissioning, and then on the USS Blackfin, with deployments to Vietnam, Thailand, Korea, and Australia. Emmet Bakke served in the Air Force from 1967 to 1974 as a survival training instructor, teaching crews how to survive being shot down over land or water, and served at domestic bases as well as in Guam working on bombers and tankers.

Curtis "Curt" Fried enlisted in the Naval Reserve in 1968, completed two years of active duty in Guam at the Naval Hospital and a communications station monitoring ocean traffic, returned home in 1971, and served until his 1974 discharge. Barry Nauman served in the Army from 1968 to 1969, deploying to Bangkok, Thailand, with a Transportation Company hauling cargo that ranged from food to bombs and ammunition. David Erp was drafted into the Army in 1969, handled communications at an Air Force base in Thailand, was discharged in 1971, and served three additional years in the National Guard.

Lee Steven Simmons enlisted in the Army National Guard in Tracy in 1971, transferred to Redwood Falls with the 682nd Engineer Battalion, worked as a full-time Readiness NCO and 1st Sgt., oversaw deployed troops during a 2003 deployment to Kosovo, and retired in 2009 after 38 years of military service.

Nancy McClain, speaking for Pieces for Heroes, told the audience, "It is a blessing to see all of you so very proud of our veterans." McClain said the quilters hold veterans dear to their hearts and seek the right piece for each person, adding, "the ladies are very particular about that perfect quilt to go along with that perfect veteran or service member." The Marshall Independent reported that McClain closed the ceremony by reminding recipients that the quilts serve as a lasting sign of community gratitude, stating, "We want you to know that you will always be in our hearts."
