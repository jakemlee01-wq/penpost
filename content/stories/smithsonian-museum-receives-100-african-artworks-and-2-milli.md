The Smithsonian’s National Museum of African Art announced on Thursday, October 15, 2026, that it received a major bequest from the estate of former Michigan state senator and educator Jack Faxon. The gift includes 100 African artworks dating from the 19th and 20th centuries, which Faxon assembled over his lifetime, along with more than $2 million in endowment funds.

The museum identified the contribution as one of the most significant philanthropic acquisitions in its history. The donation formally establishes the Jack Faxon Collection of African Art. The financial gift creates an endowment, which is an invested fund where earnings support specific long-term needs. For the museum, the money will directly support collections care, conservation, research development, and the stewardship of permanent holdings.

The National Museum of African Art was originally established in 1964 as a private educational institution before later joining the Smithsonian. Today, its permanent collection holds nearly 13,000 artworks spanning more than 1,000 years of African history.

John K. Lapiana, the museum’s director, stated that Faxon set an example through leadership in the arts and education, noting that the former lawmaker understood the value those fields bring to public life. Lapiana said the museum plans to honor Faxon’s legacy by displaying the collection on the National Mall and preserving the pieces for future generations.

Kevin D. Dumouchelle, a curator at the museum, noted that the Faxon collection is already connecting with other artworks currently under the museum's stewardship. Dumouchelle added that the endowment funding significantly expands the institution's ability to maintain that dialogue over time. The museum also highlighted a quote from a 2013 episode of Faxon’s documentary series, The Collection, in which Faxon stated, "We honor the world by what we contribute to it." Faxon lived from 1936 to 2020.

Artworks from the bequest will make their public debut in an upcoming exhibition curated by Dumouchelle titled New & Now: A Decade Building the National Collection of African Art, which highlights key acquisitions made over the past ten years.

The announcement coincided with the release of a companion volume, African Art Works: Artists Creating Change / The Jack Faxon Collection, written by Dumouchelle and published by Smithsonian Books. The volume features more than 200 photographs documenting sculptures, paintings, and textiles. According to Smithsonian Books, the publication pairs historic objects, including a Baga Sibondël headdress from Guinea, an early-20th-century slit gong, and a Zulu milk pail, with modern and contemporary pieces such as Wangechi Mutu’s 2016 sculpture Tree Woman.

According to reporting from PoPville, Dumouchelle is scheduled to speak about the new book during an author presentation on Saturday, October 17, 2026, at the Politics and Prose location at Union Market in Washington, D.C.
