Southwest Minnesota State University has permanently installed the flags of all 11 federally recognized Minnesota Tribal Nations across the upper level of its Student Center on the Marshall campus. The flags were unveiled during an official dedication ceremony on Wednesday, October 14, 2026.

The ceremony, formally listed as the Indigenous People's Day Flag Dedication and Proclamation, began at 11:30 a.m. in the Upper Student Center. As reported by SMSU Today, the university issued a formal proclamation recognizing sovereign Dakota and Ojibwe tribal governments from across the state.

The permanent display is the result of initiatives and advocacy led by SMSU student leaders and faculty members who worked to establish visible Indigenous representation and honors on campus. The event was organized and sponsored by the university's Office of Diversity & Inclusion.

The Wednesday gathering brought together members of the student body, campus faculty, and residents from the broader Marshall community. According to university event notices, the ceremony was scheduled in connection with regional Indigenous Peoples Day programming and campus diversity observances leading into Native American Heritage Month.

University releases stated that the installation will serve as an educational and cultural landmark for current students, visiting families, and prospective attendees arriving for upcoming campus discovery events. The 11 flags will remain hung permanently in the Student Center as an ongoing visual acknowledgement of tribal sovereignty and Native heritage in Minnesota.
