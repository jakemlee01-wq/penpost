The St. Cloud City Council voted 5-0 on Monday night, October 19, 2026, to approve the sale of $45.335 million in municipal bonds, which are loans local governments issue to pay for public works. KNSI reported that council members Scott Brodeen and Mark Johnson were absent from the vote.

The finalized borrowing total came in roughly $1.2 million below the $46.54 million bond issuance that the council initially authorized the previous month. The bonds sold at 10:30 a.m. Monday, drawing competitive bids from 15 banks and financial lenders. UBS Bank submitted the winning bid with an interest rate of 4.38%, establishing a 20-year repayment term for the city.

Mayor Jake Anderson credited the turnout and the borrowing rate to the city's AA+ bond rating. Anderson stated that a bond rating functions like a credit score, helping secure better rates and making the debt less painful to pay for, KNSI reported.

The majority of the bond proceeds will pay for Fire Station 6, a regional firefighter training center, and related equipment. The remaining money will finance the city's 2026 street reconstruction projects. St. Cloud voters had previously authorized up to $43.5 million for the fire station and training center during the November 5, 2024, general election referendum.

Property taxes will cover the bond payments over the next two decades. The city has allocated $1.5 million in its 2026 tax levy toward the debt and has proposed $3.5 million in the 2027 levy.

Excavation and dirt moving are already underway at 4850 33rd Street South following an early autumn groundbreaking, KNSI and WJON reported. Anderson stated that the city aims to finish construction by the end of 2027.
