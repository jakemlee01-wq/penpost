A vacant Bremer Bank branch that closed in 2020 at West 36th Street and Boone Avenue South is slated to become a seven-story residential complex called Creekline Flats.

The St. Louis Park City Council scheduled two public hearings for Monday, October 26, 2026, at City Hall, 5005 Minnetonka Boulevard. At 6:00 p.m., the council will consider establishing the Creekline Flats Tax Increment Financing District within Redevelopment Project No. 1. At 6:15 p.m., council members will review a housing program to issue up to $45 million in conduit revenue bonds under Minnesota Statutes Chapter 462C, the Minnesota Housing Act.

Conduit revenue bonds allow local governments to help finance private projects without using taxpayer funds. City public notices specify that the obligations will not constitute a charge, lien, or debt on city property and will not be backed by the full faith and credit of St. Louis Park. The debt service will be paid entirely from project revenues under an agreement with the borrower.

The project developer is LSBD St. Louis Park Boone, LLC, which is affiliated with Minneapolis-based Broadway Street Development, according to city filings and reporting from Finance & Commerce. Finance & Commerce reported the overall cost of Creekline Flats is estimated at approximately $99.7 million.

City agenda records show the planned 280,000-square-foot building wraps seven levels of living space around a 5.5-level structured parking ramp with roughly 315 parking stalls. The planned address is 8855 36th Street West, on ground previously designated as 8800 State Highway 7 near Minnehaha Creek.

Finance & Commerce reported the 208 apartments will be 100% affordable under federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credit guidelines for 30 years, serving households earning between 30% and 80% of Area Median Income. The planned unit mix includes 47 one-bedroom, 81 two-bedroom, and 80 three-bedroom homes. Under the city filings, 11 units are set at 30% AMI, 21 at 50%, 131 at 60%, 36 at 70%, and 9 at 80%.

City Council agenda materials note that the project plans incorporate geothermal heating and cooling, rooftop solar panels, bicycle storage, electric vehicle charging spaces, and improved public access to Minnehaha Creek.

To balance the project budget, the developer's pro forma indicates plans to defer nearly 100% of the developer fee. City records show the developer secured a $45 million bond allocation from Minnesota Management and Budget in July 2026. The developer also applied for a $1.125 million redevelopment grant from the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development and roughly $1 million from the Metropolitan Council's Livable Communities Demonstration Act.

Bond proceeds are earmarked for project construction, capitalized interest during construction, debt service reserves, and issuance costs. Finance & Commerce and city documents note that financing is scheduled to close in December 2026, the developer must begin spending bond proceeds by December 31, 2026, and the building has an estimated opening date in summer 2028.
