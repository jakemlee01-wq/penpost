The St. Paul City Council is scheduled to vote Wednesday, October 14, 2026, on a resolution directing municipal staff to study and draft regulations for driverless commercial vehicles before companies start service in the city, according to WCCO and MinneapoliMedia News.

Introduced by Council President Rebecca Noecker, the measure asks the city to establish local standards ahead of commercial operations so officials can weigh the role of autonomous vehicles while protecting workers and residents.

If council members approve the resolution, the St. Paul Planning Commission will spearhead the review alongside city staff, WCCO reported. The inquiry will analyze how autonomous fleets affect local jobs, traffic congestion, parking, and public safety.

Reporting from the Pioneer Press, Mass Transit, and WCCO notes that the study will also examine vehicle performance during Minnesota winter weather, accessibility for people with disabilities, data storage, and law enforcement access to vehicle records. The commission will review St. Paul's authority to license operators and require transparent reporting on vehicle mileage, daily operations, and safety incidents.

As part of the study, the planning commission will gather formal input from transit workers, labor unions, disability community advocates, and emergency responders.

Explaining her motivation, Noecker stated that St. Paul needs to be proactive about regulating big tech companies so local communities are not rolled over without guardrails, WCCO reported. Noecker also said state statutes remain vague on commercial autonomous operations, leaving open loopholes that require legislative action at both the local and state levels.

Across the river, Minneapolis lawmakers recently debated similar issues. On October 1, 2026, the Minneapolis City Council passed an ordinance by a 7 to 6 vote requiring driverless rideshare vehicles to keep a licensed human safety monitor behind the wheel, according to MPR News.

Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey vetoed that ordinance, arguing that requiring human drivers acts as a backdoor ban and that the city should stay open to innovative transportation technology, WCCO and MPR News reported.

Alphabet subsidiary Waymo began testing autonomous passenger cars across Minneapolis in late 2025 to evaluate mapping and snow handling, but the company does not currently operate in St. Paul, according to WCCO.

If the St. Paul City Council passes the resolution, municipal staff and the planning commission will begin public consultations to formulate draft ordinances ahead of any commercial permit applications.
