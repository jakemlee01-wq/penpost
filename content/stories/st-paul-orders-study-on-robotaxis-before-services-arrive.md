The St. Paul City Council unanimously approved a resolution directing city staff and the planning commission to study autonomous vehicles before commercial robotaxis launch, according to reports from FOX 9 and MPR News. The measure, approved on Wednesday, Oct. 14, 2026, does not ban driverless cars or order immediate operational restrictions, Power 104.7 FM reported. Instead, it sets up a study period to draft municipal guardrails before companies deploy paid passenger rides.

Council President Rebecca Noecker, who authored the resolution, told CBS Minnesota that finishing the study will take between six months and a full year. Noecker stated that St. Paul needs to set expectations early, cautioning that without local rules, major technology corporations can roll right over a local community. According to MPR News, Noecker noted that St. Paul currently has more breathing room than Minneapolis to evaluate its choices before companies like Waymo arrive at the city's door.

Commercial driverless rides do not operate in St. Paul yet, but mapping vehicles have already been spotted navigating city streets, MPR News and KSTP reported. Mass Transit reported that Waymo, which started at Google in 2009 and runs in 15 metropolitan areas, lists the Twin Cities among 17 target expansion markets alongside competitors like Amazon's Zoox and Tesla's Cybercab.

The upcoming study must examine how robotaxis affect existing transportation workers, traffic, parking, pedestrian safety, and public transit, according to Mass Transit and Power 104.7 FM. Researchers will also evaluate how driverless vehicle software handles Minnesota winter weather conditions, CBS Minnesota reported. City staff are instructed to review disability accessibility, emergency response, data privacy, police access to vehicle data, and the city's legal authority to license and monitor commercial autonomous fleets.

Input for the study must come directly from rideshare drivers, labor unions, emergency service providers, and disability advocates, Power 104.7 FM reported. Council Member HwaJeong Kim questioned whether city policy would protect working families or back corporate executives seeking to undercut wages, according to CBS Minnesota. Kim noted that immigrant residents account for more than half of all rideshare drivers across Minnesota, MPR News reported.

St. Paul's timeline contrasts with recent moves across the river. Minneapolis passed an ordinance requiring human safety operators behind the wheel of autonomous rideshare vehicles, but Mayor Jacob Frey vetoed the measure as a back-door ban, FOX 9 and KSTP reported. On Thursday, Oct. 15, the Minneapolis City Council failed to override that veto on a 7-5 vote. While Noecker told MPR News that requiring human monitors could be considered in St. Paul, she stated the city must first investigate whether that requirement is legally possible.
