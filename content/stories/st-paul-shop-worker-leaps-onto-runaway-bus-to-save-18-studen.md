Taking a work break outside usually involves some fresh air and scrolling through a phone, but on Monday afternoon, Oct. 19, Ebro Ahmed ended up chasing down a moving school bus.

The 27-year-old Eritrean-American convenience store worker was kicking a soccer ball outside his family's business, Ya Bricks Snacks and Tobacco in St. Paul's Dayton's Bluff neighborhood, when he spotted the bus rolling past at roughly 10 mph. According to reporting from Ground News and Good News Network, the vehicle was moving erratically with its hazard lights flashing, stop arm extended, and front passenger doors wide open.

Looking directly through the open entryway, Ahmed saw the driver convulsing, slumped forward in his seat, and foaming at the mouth from an apparent seizure, leaving the steering wheel completely unattended, as reported by The Guardian, Clarin, and KSTP.

"I kind of hesitated for a little bit, but I just, this was too crazy not to be real," Ahmed told KARE. "I just jumped on there, did what I had to do."

ABC News reported that Ahmed sprinted after the moving vehicle and leaped through the open doorway. To immediately free his hands for the steering wheel, he tossed his phone and eyeglasses to two frightened girls sitting in the front row, according to The Guardian.

Stopping the rolling vehicle was not simple, because Ahmed had never operated a school bus before. KSTP and ABC News reported that he briefly struggled with the unfamiliar dashboard and accidentally shifted the gear into reverse before getting the transmission into neutral and pulling the emergency parking brake.

His quick reaction brought the bus to a halt and protected the 18 to 19 Battle Creek Middle School students on board, Saint Paul Public Schools confirmed. District officials and Clarin reported that none of the children suffered physical injuries during the incident.

First responders and paramedics arrived quickly on the scene to treat the driver, Ground News noted. Paramedics transported him to a local hospital, and ABC News and Clarin reported the driver has since been discharged to continue recovering at home. A replacement bus arrived to take the middle schoolers home, while Saint Paul Public Schools deployed its mental health team to support the shaken students, according to The Guardian and KSTP.

Ahmed later credited an unexpected source for his composure, telling reporters that watching the television medical drama Grey's Anatomy helped him recognize the emergency and stay calm under pressure, Ground News and The Guardian reported.

Faith also guided his intervention. Ahmed explained to ABC News and IQNA that he was motivated by a Quran verse teaching that saving one life is like saving humanity. "I'm a numbers guy, and those numbers make sense to me," Ahmed said, describing the act as selflessness in the name of God.

"At the end of the day, youth are the most innocent humans on Earth, so anything you can do to save them, help them, it's worth it," Ahmed told KSTP.

To celebrate his quick intervention, Sheletta Brundidge and a local nonprofit presented Ahmed with a $1,000 reward check, KSTP and Inkl reported.
