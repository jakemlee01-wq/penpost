The City of St. Paul has filed a lawsuit in Ramsey County District Court against the City of Maplewood, FOX 9 and KARE 11 reported, after Maplewood officials rejected permits to rebuild a police firearms training building.

The St. Paul Police Department has operated an outdoor shooting range at 2621 Linwood Avenue East in Maplewood for more than 50 years. According to reporting from WCCO and FOX 9, the facility sits on Ramsey County-owned land under a long-term lease agreement that runs through 2073.

In its court filing, St. Paul stated that the existing range office can no longer safely operate. The city cited severe mold, rodent and vermin infestations, a damaged roof, and nonfunctional plumbing and bathrooms, as reported by FOX 9 and KARE 11.

Maplewood had previously approved a 3,196-square-foot replacement building in 2025. According to FOX 9 and KARE 11, St. Paul later scaled down the design to 1,224 square feet to stay within municipal budget constraints. Although the Maplewood Planning Commission recommended approving the smaller building, the Maplewood City Council voted to reject the permit application in August.

Maplewood council members based their rejection in part on environmental reviews that identified elevated levels of lead and other heavy metals in the property's drinking water, FOX 9 and KARE 11 reported.

St. Paul attorneys argued in the complaint that the permit denial was "arbitrary and capricious." The city asserted that Minnesota statutes protect the authority of law enforcement to repair and maintain existing shooting ranges. St. Paul also contended that it may not legally require a municipal permit, arguing the facility is restricted exclusively to sworn police officers.

St. Paul Assistant Police Chief Paul Ford told WCCO that training involves more than target practice, calling it an exercise in decision making, and added that he did not know the reason for the conditional use permit denial. A conditional use permit is a municipal approval allowing a property use that is permitted only under specific conditions.

Surrounding land use has also factored into the dispute. In May, the Maplewood City Council approved a formal resolution opposing the outdoor range, citing residential encroachment. According to reporting by WCCO and Minnesota Now, that encroachment includes a newly approved 206-unit development at The Ponds at Battle Creek golf course, where Maplewood Mayor Marylee Abrams noted future townhomes would stand as close as 65 feet from the range.

John Gregerson, a resident who has lived across the street from the training grounds since the early 1980s, told WCCO that the shooting sounds "like a war." Gregerson said that while he supports law enforcement, he does not believe the range belongs in an area that has become heavily built up.

Following a closed-session meeting, Maplewood filed a formal legal response in district court. As FOX 9 reported, Maplewood stated that its permit denial was based on legitimate public-health, safety, welfare, environmental, and land-use concerns. A court hearing date has not yet been scheduled.
