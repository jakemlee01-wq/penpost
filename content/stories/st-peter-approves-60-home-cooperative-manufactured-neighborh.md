A vacant 15-acre parcel along Klein Street in north St. Peter will soon become New North, a planned neighborhood of about 60 factory-built manufactured homes. The St. Peter City Council signed a formal development agreement with Twin Cities nonprofit CoNorth to build the community, as reported by The Minnesota Star Tribune.

The homes are projected to sell between $115,000 and $250,000, according to the Star Tribune and CoNorth. That price range is intended to create an entry-level pathway to homeownership for working-class families, first-time buyers, and seniors who are priced out of conventional site-built houses.

Under the resident-owned cooperative model, individual residents hold title to their private houses and collectively own and manage the underlying land. Southernminn.com and the Star Tribune reported that residents buy into that land ownership by purchasing a community share through a one-time fee. CoNorth, which was called Northcountry Cooperative Foundation before a summer 2025 rebrand, already provides technical and governance assistance to 18 resident-owned communities across Minnesota and Wisconsin.

The land for the project was provided by the city. Southernminn.com reported that the city council voted unanimously on August 24, 2026, to donate the municipal property, valued at $483,700, to CoNorth. The vote reversed a narrow 3 to 4 rejection of the Klein Street site in November 2025 that had stemmed from industrial zoning concerns. Council Member Dustin Sharstrom stated that a council review showed all other site options would have required raising local taxes, making the city-owned parcel the only viable site.

Holding the vacant property had been costing the city about $15,000 each year. Southernminn.com reported that St. Peter expects to eliminate those maintenance costs and collect between $15,000 and $30,000 annually in new property tax revenue once residents move in. St. Peter and CoNorth agreed to split early predevelopment costs 50/50 up to $70,000, retaining Bolton & Menk, Inc. for engineering services. Remaining city expenses will be recouped through special assessments, a funding mechanism under Minnesota Statute 429 that bills properties for public improvements that benefit them.

Neighborhood plans include paved city streets, shared green spaces, a public park, and sidewalks connecting directly into Gault Park and local trails, according to CoNorth. The layout also incorporates a community center and storm shelter. St. Peter will own and maintain the neighborhood's public streets, water lines, and sewer utilities.

A local housing study cited by CoNorth identified a need for 110 to 120 new owner-occupied homes in St. Peter over five years. Mayor Shanon Nowell told the Star Tribune that a shortage of workforce housing has restricted local employers, hospitals, and schools from recruiting essential staff, adding that sustainable growth is impossible without places for workers to live.

CoNorth Executive Director Victoria Clark-West stated that there is significant statewide demand for attainable homeownership, noting the project is intended to serve as a scalable model for rural communities across Minnesota facing workforce shortages. Todd Prafke noted that peer cities are watching the project closely, adding that factory-built homes provide a strong base for affordable housing options.

The development is planned for a phased rollout in groups of roughly 20 homes. Ground infrastructure work is scheduled to start in summer 2027 following council authorization of Phase 1 engineering services in October 2026.
