The Park Avenue Armory and the Manhattan School of Music announced that "Music for 18 Musicians: Staged Variations" will open on Wednesday, October 14, 2026. The production runs through Sunday, October 18, 2026, inside the armory's Wade Thompson Drill Hall.

The production commemorates two milestones: the 50th anniversary of the 1976 premiere of Steve Reich's minimalist composition and the composer's 90th birthday. Conceived and conducted by Alan Pierson, artistic director of the contemporary chamber band Alarm Will Sound, the presentation reimagines the score as a continuous, cycling durational performance lasting approximately three hours and 30 minutes. In the drill hall, the musicians will encircle audience members in an immersive physical arrangement.

According to reporting by BroadwayWorld, each performance presents three full successive cycles of the work played in sequence across three generations of musicians. The cycles will be performed by original members of the Steve Reich Ensemble, student musicians from the Manhattan School of Music performing under the ensemble name Tactus, and Alarm Will Sound. Between the continuous cycles, the staging incorporates newly composed connective interludes by Pulitzer Prize-winning guest artist Caroline Shaw.

The creative staging team includes John Heginbotham, who serves as movement coach, director, and associate director. Urs Schönebaum is designing the lighting, Mark Grey is the sound designer, and Daniel Neumann is serving as sound engineer.

For Alarm Will Sound, the October 2026 run aligns with a milestone of its own. Pierson noted that the ensemble gave its first public concert in 2001 performing Reich's score at New York's Miller Theatre, meaning the group is marking its 25th anniversary during this run. In materials published by the armory, Pierson described the composition as "the big bang that birthed my musical world," pointing out that Reich began sketching its initial themes only days before Pierson was born.

Performing the work also requires specialized training for the student cast. Ethan Burke, a student at the Manhattan School of Music, stated in an interview with Manhattan School of Music Spotlight that preparing for the piece requires learning unique oral and breathing traditions because Reich originally created the work without a formal written score.

Performances will take place Wednesday through Saturday at 7:00 PM, followed by a Sunday staging at 3:00 PM. Admission prices start at $55. Before the final performance on Sunday, October 18, composer Nico Muhly will host an artist talk with Reich and Pierson at 1:30 PM in the armory's Veterans Room to discuss the composition's history.
