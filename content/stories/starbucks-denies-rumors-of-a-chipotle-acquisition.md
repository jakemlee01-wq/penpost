Starbucks stated on Monday, Oct. 12, 2026, that it is not buying Chipotle, according to reports from Nation's Restaurant News and CNBC. A company spokesperson told Reuters that while Starbucks does not comment on rumors as a matter of policy, it wanted to be clear that it will not pursue an acquisition.

The denial followed an Oct. 8 report from the Financial Times stating that Starbucks had spent recent months working with financial advisers to explore an early takeover proposal. Chipotle held a market valuation between $39 billion and $41 billion at the time of the report, and analysts estimated a full buyout could have reached $50 billion.

A purchase at that valuation would have been more than three times larger than the restaurant industry's previous record transaction, Burger King's $11.4 billion acquisition of Tim Hortons in 2014, as noted by Nation's Restaurant News and Restaurant Dive. The San Joaquin Valley Sun and TradingKey reported that a merged company would have produced approximately $50 billion in annual sales across 41,000 Starbucks stores and roughly 4,200 Chipotle locations worldwide.

Market prices shifted sharply across the week. Nation's Restaurant News reported that Chipotle shares jumped more than 7% and Starbucks shares fell 5% after the initial report, with Chipotle briefly rising as much as 8%. Following the company's Monday denial, Forbes, Quartz, and CBS News reported that Chipotle shares fell between 2% and 4% to $30.56, while Starbucks shares rose slightly over 1%.

Speculation around a tie-up was driven partly by Starbucks chief executive Brian Niccol, who led Chipotle for six years from 2018 until August 2024, according to Forbes and CBS News. Chipotle shares have fallen roughly 50% since Niccol left and about 25% over the past year. CBS News and Forbes reported that those declines were compounded by an August salmonella outbreak that forced some Chipotle restaurants to remove jalapeños from their menus. Semafor reported that Chipotle had recently brought in defense advisers to protect against hostile takeovers or activist investors.

Analysts had expressed doubt about the rumored transaction. Quartz reported that TD Cowen analyst Andrew Charles viewed a deal as a low-probability outcome, while Brian Jacobsen noted that taking on a massive acquisition made little sense during an intensive operational turnaround. In a statement to CBS News and Inc., Starbucks said its leadership is laser-focused on executing its "Back to Starbucks" strategy and maintains confidence in its long-term growth. Inc. reported that the strategy focuses on store renovations, returning to the concept of a comfortable gathering place, and reducing café wait times to a four-minute target. Nation's Restaurant News and CBS News reported that Starbucks will release its fiscal fourth-quarter results and fiscal 2027 guidance later in October.
