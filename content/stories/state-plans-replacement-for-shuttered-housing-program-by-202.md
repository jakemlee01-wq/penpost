The Minnesota Department of Human Services plans to design a replacement for the state's shuttered housing stabilization initiative and submit a legislative proposal by 2028 under the Olmstead Plan, the Minnesota Reformer reported.

The original Medicaid-funded Housing Stabilization Services program launched in 2020. It was created to assist seniors, people with disabilities, and individuals transitioning out of residential care or substance use treatment with finding and keeping homes. According to the Reformer, state spending reached more than $104 million across more than 700 service providers at its 2024 peak, before the Walz administration shut the program down.

The shutdown followed federal investigations into billing issues across the network. Federal prosecutors have charged more than a dozen people in fraud cases tied to the initiative, accounting for at least $26 million in stolen Medicaid funds, the Reformer reported. According to the U.S. Department of Justice, a federal jury in St. Paul recently convicted the owner of a local agency on three counts of healthcare fraud connected to a $4.3 million scheme.
