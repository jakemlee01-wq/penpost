A room full of matching maroon shirts can alter a county budget faster than a red pen. On Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, the Steele County Board of Commissioners voted to reverse an earlier decision to defund the county Master Gardener coordinator position before finalizing the 2027 budget, according to reporting by Southernminn.com and the Owatonna People's Press.

The reversal came after roughly 20 Master Gardener supporters packed the boardroom wearing coordinated maroon attire, Southernminn.com reported. Before formal discussions began, eight Master Gardeners and community members from Steele, Rice, and Waseca counties delivered about 30 minutes of public testimony.

During public comments, Owatonna resident Michelle Waddell argued that the coordinator serves an indispensable guiding role, describing the position as the program's vital head of household, the Owatonna People's Press reported. Resident Maureen Lyons asserted that Commissioner Jim Abbe had a conflict of interest because his wife is a gardener, and she asked him to recuse himself before the vote took place, according to Southernminn.com.

When the vote was called, the motion to preserve funding passed, with Abbe casting the lone dissenting vote, Southernminn.com reported.

The board had previously voted unanimously on Sept. 22, 2026, to issue a formal termination notice to the University of Minnesota Extension, ending a three-year contract early ahead of its scheduled conclusion in 2027. That initial move would have eliminated county funding for both the Master Gardener coordinator and an agricultural educator.

Administrator Renee Fry and Abbe had justified those cuts as necessary discretionary reductions to keep the proposed 2027 property tax levy under 7 percent. A tax levy is the total amount in property taxes a local government decides to collect to fund operations. Southernminn.com and APG State News reported that county officials pointed to budget pressures, including a 3 percent cut in state county public aid funding and remodeling costs for the county annex building.

Commissioner Greg Krueger put the issue back on the table by motioning to add the contract to Tuesday's agenda, Southernminn.com reported. Krueger stated he received more than 26 emails from citizens urging the board to protect the Master Gardener coordinator, compared to zero emails regarding the agricultural educator. That contrast prompted him to propose uncoupling the two positions so the county could fund the coordinator.

Commissioners initially considered postponing the decision until a later budget session. Administrator Fry insisted on an immediate vote Tuesday, citing University of Minnesota termination notification deadlines, statutory three-day public meeting notice rules, and Krueger's upcoming vacation absence, Southernminn.com reported.

Lorrie Rugg holds the coordinator post, overseeing programming across both Steele and Rice counties and coordinating local volunteers with the University of Minnesota Extension, according to Southernminn.com and APG State News.

Program records reported by APG State News show that in 2025, 43 Steele County Master Gardener volunteers logged 3,206 service hours, valued at $38.16 per hour for a total of $115,865 in volunteer labor. The group also donated 1,600 pounds of fresh produce valued at $3,200 to Community Pathways of Steele County, and provided $4,000 in grants toward the Blue Ribbon Learning Garden at the Steele County Free Fairgrounds.

Following Tuesday's vote to restore the coordinator, the University of Minnesota Extension agriculture educator remains the only position slated for early contract termination and defunding in the county budget, Southernminn.com reported.
