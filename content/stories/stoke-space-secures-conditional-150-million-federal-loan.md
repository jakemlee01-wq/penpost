On Monday, October 26, 2026, the U.S. Department of War’s Office of Strategic Capital announced a conditional $150 million loan commitment to Stoke Space Technologies, GeekWire reported. The federal financing will combine with private capital to construct a second orbital launch pad at Launch Complex 14 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida, according to reporting from the Puget Sound Business Journal and Dow Jones Newswires.

The loan agreement is not yet final. GeekWire reported that Stoke Space must complete legal, technical, financial, and due-diligence requirements set by the federal government before funding is finalized. According to data tracked by Dealroom, the $150 million commitment ranks in the 96th percentile of 1,927 historical deep-tech debt rounds, making it one of the largest deep-tech debt transactions on record in the United States.

The new facility is planned to support the Nova Block 2, Stoke Space’s next-generation fully reusable rocket, according to the Puget Sound Business Journal. Dealroom and GeekWire reported that the Nova Block 2 is designed to lift up to 15 metric tons to low-Earth orbit, which provides five times the payload capacity of the company's first-generation rocket, the Nova Pathfinder.

The company's existing launch pad at Cape Canaveral will support the Nova Pathfinder, which is scheduled to conduct its maiden orbital test flight in early 2027, GeekWire reported. SpaceNexus and GeekWire reported that the larger Nova Block 2 rocket is scheduled to make its operating debut from the newly planned second pad in 2029.

David A. Lorch stated that the investment aligns with President Donald J. Trump’s National Space Transportation Policy to expand domestic launch capacity, enabling reliable access to space for defense and commercial partners. Jordan Sebold stated that supporting additional launch infrastructure enhances the resilience and capacity of the commercial space industrial base. Stoke Space projects that its reusable rocket architecture will reduce the cost of reaching orbit by up to 20 times while supporting satellite delivery, orbital maneuvers, and the capture and return of space assets.

Stoke Space was founded in 2019 by Chief Executive Officer Andy Lapsa, a former Blue Origin engineer of more than a decade, and Chief Technology Officer Tom Feldman, according to the Puget Sound Business Journal. The federal loan commitment follows an initial close of a $1 billion Series E equity round completed in September 2026, which brought the company's total lifetime capital raised to approximately $2.3 billion, Dow Jones Newswires reported. The outlet noted that the company’s investors include Point72 Ventures, Spark Capital, Y Combinator, US Innovation Technology, and Porsche.

The U.S. Space Force allocated Launch Complex 14 to Stoke Space in 2023 for renovation and orbital flight operations, according to the Puget Sound Business Journal. In 2025, the Space Force added Stoke Space to the pool of providers eligible to compete under Lane 1 of the National Security Space Launch program, though bidding remains contingent on completing an orbital flight, Dealroom reported. GeekWire reported that Stoke Space employs about 400 people across its 168,000-square-foot facility in Kent, Washington, its rocket test range in Moses Lake, Washington, and its site at Cape Canaveral.
