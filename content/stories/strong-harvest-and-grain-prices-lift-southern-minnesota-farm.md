Combines across Minnesota have made rapid progress this autumn, advancing the state corn harvest to 65 percent complete and soybean harvesting to 93 percent, according to the USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service. Farm Progress reported that field operations moved ahead of neighboring agricultural regions, where heavy rains significantly delayed harvest work across Iowa and sections of Nebraska and Illinois.

Field yields across southern Minnesota are surpassing earlier expectations, with producers frequently reporting near-record output, according to Jared Augustine of Hertz Farm Management. In an October 27, 2026, market report covered by Farm Progress, Augustine examined how fall harvest results and commodity prices are reshaping rural real estate. Grain prices have approached $5 per bushel for corn and $12 per bushel for soybeans, marking the first time in a prolonged stretch that high yields have aligned with that price level.

Individual field reports reflect the stronger harvest totals. Brownfield Ag News reported that farmer Matt Widboom recorded 60-bushel soybean yields and 200-bushel corn yields in Nobles County, managing those numbers despite weather swings that brought 13 inches of rain between mid-August and mid-September.

While growers focused on field operations, farmland market activity saw a temporary lull. Farmers, agricultural lenders, and real estate brokers anticipate that harvest optimism will encourage an active fourth quarter, with trendsetting auctions and sales expected in November and December, according to Farm Progress. Beyond high-quality land sustaining strong prices, brokers also anticipate rising demand for parcels rated as B-quality and C-quality. Farm Progress noted that stronger bids on B-quality tracts suggest lower-quality farm operations are moving into improved financial health compared to the previous two years.

Recent property sales documented by Farm Progress reflect strong pricing across several southern and central counties. North of Comfrey in Brown County, an 80-acre tract with 74.97 tillable acres and a 93.3 Crop Productivity Index, a measure of soil yield potential, recently sold for $13,750 per acre, which works out to $14,672 per tillable acre. Southwest of West Concord in Dodge County, a 231.08-acre parcel with 225.99 tillable acres and a 96.1 productivity index closed at $13,415 per acre, or $13,717 per tillable acre.

Other transactions show a similar range of valuations tied to soil productivity. Southwest of Frost in Faribault County, a 156.32-acre farm with 149.21 tillable acres and an 85.6 productivity index sold for $12,052 per acre, or $12,626 per tillable acre. East of Canton in Fillmore County, 69.41 acres with 68 tillable acres and an 88.5 index traded for $10,200 per acre, or $10,411 per tillable acre. In Goodhue County, a 40-acre farm east of Dennison featuring 36.2 tillable acres and a 94.1 index fetched $10,250 per acre, or $11,325 per tillable acre. Further north, northwest of Randall in Morrison County, a 40-acre parcel with 37.52 tillable acres and a 40.2 productivity index closed at $6,000 per acre, or $6,396 per tillable acre.
