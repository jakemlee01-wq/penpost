Federal immigration enforcement operations eliminated roughly 32,000 jobs across Minnesota, according to an economic study released by the Immigration Research Initiative and the Minnesota Budget Project.

The researchers calculated that 25,000 of those jobs were lost by U.S.-born workers due to downstream economic contractions. Another 7,000 were lost by non-arrested immigrants who curtailed their participation in the labor market.

Enforcement activity climbed steeply during the winter. As reported by the Latin Times, federal agents conducted nearly 4,000 arrests in Minnesota between December 2025 and February 2026 under Operations Metro Surge and PARRIS. That included 2,524 arrests in January 2026 alone, compared to a prior monthly average of 185.

Local balance sheets felt the effects. The City of Minneapolis calculated that the operations caused nearly $700 million in cumulative economic losses between December 2025 and April 2026, which included $445 million in lost business revenue and $152 million in lost worker wages.

Key service industries also shifted during the surge. Parallel research by North Star Policy Action and the Upjohn Institute showed Minnesota shed 5,800 leisure and hospitality jobs in the first quarter of 2026, while the rest of the nation gained 38,000 jobs in the sector.
