Tourism-related businesses generated $1.1 billion in total economic activity across Rochester in 2025, according to a report released on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, reported by KDHL, KROC-AM News, and the Post Bulletin. The economic impact study was commissioned by Experience Rochester, the city's destination marketing organization, and conducted by researchers at University of Minnesota Extension, Power 96 Radio reported.

Bill Von Bank, vice president of marketing and communications for Experience Rochester, stated that this is the first comprehensive report evaluating the overall footprint of the tourism sector on the city's economy, KDHL and KRFO-AM reported. Rochester ranks as Minnesota's third-largest city, with an estimated population of more than 115,000 residents, according to KROC-AM News.

Leisure travel, conventions, and medical-related visitation continue to serve as cornerstone economic drivers for Olmsted County, according to Experience Rochester and KROC-AM News. Visitor expenditures were concentrated across hospitality accommodations, food and beverage, retail shops, recreation, and local transportation, WJON reported.

As reported by KDHL and KRFO-AM, visitor spending supported a total of 7,670 local jobs and generated $323 million in labor-related income for workers. Of those positions, 6,100 jobs were tied directly to visitor expenditures.

Restaurants and bars are the top tourism employers in Rochester, where roughly half of the city's 6,400 bar and restaurant jobs are directly sustained by visitor spending, KDHL and KRFO-AM reported. Hotels and accommodations ranked as the second-largest category of tourism-related employment in the city.

Compared to national averages, Rochester showed a noticeably higher concentration of workers employed in lodging and accommodations, sporting goods and hobby retail stores, and amusement and recreation businesses, according to KDHL and KRFO-AM. The report also noted that Rochester holds a lower concentration of employees than national averages in air transportation, as well as in breweries, wineries, and distilleries.

Experience Rochester and city planners will use the research as an official baseline metric to guide future destination master planning and local tourism development strategies, according to reporting from KDHL, KRFO-AM, and the University of Minnesota Tourism Center.
