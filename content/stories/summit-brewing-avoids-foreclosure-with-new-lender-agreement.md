Running a brewery takes grain, water, yeast, and occasionally a very patient banker. Summit Brewing Company found one just in time, announcing an agreement on Thursday, Oct. 15, 2026, that removes an immediate threat of foreclosure, according to reporting by MinneapoliMedia News and MPR News.

An undisclosed financial backer has agreed to step in and buy out Summit's outstanding debt from BMO Bank. Neither the identity of the incoming lender nor the financial terms of the loan buyout were released, Bring Me The News and MPR News reported.

The St. Paul brewer had been facing mounting legal and financial pressure. As Bring Me The News reported, BMO Bank extended Summit a $1.25 million revolving line of credit alongside a $6.88 million term loan in 2024. Trouble emerged when Summit missed a scheduled payment in December 2025, according to MPR News and KARE 11.

The lender initially granted a forbearance agreement, which is a temporary pause on debt enforcement, that extended through July 31, 2026, MinneapoliMedia News and KARE 11 reported. Even with that breathing room, cash stayed tight. KARE 11 and Bring Me The News reported that Summit sent BMO Bank formal written notice in June 2026 stating it could not make the minimum payment because its liquidity, meaning accessible cash, and operational flexibility continued to deteriorate.

In September 2026, BMO Bank filed a civil complaint and foreclosure lawsuit seeking immediate repayment of more than $8.2 million, KARE 11 and Bring Me The News reported.

The buyout agreement halts that foreclosure threat. Summit President and CEO Brandon Bland announced the resolution, stating the financing arrangement is part of a broader recapitalization, which is a restructuring of a company's debt and equity balance, to secure long-term operations. Bland said the new lender is working directly with management and ownership to steady the balance sheet.

Bland shared praise for the staff during the announcement. "Today is a day of gratitude and genuine excitement for all of us at Summit. That starts with our employees, who have shown up every day through months of uncertainty," Bland said in a statement reported by KARE 11 and Bring Me The News. He added that while meaningful work remains ahead, the company now has a clear path forward to make beer and support community partners.

Founded in 1986, Summit is one of Minnesota's oldest and largest craft brewing operations. Company leadership confirmed that production, distribution, and taproom operations at its facility on Montreal Circle in St. Paul's West Seventh neighborhood will continue uninterrupted while the transition between lenders is finalized.
