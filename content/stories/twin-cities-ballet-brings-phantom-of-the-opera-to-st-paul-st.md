Twin Cities Ballet is preparing to debut a new, full-length production of "Phantom of the Opera: The Ballet," as reported by KARE 11 and Hometown Source. The Lakeville-based dance company will premiere the staging at the historic Fitzgerald Theater, located at 10 E. Exchange St. in downtown Saint Paul near Rice Park.

The production is scheduled for Halloween weekend, according to Visit Saint Paul. Performances will take place on Saturday, Oct. 31, at 2:00 p.m. and 7:30 p.m., with a final performance on Sunday, Nov. 1, at 2:00 p.m. The opening weekend run marks the launch of the company's fall performance season, according to reporting from Hoodline and Shout MN.

Artistic directors Denise Vogt and Rick Vogt conceived and choreographed the work, Hometown Source reported. Denise Vogt arranged the original story adaptation and compiled the music, while both directors shared choreography duties, according to Hoodline. The creative duo previously collaborated on rock-infused adaptations, including ballets set to the music of Queen and Pink Floyd's "The Wall," as noted by Explore Minnesota.

Rather than using Andrew Lloyd Webber's Broadway musical score, the production features an original classical music compilation arranged specifically for this story, Hometown Source reported. Denise Vogt stated that she wanted to create a version inspired by the heart of the original tale, using dance, music, and visual storytelling to explore the characters' emotional depth, including themes of beauty, loneliness, jealousy, obsession, and the longing to be loved.

The dancers will perform alongside digital stage environments created by multimedia and projection designer Peter Morrow, according to Hometown Source and Explore Minnesota. Those projections depict the subterranean passages of the Paris Opera House.

The setting inside the Fitzgerald Theater offers a historic atmosphere for the gothic production. Hoodline reported that the theater was constructed in 1910 and stands as Saint Paul's oldest surviving theater space.

Base ticket prices start at $42.50, which comes to $53.63 with fees included, according to DanceMN.org and Hoodline. The production also received public funding through a grant from the Minnesota State Arts Board, supported by the voter-approved Arts and Cultural Heritage Fund.
