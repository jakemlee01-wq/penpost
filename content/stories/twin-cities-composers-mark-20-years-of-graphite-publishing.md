Choir conductors can pull up a website, buy a digital score, and immediately print copies for a rehearsal. That direct link between living composers and vocal ensembles is the model Twin Cities spouses and composers Timothy C. Takach and Jocelyn Hagen built into Graphite Publishing, which is now marking its 20th anniversary.

The pair founded the sheet music business in 2006 to bridge the gap between composers writing new choral music and choir directors looking for original repertoire, MPR News reported. Headquartered on Nicollet Avenue in Minneapolis, the venture was established to provide an artist-first royalty model. Through digital PDF score distribution, composers retain their copyright and earn a significantly higher percentage of sales than traditional print publishing companies typically provide.

Hagen and Takach discussed the 20-year milestone on Wednesday, October 14, 2026, on MPR News's "Minnesota Now" with host Nina Moini and producer Ellen Finn. Arts reporter Sheila Regan also documented the company's anniversary for MinnPost. Hagen told MPR News that emerging composers two decades ago faced a binary choice between signing with large corporate publishing houses or staying fully independent and handling every piece of printing, marketing, and distribution alone.

Takach and Hagen explained that Graphite was designed to make performing new work feel normal, encouraging choir directors to take creative risks on living writers. Over two decades, the catalog has expanded from Minnesota across the country and internationally, centering on choral repertoire and art song, which is music composed for solo voice and piano. The founders credited Minnesota's unusually dense and active choral scene with providing the fertile ground an independent sheet music publisher needed to survive.

Twin Cities choral group VocalEssence publicly congratulated the founders, thanking them for 20 years of partnership in helping choirs license and digitally distribute commissioned works.

Both founders maintain full-time composition careers while operating the business. Hagen has gained international acclaim for major choral-orchestral pieces, including "The Notebooks of Leonardo da Vinci," which synchronizes live choral singing with digital projections. Takach, a former long-time singer with the Minneapolis vocal ensemble Cantus, continues composing full-time and recently received grant development backing for a new opera project, according to reporting from MPR News and the Central MN Arts Board.

Graphite Publishing stated that it is continuing to expand its creator-focused programs from its Minneapolis office, including composer consortium commissions and the choral magazine Take Note.
