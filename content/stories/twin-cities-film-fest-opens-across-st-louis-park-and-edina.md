Screenings for the 17th annual Twin Cities Film Fest began on Thursday, October 15, opening a 10-day schedule that runs through Saturday, October 24, 2026. As reported by KARE 11 and Eden Prairie Local News, the festival presents more than 110 independent feature films, documentaries, and shorts.

In-person screenings and red-carpet events are taking place at the Marcus West End Cinema at The Shops at West End in St. Louis Park and at the Edina Mann 4 Theatre at 50th and France. For viewers watching from home, CBS News and Hometown Source reported that more than 60 titles are streaming online through the TCFF Streams platform.

Local productions occupy a noticeable share of this year's schedule. MPR News reported that approximately 30 percent of the lineup carries a Minnesota connection, while CBS News noted organizers put that figure at more than one-third across the full slate. Among those titles, the Minnesota-focused documentary "A Month in Minnesota" sold out three separate theatrical screenings in advance, according to MPR News.

Opening night featured a food tasting and a screening of the documentary "You Belong Here." Eden Prairie Local News and KARE 11 reported that the film's subject, Kentucky chef Ouita Michel, attended alongside Minnesota-born actor and producer Steve Zahn. Opening night also included the premiere of director Martin McDonagh's Chilean-set CIA dark comedy "Wild Horse Nine," starring John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell.

According to CBS News, organizers reported that nearly 50 percent of this year's narrative and short films belong to the "HER Series," highlighting work by, for, and about women. Organizers also reported that over 40 percent of selections highlight BIPOC stories, telling Hometown Source that the festival focuses on discovering stories and offering artists an audience connection.

This year's social issue spotlight, titled the Changemaker Series, focuses on "The Refugee Experience." Eden Prairie Local News and Hometown Source reported that the series is anchored by the PBS Frontline documentary "One in a Million" and includes community panel discussions organized with the humanitarian nonprofit partner Alight.

Actress Frances Fisher is attending in person to receive the festival's Lifetime Achievement Award. KARE 11, the 50th & France Business Association, and MPR News reported that Fisher will participate in a career retrospective discussion on Tuesday, October 20, at the Edina Theatre, alongside the Midwest premiere of her film "Out of the Woods."

Industry events are also set throughout the week. CBS News and festival organizers noted two pitch competitions: the Denver-partnered SeriesFest: Pitch-A-Thon! Roadshow on Saturday, October 17, and the Make the Cut Award Competition with the Raymond J. Christensen Foundation. The Christensen Foundation competition will fully fund post-production costs, meaning the editing, sound, and color work finished after filming, for one documentary project.

Off the screen, the festival is operating a free gathering space called the "All About the Culture Lounge" at 1615 West End Blvd. in St. Louis Park. Meet Minneapolis and festival organizers reported that the lounge hosts an Industry Night mixer on Friday, October 16, as well as "Mission: Pawssible," a rescue pet adoption night with The Bond Between on Thursday, October 22.

The festival concludes on Saturday, October 24. CBS News reported that closing night features a screening of the comedy-drama "Not Dead Yet," followed by an in-person discussion with Academy Award-nominated actor Paul Raci.
