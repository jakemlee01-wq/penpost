On the northern edge of Two Harbors, a 62-acre industrial parcel appraised at $143,200 is set to sell for $1.00. The Two Harbors City Council voted to approve a purchase and development agreement with Pulsar Helium MN, Inc., establishing the framework for an $85.5 million to $90 million facility called the Rare Gas Hub, as reported by Twin Cities Business.

The project is planned as Minnesota's first commercial helium processing facility, according to reporting from Twin Cities Business and the Mesabi Tribune. The plant will liquefy raw gas brought by rail and pipeline from Pulsar's Topaz Project appraisal wells near Babbitt in Lake County, where five wells have encountered gas under high pressure. The facility may also process raw gas transported across Lake Superior from Michigan's Upper Peninsula.

Pulsar accepted an equipment proposal totaling about $85.5 million from Chart Industries to build purification, liquefaction, storage, and loading infrastructure, representing an overall $90 million investment, the Mesabi Tribune reported. Reporting by gasworld noted the hub is engineered to produce up to 7.5 million liters of liquid helium annually and capture up to 300 metric tons per day of food-and-beverage-grade carbon dioxide.

Pulsar Helium President Cliff Cain said the plant is expected to directly create 20 to 30 permanent full-time jobs, along with additional positions in rail transit, electrical work, and construction. Cain called the plant "probably the largest project in Two Harbors in years," telling the Mesabi Tribune that the location checked all the boxes with its road access, industrial setting, and rail connections.

To help establish the site, Two Harbors agreed to contribute up to $1 million toward building a rail siding connected to Canadian National rail lines, with Pulsar covering any rail costs above that amount, the Mesabi Tribune and gasworld reported. Under the agreement, the city retains permanent rights to use the spur for municipal needs and can charge third parties a fee to use it. Two Harbors will also fund site clearing, road corridor preparation, surveying, and wetland work, while extending city water, sewer, natural gas, and electrical utilities to the property.

Gasworld reported the council also approved a 15-year tax abatement that reimburses Pulsar 90% of qualifying property tax increases generated by the completed initial phase. Pulsar has a 365-day due diligence window starting Oct. 14, 2026, to complete site reviews, environmental permitting, utility contracts, and project financing. Once the company issues a formal project commitment, it has 36 months to bring the first phase into commercial operation. Pulsar's Thomas Abraham-James said the council's approval is an important step toward strengthening the domestic supply chain for rare gases.
