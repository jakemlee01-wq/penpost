The U.S. Department of Agriculture National Agricultural Statistics Service published its weekly Crop Progress report on Monday, October 19, 2026, tracking field conditions through Sunday, October 18.

American soybean harvest advanced 18 percentage points in a single week to reach 64 percent complete, up from 46 percent the week prior, Brownfield Ag News and DTN reported. That pace put the soybean harvest 1 percentage point behind the national five-year average of 65 percent. About 96 percent of the crop was dropping leaves, up from 92 percent the prior week and just behind the five-year benchmark of 97 percent. Because the harvest passed the halfway mark, USDA has stopped publishing weekly condition ratings for soybeans for the rest of the season, according to Farm Progress.

Combines brought the national corn harvest to 44 percent complete, an 11-point increase from 33 percent the previous week, DTN and Farm Progress reported. The corn pace remains 8 percentage points behind the five-year average of 52 percent, leaving 56 percent of the country's corn acreage unharvested as of October 18. About 95 percent of the corn crop had matured, compared to 91 percent a week earlier and a 96 percent five-year average. For corn still standing, 56 percent was rated good to excellent, with 43 percent good and 13 percent excellent, matching the previous week. Another 27 percent was rated fair and 17 percent was rated poor or very poor, and Farm Progress reported this is the final week USDA will publish corn condition ratings.

Fieldwork varied across the Midwest. Top-producing Iowa reached 26 percent of its corn harvested against a five-year average of 50 percent, and 57 percent of its soybeans against an average of 74 percent, according to DTN and the Southern Farm Network. Michigan corn harvest stood at 13 percent complete, with 62 percent of the crop mature, DTN reported. In Indiana, growers matched historical marks, reaching 46 percent on corn and 59 percent on soybeans, 101.5 WKKG reported.

Other autumn crops also advanced. Winter wheat seeding reached 63 percent planted across major producing states, with 39 percent emerged from the soil, both slightly behind historical averages. Cotton harvest reached 41 percent complete, ahead of the 37 percent multi-year average, with 35 percent rated good to excellent, Brownfield Ag News reported. Rice harvest reached 97 percent complete, ahead of its 93 percent average, and sorghum harvest reached 46 percent.

Rapid harvest progress and selling pressure pushed corn and soybean futures prices lower, offsetting export demand, according to Brownfield Ag News and Palmetto Grain analyst Brooks Schaffer. China purchased an additional 114,000 metric tons of export grain on Monday. For the week ahead, DTN ag meteorologist John Baranick stated that most producers can expect sunny skies, warm temperatures, and favorable field conditions. Ag Bull Trading and AgroLatam reported little to no rain across the Corn Belt through at least Friday, October 23, allowing machinery to operate without weather delays.
