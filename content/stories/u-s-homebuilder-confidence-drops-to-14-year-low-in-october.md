Prospective buyer traffic at new residential developments dropped five points in October to a reading of 18, reaching levels unseen since April 2012 according to the National Association of Home Builders.

The broader National Association of Home Builders and Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell three points in October 2026 to 29, as reported by Reuters. That reading marks the lowest level of homebuilder confidence since June 2012, falling short of the consensus forecast of 31 projected by economists surveyed by Reuters.

Any score below 50 means more homebuilders view sales conditions as poor rather than good. As noted by Homes.com News and TheStreet Pro, the index has now spent 30 consecutive months below that 50 mark, and 18 straight months below 40.

Behind the drop is a combination of borrowing costs and rising expenses for contractors. Freddie Mac data reported by Business Insider showed the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate held at 7.40% for the second straight week ending October 15, 2026. NAHB Chairman Bill Owens stated that borrowing rates well above 7%, alongside inflation and higher prices for diesel, building materials, and gas, weakened customer visits across much of the country.

NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz stated that supply constraints and steep affordability challenges are keeping more buyers on the sidelines. More than one-third of builders surveyed by the NAHB also reported local immigration enforcement actions over the past month, which kept workers off job sites and delayed construction.

To keep buyers interested, 43% of builders cut home prices in October to stimulate slowing sales, according to TheStreet Pro and REI Prime. The average price reduction held at 6% for the seventh month in a row, according to Dow Jones Newswires. RTTNews reported that 65% of builders offered incentives such as closing cost credits and mortgage rate buydowns, which are payments made upfront to lower a borrower's interest rate.

The component tracking current single-family sales dropped two points to 33 in October, according to Trading Economics. Expectations for sales over the next six months fell five points to 32, down from 37 in September and reaching the lowest reading since November 2022.

Sentiment weakened across every region in three-month moving averages tracked by Seeking Alpha, dropping to 40 in the Midwest, 38 in the Northeast, 30 in the South, and 26 in the West. Market Briefs reported from Zelman that contract cancellations for new homes in the Western United States climbed to their highest rate since November 2023.

Forecasters expect the slowdown to continue. Capital Economics reported that 2026 is on track to be the weakest year for total U.S. home sales since 2011, according to Business Insider. Sara Godfrey of Oxford Economics described builder sentiment as depressed and forecasted that housing activity will remain stagnant through next year, while Goldman Sachs projected that buyer demand will stay tepid into 2027.
