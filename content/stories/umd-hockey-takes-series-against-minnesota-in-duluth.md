The University of Minnesota Duluth men's hockey team earned three of four nonconference points against the University of Minnesota over the weekend, opening with a 7-1 win on Friday before finishing with a 2-2 tie on Saturday at AMSOIL Arena. As reported by UMD Athletics and the Duluth News Tribune, Friday's game drew a sellout crowd of 7,052, marking the 11th-largest crowd in arena history.

Ninth-ranked UMD built Friday's victory with two goals in the first period, four in the second, and one in the third against the 22nd-ranked Gophers. Hunter Anderson scored two goals, defenseman Ty Hanson added two power-play goals, and Luke Bibby scored once, according to the University of Minnesota Athletics box score, while Jacob Kvasnicka scored Minnesota's single goal. According to UMD Athletics, the six-goal victory was UMD's largest margin of victory against Minnesota since an 8-2 win on Jan. 14, 1983, and marked the first time UMD scored seven goals against the Gophers since Feb. 27, 1999. UMD head coach Scott Sandelin remarked after the game that he loved seeing the building's energy and praised the student section.

Before Saturday's matchup, UMD retired the No. 00 jersey of longtime volunteer and assistant equipment manager Dale "Hoagie" Haagenson, raising a banner to the arena rafters. UMD Athletics and The Ely Echo reported that Haagenson, a Babbitt native who passed away in February 2026 at age 65, joined the Bulldog staff in 1981 under head coach Gus Hendrickson and volunteered with the program for more than four decades. He became the seventh person in program history to have a number retired. Fans attending Saturday's game received commemorative rally towels placed on every seat. For both games, UMD wore throwback maroon 1980s-and-90s-style sweaters paired with white helmets.

In Saturday's game, senior defenseman Aaron Pionk scored with 29.6 seconds left in the first period, and Braden Fischer tipped in a power-play goal at 4:28 of the second period to give UMD a 2-0 lead. Gophers captain Brodie Ziemer answered with two goals in the second period, scoring at 6:13 and 12:13. As reported by Gopher Puck Live, Ziemer's second goal came on a power play and ended UMD's run of 21 consecutive successful penalty kills to open the season.

The game officially closed as a 2-2 tie after a scoreless three-on-three overtime, though Gopher Puck Live noted that Minnesota won an exhibition shootout on a second-round goal by LJ Mooney. UMD outshot Minnesota 36-18 on Saturday, including 7-1 in overtime, and held a 74-43 shot edge across both games. UMD goaltender Carl Axelsson made 16 saves in his third straight start, while Minnesota goaltender Di Pasquo stopped 34 shots. Sandelin said after the game that UMD's third period was its best, though he noted the team must learn to put away 2-0 leads.

The weekend gave UMD the regular-season series win over Minnesota for the second straight year, according to UMD Athletics and the Big Ten Conference, and stretched UMD's unbeaten run against the Gophers to four games. Gopher Puck Live reported the series moved UMD to 3-1-2 on the season while Minnesota fell to 0-3-1. UMD plays Bemidji State University next in a home-and-home series, starting Friday, Oct. 23, at AMSOIL Arena before traveling to Bemidji on Saturday.
