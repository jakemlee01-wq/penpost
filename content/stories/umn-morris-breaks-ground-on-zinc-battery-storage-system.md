A new energy storage site is taking shape next to the transportation garage on the University of Minnesota Morris campus. The university held a formal groundbreaking ceremony for the project at 3:00 p.m. on Monday, October 26, 2026.

The project installs a 1-megawatt, 6-megawatt-hour non-lithium battery system. Otter Tail Power Company reported that the setup can deliver 1 megawatt of power for up to six hours.

Open Access Technology International reported that the unit is an Eos Z3 system manufactured by Eos Energy Enterprises. The hardware uses an aqueous zinc chemistry with a water-based electrolyte, providing a non-flammable alternative to lithium-ion batteries.

Best Magazine reported that the static zinc battery runs in temperatures from -20 degrees Celsius to 50 degrees Celsius without external heating, ventilation, or air conditioning thermal management systems. Bryan Herrmann stated that it is important to test battery technologies well suited to the northern climate without requiring extensive thermal management.

Canary Media reported that the Morris campus already produces more than 60 percent of its electricity on-site using solar installations and two wind turbines, nicknamed Bert and Ernie.

According to Best Magazine, the battery will absorb excess electricity from the grid when campus wind and solar output peaks. It will discharge during times of high grid demand, providing load leveling, peak shaving, and potential ancillary and energy arbitrage services through the Midcontinent Independent System Operator market.

The installation is a partnership between UMN Morris, Fergus Falls-based Otter Tail Power Company, and OATI. OATI reported that Otter Tail Power will own and operate the battery on its distribution network. Engineering firm Burns & McDonnell is supplying engineering support for the installation.

Nathan Kroeker stated that the project puts the Z3 technology into a practical operating setting to support grid reliability, campus energy goals, and applied research. Chris Ruckman noted that solving growing power demand requires connecting generation, storage, and infrastructure so they work together.

Funding includes a $2.408 million appropriation from the Minnesota Environment and Natural Resources Trust Fund, recommended by the Legislative-Citizen Commission on Minnesota Resources. Best Magazine reported that Otter Tail Power contributed a matching $2.5 million to pay for 90 percent of the battery purchase, with the Minnesota Public Utilities Commission approving Otter Tail Power's investment in March 2026.

Best Magazine also reported that the project received its initial trust fund money in 2021 and was first planned as a flow battery. Partners later switched to the static aqueous zinc design because it operates without mechanical pumps to move electrolyte.

Otter Tail Power Company reported that construction will move forward after the groundbreaking toward full commissioning and grid connection at the Morris campus in 2027.
