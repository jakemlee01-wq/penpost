On Saturday, Oct. 24, 2026, U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke L. Rollins and NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman signed a Memorandum of Understanding in Washington, D.C., to integrate satellite data with federal agricultural reporting.

New Space Economy reported that the agreement takes effect immediately. It pairs NASA Earth-observing satellites and geospatial analytics with statistical models run by USDA agencies.

According to Action News Now, the partnership aims to modernize national crop acreage measurements and improve the precision of seasonal harvest yield forecasts.

KGAN reported that the initiative builds on directives from Executive Order 14369, "Ensuring American Space Superiority," which President Donald Trump signed in December 2025 to channel space agency capabilities toward civilian economic sectors.

Isaacman stated that NASA intends to deploy its advanced remote sensing and computing resources to deliver practical benefits to food producers, according to The Gilmer Mirror. Rollins stated that using orbital Earth science data will sharpen statistical reporting, enhance yield estimation, and protect farm profitability across commodity sectors.

Wisevoter reported that the satellite stream feeds directly into the USDA Data Modernization Plan, which first launched in September 2026 to test satellite modeling alongside producer-reported surveys. An application programming interface, or API, is a software bridge that allows two computer systems to share data automatically. The agreement calls for upgrading infrastructure with cloud-compatible formats, archives, and APIs, according to New Space Economy.

New Space Economy also reported that the partnership focuses on measuring planted and harvested acreage, tracking real-time crop conditions, modeling harvest yields, and assessing damage from natural disasters and weather hazards. The agreement outlines three operational goals: aligning NASA data products with USDA needs, updating technical data infrastructure, and conducting joint reviews of analytical algorithms, bias correction, and ground-truth validation.

Officials noted to Wisevoter and RFD-TV that integrating satellite observations with administrative records is designed to reduce paperwork and reporting burdens on producers while delivering faster, more reliable crop updates.

To check accuracy, the two agencies will conduct regular joint technical reviews to evaluate crop-yield algorithms, according to Wisevoter and New Space Economy. They plan to run structured parallel-testing pilots alongside traditional farmer surveys to validate satellite-derived metrics against real ground conditions.

The Gilmer Mirror reported that both agencies also plan to explore future applications linking Earth observation data with environmental monitoring and soil health metrics.

Around the signing, Isaacman and Rollins highlighted the partnership in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, during NASA's "Inspiration Tour," according to reporting from The Crimson White. That event included a pre-game flyover at the Alabama to Texas A&M football matchup in an F-5 jet piloted by Isaacman, with Rollins as copilot.
