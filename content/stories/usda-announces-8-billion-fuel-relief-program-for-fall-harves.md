Combines and tractors running through the fall harvest will soon see financial backing from Washington. On Oct. 19, 2026, the U.S. Department of Agriculture announced an $8 billion relief effort, named the Harvest Fuel Relief program, to provide one-time payments to domestic agricultural producers.

President Donald J. Trump first announced the initiative during an election campaign rally in Park City, Kansas, alongside U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke L. Rollins, according to Reuters and the USDA. Formal program details were then released by the USDA in Washington, D.C. Secretary Rollins stated that the investment is intended to bring certainty to farmers during the harvest so they can continue feeding the nation. The USDA said the funds are designed to offset higher operating expenses, including spikes in diesel fuel and energy inputs, and help producers navigate ongoing market disruptions.

Payouts are authorized through the Commodity Credit Corporation Charter Act. As reported by Reuters and the USDA, this authority allows the department to deploy money without needing an additional congressional appropriation. The USDA's Farm Service Agency will administer the relief directly.

Rather than offering a direct per-gallon diesel rebate, the program uses a uniform formula to issue proportional payments covering a share of modeled losses across the 2026 crop year, according to the USDA and Bloomberg. The calculation combines three data sources: FSA-reported planted acreage, cost-of-production metrics from the Economic Research Service, and price and yield projections from the World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates. The USDA noted that this setup mirrors the previous Farmer Bridge Assistance initiative, which distributed $12 billion in one-time payments to more than 560,000 farmers.

Producers do not need federal crop insurance linkage to participate. To speed up the application process, the USDA instructed farmers to set up or link a Login.gov account ahead of the launch, noting that pre-filled applications will be generated for producers already registered in USDA systems.

Specific payment rates for Title I crops are scheduled for release in November 2026, according to the USDA and Bloomberg, with payment systems for non-Title I commodities coming later. The USDA has asked non-Title I producers, including specialty crop growers and commercial fishermen, to email operational fuel expense records to HarvestFuel@usda.gov to help the agency establish separate payment formulas.
