The U.S. Department of Agriculture is distributing more than $325 million across 42 conservation projects nationwide. U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins announced the initiative on October 26, 2026, during an appearance in Cedar Rapids, Iowa. The awards are organized through the Regional Conservation Partnership Program, which pairs federal funding with private and local matching dollars to address natural resource and water challenges on working farmland.

Under revised program rules, 75 percent of the $325 million pool goes directly to agricultural producers or pays for on-the-ground conservation work, according to the USDA. Natural Resources Conservation Service Chief Colton Buckley said the agency aims to invest in practical solutions that support landowners while protecting resources. Rollins stated that recent administrative adjustments were intended to reduce red tape and increase flexibility for growers.

In Iowa, federal officials awarded $10 million over five years to expand the Iowa Cattle and Conservation Working Lands Program, as reported by World News Report and EIN Presswire. The effort operates through a partnership among the Iowa Department of Agriculture and Land Stewardship, the Iowa Cattlemen’s Association, the Iowa Agriculture Water Alliance, and Ducks Unlimited.

Iowa Secretary of Agriculture Mike Naig stated that the state effort began as a pilot project in Taylor County in 2016 to help farmers shift marginal, highly erodible crop ground into grazing pastures and hay production. According to reporting from EIN Presswire, the initiative later expanded into Page, Woodbury, Cherokee, Ida, Guthrie, Carroll, and Adams counties, engaging more than 1,800 producers and converting over 20,000 erodible row-crop acres into pasture.

The expanded Iowa program is scheduled to establish more than 300,000 acres of cover crops, which are off-season plants grown to hold soil and absorb nutrients. The USDA projects this work will prevent 1 million tons of soil erosion and keep an estimated 3.3 million pounds of nitrogen and 336,000 pounds of phosphorus out of waterways. Naig noted that the approach demonstrates ways to improve water quality, rebuild the cattle herd, and make the most of productive ground.

The national package also sets aside $30 million specifically for conservation projects led by Native American Tribes, according to GSM News. In Washington state, the Lummi Nation and Nooksack Indian Tribe will lead 256 acres of salmon habitat restoration. In Yakutat, Alaska, Yak-Tat Kwaan Inc. received funding to carry out reforestation and riparian restoration on more than 1,609 acres of Alaska Native Corporation-owned forestland, the Natural Resources Conservation Service reported.

Other selections focus on forestry and grassland preservation across the South, GSM News reported. In southwest Georgia, one project leverages more than $10 million in matching contributions to restore fire-dependent longleaf pine forests and protect at least 6,000 acres across 16 counties from development, according to the USDA. In Texas, federal funds will support coastal prairie conservation easements.

Program funding draws from statutory increases under the Working Families Tax Cuts Act, according to reporting by Mainstreet Media and FarmRaise. The legislation allocated $425 million for the partnership program in fiscal year 2026 and sets annual funding at $450 million each year from 2027 through 2031.
