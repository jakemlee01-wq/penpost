The U.S. Department of Agriculture announced $86.6 million in federal grant awards on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, to support specialty crop producers across the country. The funding is disbursed through the Agricultural Marketing Service Specialty Crop Block Grant Program. Under federal statute, specialty crops include fruits, vegetables, tree nuts, dried fruits, horticulture, and nursery crops such as floriculture.

As reported by the Oklahoma Farm Report, the package finances 609 separate state-level initiatives distributed across all 50 states and six U.S. territories. Eligible recipient jurisdictions include the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands. State departments of agriculture will manage the block grants, distributing subawards to farmers, university researchers, and agricultural associations. The Agricultural Marketing Service posted a searchable directory of the individual project descriptions.

The grants target challenges along agricultural distribution chains. Initiatives funded through the program focus on food safety enhancements, applied research, the breeding of resilient crop and seed varieties, plant pest and disease management, nutrition education, and efforts to lower transportation and marketing costs. U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke L. Rollins stated that the investments are intended to drive economic growth and keep specialty producers competitive in global markets. Rollins added that putting farmers first will drive growth and strengthen the fabric of rural communities.

Individual allocations reflect regional crops and priorities across the country. Florida received more than $4.3 million to finance research, education, outreach, and marketing efforts for crops including mangoes, blueberries, sweet corn, and muscadine grapes. In Michigan, more than $2.64 million in subawards will support growers of asparagus, apples, blueberries, beans, Christmas trees, mushrooms, herbs, and grapes. Texas secured more than $2 million for projects aiding producers of pecans, watermelons, grapes, onions, and spinach.

Georgia was awarded over $1.7 million to fund 13 initiatives, consisting of two research projects, nine education and outreach programs, and two marketing projects. Those funds benefit pecans, peaches, blueberries, strawberries, peppers, apples, citrus, and Vidalia onions. North Carolina received over $1.5 million for projects assisting sweet potatoes, Fraser fir Christmas trees, tomatoes, blueberries, apples, canary melons, and centipede grass seed. In Pennsylvania, more than $1.1 million was awarded for research, outreach, and marketing programs focused on mushrooms, apples, and potatoes. Ohio was allocated more than $800,000 to assist growers of apples, leafy greens, brassicas, herbs, honey, nuts, and maple syrup.
