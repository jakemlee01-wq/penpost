The U.S. Department of Agriculture announced it is distributing roughly $1.8 billion in Conservation Reserve Program payments to agricultural producers and private landowners for the 2026 program year. Eligible landowners receive these annual rental payments through local Farm Service Agency county offices, which administer contracts that typically last 10 to 15 years, according to reporting from WCSM Radio.

As part of the current cycle, the Farm Service Agency accepted offers on nearly 1 million new acres of Grassland CRP, offering rental payments and cost-share assistance to producers. Following this enrollment period, Grassland CRP is projected to make up more than 40 percent of all acreage enrolled in the federal program across the country.

Total nationwide enrollment will reach 26.8 million acres for fiscal year 2027 after the agency accepted 2.2 million acres across Grassland, General, and Continuous CRP signups, AGDAILY reported. According to AGDAILY's reporting, the leading states receiving payments for 2026 are Iowa, Illinois, Minnesota, South Dakota, and Missouri.

Traditional CRP contracts pay landowners to take environmentally sensitive ground out of agricultural production to curb soil erosion, protect waterways, and boost wildlife habitat. By contrast, Grassland CRP operates as a working lands program, which allows ranchers to keep grazing and haying active while maintaining protective plant cover.

The USDA tied the grassland funding directly to the federal Ranchers First Initiative. According to the department, that initiative focuses on expanding forage on working lands and rebuilding the domestic cattle herd amid historic lows in national herd size. Agriculture Secretary Brooke L. Rollins stated that protecting grazing land through the program encourages cattle industry growth while preserving natural resources.

Richard Fordyce, the Under Secretary for Farm Production and Conservation, noted that enrollment was a highly competitive process, stating that the funds support producers who pursue voluntary stewardship while keeping their operations active. Related to federal grazing policies, Drovers reported that Secretary Rollins issued an administrative directive to livestock permittees implementing a June 12, 2026 directive aimed at streamlining permits and adding 500,000 head months of grazing across 240 million acres of Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management rangelands.

The distribution follows the September 30 expiration of the underlying farm bill statutory authority for new CRP signups, according to reporting from KQLX and Iowa Ag News. While that expiration affects new signups, existing and newly accepted multi-year contracts remain legally intact and fully funded.
