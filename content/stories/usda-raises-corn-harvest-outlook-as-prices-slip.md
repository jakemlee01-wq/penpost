The U.S. Department of Agriculture increased its 2026 corn production forecast by 234 million bushels to 16.0 billion bushels, marking the second-largest crop on record, according to High Plains Journal. Total harvested corn acreage held steady at 88.5 million acres.

Global Agriculture reported that the projected national average yield climbed by 2.7 bushels to 181.2 bushels per acre. That adjustment moved against earlier trade estimates, which had anticipated a drop to 177.7 bushels. With higher yields, the agency raised ending stocks, or the unused grain left over before the next harvest, by 282 million bushels to 1.849 billion bushels, High Plains Journal reported.

Larger supply expectations weighed on crop values. High Plains Journal noted that the USDA cut its projected season-average price by 10 cents to $4.70 per bushel, while December futures dropped roughly 20 cents to about $4.80 per bushel.

Following the revision, National Corn Growers Association President Matt Frostic urged Congress on October 12 to pass year-round E15 fuel legislation and finalize the farm bill in November, American Ag Network reported. Frostic stated the policies are needed to help offset declining crop prices and high diesel costs.
