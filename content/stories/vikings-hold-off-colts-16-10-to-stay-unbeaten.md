Safety Harrison Smith placed the football on the turf at the 40-yard line at U.S. Bank Stadium on Sunday while the home crowd chanted his name, according to reporting from The Associated Press and CBS Sports. That play, an interception near midfield with 1:15 remaining, sealed a 16-10 victory for the Minnesota Vikings over the Indianapolis Colts.

The Associated Press reported that the win moved Minnesota to 6-0 on the season and dropped Indianapolis to 2-5. Following a San Francisco 49ers loss, the outcome left the Vikings as the NFC's only remaining undefeated team, which FOX 9 and CBS Sports noted is the franchise's first 6-0 start since 2009.

Indianapolis opened the scoring on its first offensive series with a 38-yard touchdown run by Jonathan Taylor behind a block from left tackle Bernhard Raimann, according to Colts.com. Colts kicker Spencer Shrader added a 43-yard field goal before halftime, but CBS Sports reported that Shrader missed two kicks in the second half, including a 56-yard attempt on fourth-and-2 that ended his run of 22 consecutive made field goals and gave Minnesota short field position.

Vikings kicker Will Reichard kept Minnesota close by converting field goals from 28, 47, and 52 yards, according to FOX 9. His 47-yard kick in the second quarter marked his 36th consecutive made field goal, which broke Gary Anderson's previous franchise record of 35 straight. The Associated Press reported that Reichard had a 59-yard kick blocked in the fourth quarter before making the 52-yarder with 1:39 left to push the lead to six points.

The Vikings took the lead early in the fourth quarter after breaking a 10-10 tie, according to NBC Sports. Quarterback Kyler Murray ran 35 yards on third-and-8 from the Indianapolis 40-yard line, then threw a 5-yard touchdown pass to tight end T.J. Hockenson on the next snap. CBS Sports noted that Murray finished 14 of 24 passing for 150 yards and one touchdown with 41 rushing yards.

In the backfield, running back Jordan Mason returned from injured reserve to carry 15 times for 68 yards, while Aaron Jones Sr. ran for 26 yards on eight carries before leaving in the fourth quarter with a knee injury, according to NBC Sports and Heavy Sports.

Minnesota's defense, coordinated by Brian Flores, held Indianapolis scoreless through the entire second half, according to NBC Sports. CBS Sports reported that the unit sacked Colts quarterback Daniel Jones four times for 44 lost yards, limited him to 108 passing yards on 13-of-22 passing, and held an NFL-low 74 total points allowed through six games. Linebacker Blake Cashman led the effort with 12 tackles, two sacks, four passes defensed, and three quarterback hits.

Smith's game-ending takeaway was the 40th interception of his career, making him just the fourth defensive player in league history with at least 40 interceptions and 20 sacks alongside Ronde Barber, Larry Wilson, and Charles Woodson, according to Vikings.com and CBS Sports. Smith told reporters afterward, "There's a lot of fight in this group." Head coach Kevin O'Connell stated after the game: "We feel really comfortable and confident to win football games when we don't turn it over and we win the field position and boring stuff like that, because we know over the course of 60 minutes what that side can do."

NBC Sports reported that the Vikings travel to face the Detroit Lions on the road in Week 8, while Colts.com reported that Indianapolis travels to face the Jacksonville Jaguars.
