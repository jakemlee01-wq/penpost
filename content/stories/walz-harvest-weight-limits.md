Farm trucks in Minnesota can carry more per trip for the rest of harvest.

Gov. Tim Walz signed Executive Order 26-11 on Oct. 1. It lets vehicles hauling corn, soybeans, wheat, potatoes, sugar beets, carrots and forest products exceed normal weight limits by up to 10% on state and local highways. It also gives carriers relief from federal hours-of-service rules, which limit how long drivers can be on the road. The order runs through Oct. 30.

The logic is simple arithmetic. If each load is 10% heavier, it takes fewer loads to move the same crop, which saves fuel and driver time. That matters this fall. The Minnesota Corn Growers Association says diesel costs about 70% more than it did at last year's harvest, and CBS Minnesota reported a national average of $6.38 a gallon on Oct. 1. The governor's office tied the jump to the war with Iran and federal trade policy.

Two practical notes. The 10% cap is set in state law, so an executive order can't go further. And farmers who use the extra weight need to update their vehicle registration to match, according to the corn growers.

Farm groups have asked for more, including letting farmers use red-dyed diesel in road vehicles during harvest and pausing fuel taxes. Neither is part of this order.
