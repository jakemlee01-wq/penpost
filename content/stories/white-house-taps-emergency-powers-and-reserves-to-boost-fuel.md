The White House announced a series of federal interventions on Sunday, October 25, 2026, aimed at easing fuel market shortages and lowering diesel costs. President Donald J. Trump invoked Title III of the Cold War-era Defense Production Act of 1950 to direct up to $150 million toward expanding domestic petroleum production, storage, and refining capacity, according to reports from CBS News and the U.S. Department of Energy. Alongside the domestic funding, the administration finalized terms with European partners to release 100 million barrels of refined diesel from strategic reserves over the next four months.

The policy moves follow steep fuel price increases across the country. Figures from AAA, reported by CBS News and TIME, showed retail diesel prices averaging $6.32 per gallon, representing a 74 percent increase year over year. Regular gasoline averaged $4.29 per gallon. OilPrice.com and Investing.com reported that domestic refinery utilization climbed to 98 percent in late summer, strained by conflict in the Middle East, shipping disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz, and strikes against Russian refineries.

Those domestic refining crunches developed even as raw energy extraction set records. White House figures show domestic crude oil production reached an all-time high of 13.6 million barrels per day, while natural gas production touched a record 118.5 billion cubic feet per day. The United States also became the first nation to export more than 100 million metric tons of liquefied natural gas within a single calendar year.

To address fuel processing bottlenecks, the administration is focusing on physical refinery capacity. In a formal memorandum reported by TIME, Trump determined that petroleum production and refining activities are essential to national defense readiness, writing that U.S. defense capabilities remain vulnerable without immediate federal action. The Department of Energy is establishing partnerships with refiners to direct the initial $150 million into debottlenecking logistics and expanding throughput. Vice President JD Vance indicated plans to use these authorities at facilities including Delek US's refinery in Texas, according to Seeking Alpha. Energy Secretary Chris Wright claimed the administration is expanding refining capacity after what he termed 17 years of energy subtraction policies by Democratic leaders.

For immediate supply relief, executive directives temporarily permit commercial freight transport to burn untaxed, red-dyed off-road diesel on public highways. The White House also deferred the federal diesel excise tax through the end of the year. To address regional heating costs, the Department of Energy launched an emergency exchange to loan out up to 315,000 barrels of fuel oil, roughly one-third of the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve, NewsCord and the agency reported.

The White House is also leaning on international agreements to supplement fuel volumes. Administration data showed bilateral arrangements contributed to a 400 percent surge in Venezuelan oil shipments to the U.S. Under agreements with private operator NABEP, the U.S. secured rights to purchase 20 percent of production at $30 per barrel alongside a 35 percent equity stake managed by the Pentagon's Office of Strategic Capital. The White House and CiVL also highlighted a broader 65-billion-barrel energy framework involving Venezuela, more than 130 million barrels of diesel secured from foreign stockpiles, and a $54 billion agreement with South Korea and Taiwan to build the Alaska natural gas pipeline. White House representative Taylor Rogers stated the actions are intended to stabilize market supplies and reduce pump prices for families and commercial truckers.
