The City of Willmar is nearing completion on the initial infrastructure for Willmar Connect, its 24.5 million dollar municipal broadband network. According to reporting from the Institute for Local Self-Reliance and Blandin on Broadband, the city plans to bring its first residential and commercial customers online before the end of 2026. Willmar City Administrator Kyle Box stated that officials expect to light up the network's first users this year.

The multi-year project is designed to deliver citywide fiber coverage to Willmar's roughly 21,000 residents. A city broadband task force formed in 2022 determined that private incumbent carriers were unwilling to modernize infrastructure throughout the community, as reported by Broadband Breakfast and the Minnesota Star Tribune. Physical construction broke ground in May 2026 following several years of feasibility studies and public hearings.

The buildout moved ahead after the Willmar City Council voted 4 to 3 in March 2026 to approve the 24.5 million dollar bond financing and development agreements. Under the municipal open-access model, the city retains ownership of the physical fiber lines. Multiple competitive internet service providers can then lease access to deliver symmetric speeds ranging from 300 megabits per second up to multi-gigabit tiers. Lease revenue and subscriber sign-ups will fund debt service payments and subsequent phases of the project.

The overall deployment is divided into three distinct stages. Phase 1 centers on southwest Willmar, covering homes and commercial addresses west of 1st Street between U.S. Highway 12 and 19th Avenue, while excluding the Willmar Industrial Park. Reporting from the West Central Tribune and the Institute for Local Self-Reliance indicates that Phase 1 is budgeted between 7.6 million and 9.2 million dollars.

The council funded this initial stage by approving 8.79 million dollars in general obligation tax-abatement bonds in January 2026 at an interest rate of roughly 3.9 percent. These bonds are local government debt backed by property tax revenues. Box specified that the Phase 1 bond financing covered construction, construction administration, bond fees, and one year of capitalized interest. Kramer Service Group of Wisconsin received the primary construction contract for Phase 1, valued at approximately 7.6 million to 8.2 million dollars.

Over the summer, field crews completed directional boring, conduit installation, and fiber splicing across town. To house network routing equipment, the city converted a decommissioned municipal wastewater facility into a network operations center. Minnetonka-based Hometown Fiber is serving as the retail and operational management partner for the system.

The Willmar City Council is scheduled to review Phase 2 in the fall of 2026. That section covers East and South Willmar, with construction tentatively set to begin in the spring of 2027. Phase 3, which will expand the network across North Willmar, is scheduled to follow in 2028.
