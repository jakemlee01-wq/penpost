The remains of U.S. Marine Corps Pvt. Ernest LaVerne Lund arrived at Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport on Thursday morning, Oct. 15, 2026, more than 80 years after he died during World War II. As reported by FOX 9 and CBS Minnesota, Lund’s flag-draped casket was received on the tarmac with military honors and escorted by members of the Minnesota Patriot Guard.

According to an obituary from Swedberg-Taylor Funeral Home reported alongside coverage from WCCO and FOX 9, Lund was born on Nov. 10, 1915, in Cannon Falls Township in Goodhue County. The second youngest of 14 siblings born to Carl and Anna Lund, he worked as a farmhand in Wilson and Hart, Minnesota, earning $30 a month before entering the military. Lund enlisted in the Marine Corps in November 1943 at age 27. He completed boot camp and tank training at Marine Corps Recruit Depot San Diego before joining Company B, 2nd Armored Amphibian Battalion, Second Marine Division, Fleet Marine Force.

Lund died at age 28 on May 21, 1944, aboard the tank landing ship USS LST-353 in Hawaii. FOX 9 and CBS Minnesota reported that the vessel was stationed at the West Loch staging area in Pearl Harbor, preparing ammunition and vessels for Operation Forager, the planned invasion of the Mariana Islands. An accidental blast triggered a chain reaction of explosions that destroyed multiple ships. According to the Naval History and Heritage Command, cited by WCCO and FOX 9, the disaster killed 163 service members and wounded 396 others, making it one of the deadliest non-combat naval incidents in U.S. history. Fellow service members described Lund in his obituary as a quiet and kind young man who spent his rest hours reading in the shade of a landing craft.

Kayakers originally found skeletal remains in Pearl Harbor waters in 1985, CBS Minnesota and FOX 9 reported. An initial review and DNA test by the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency, known as the DPAA, took place in the 2010s but failed to produce a match. In 2020, Lund's nieces and nephews submitted reference DNA samples. Those records enabled the agency to disinter the grave again, officially confirming on May 14, 2026, that three portions of the remains belonged to Lund.

Relative Roger McKelvey of Bloomington shared the family's relief following the identification. McKelvey told WCCO, "It's uplifted us all that he's finally coming home. We're finally going to have closure about Ernest Lund, our hero in our family."

Services for Lund begin with a visitation on Friday, Oct. 16, in Webster, Wisconsin. As reported by WCCO and FOX 9, a funeral service and burial with full military honors will follow on Saturday, Oct. 17, at Markville Cemetery in Markville, Minnesota.
