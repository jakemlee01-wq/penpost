The Worthington City Council is scheduled to vote Monday, October 26, 2026, on the final finance and construction agreement for a new $16 million single-sheet ice arena, as reported by Radio Works.

The proposed venue will sit on the Worthington Independent School District 518 campus along Crailsheim Road, located just west of the Community Education building. According to reporting from Radio Works and Let's Play Hockey, keeping the city's existing arena open alongside the new building will give Worthington two sheets of ice.

The baseline budget relies on three partners. The City of Worthington is providing $10 million, which covers 62.5% of the cost. District 518 is contributing $5 million, or 31.25%, along with the underlying land and site preparation. The Worthington Hockey Association is committing a baseline share of $1 million, representing 6.25% of the total budget.

Both partner boards have already signed off on the deal. The District 518 Board of Education approved the finance and construction agreement on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, and the Worthington Hockey Association board approved it ahead of the city council's vote, Radio Works reported.

Under the arrangement, District 518 will serve as the property and facility owner, project manager, and fiscal agent. The district will issue monthly prorated payment requests to the city for design and construction expenses.

A separate 25-year operational lease takes effect once the building reaches substantial completion. Under that contract, the city will lease the arena from the school district as tenant for $1 per year. The city will handle daily operations, staffing, ice maintenance, scheduling priorities, operational expenses, and rental rate formulas across both the new and existing rinks. The venue will host District 518 curricular and athletic programs, youth hockey, high school hockey, figure skating, open skating, and off-season events, according to Let's Play Hockey.

To cover its $10 million commitment, Worthington city staff proposed drawing from four municipal funds, Radio Works reported. The proposal allocates $2 million from hospital impact funds, $3 million from Fund 101 unreserved general reserves, $2 million from future undesignated sales tax revenue, and $3 million from Worthington Public Utilities.

The project timeline reported by Let's Play Hockey calls for breaking ground in fall 2027, with substantial completion and a ceremonial first puck drop targeted for October 2028.

Initial cost estimates had forced planners to drop a dryland training room and a meeting room to stay within the $16 million cap. Let's Play Hockey reported that the Worthington Hockey Association responded by pledging to raise an additional $725,000 to restore both spaces to the final design, lifting its total construction commitment to $1,725,000. In total, the association launched a $2 million campaign, which includes $275,000 for furnishings and equipment, and has confirmed raising $953,162 so far.

Jason Johnson told Let's Play Hockey that the association is growing alongside demand for ice time, calling the collaboration an amenity for everyone who uses it both during the season and off-season.

The agreement settles years of joint discussions among the city, the school district, and local hockey organizers. According to reporting from Radio Works and Citizen Portal, planners previously studied sites north of town and explored an expansion at the Nobles County Fairgrounds before selecting the Crailsheim Road campus.
